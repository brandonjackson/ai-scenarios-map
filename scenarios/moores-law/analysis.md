# Moore's Law for Everything — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Strongly policy on (prescriptive).** Altman says the policy-off outcome is that "most people will end up worse off than they are today." The good future he describes depends on the American Equity Fund and land-value taxation. The map's "DIFF (prescriptive)" tag is right: diffusion is the *goal*, not the default.

## Big assumptions
1. **Capability arrives on schedule.** Legal and medical advice within five years of 2021 (largely true by 2026), assembly-line work within ten, "almost everything" in the decades after. Physical automation is assumed to follow cognitive automation without much trouble.
2. **Labour is the main cost.** Prices fall toward the cost of renting robots because labour drives costs "at many levels of the supply chain". Land, energy, materials, regulation and market power are treated as minor, except land, which the tax handles.
3. **Abundance lowers prices rather than raising profits.** Competition passes cost savings to consumers instead of letting AI owners keep them as margin.
4. **Asset values keep doubling.** US market capitalisation ($50tn) and land ($30tn) double in a decade, falling only 15% from the tax and recovering in "a few years".
5. **Politics cooperates.** A new wealth tax passes, is "enormously popular", and survives lobbying, offshoring, companies staying private and capital flight, with a constitutional amendment as a safeguard.
6. **Meaning without economically valuable work.** People find fulfilment in care, art and community once income no longer comes from jobs.

## What must be true for this to occur
- AI really does drive the price of housing, healthcare and education down. These are sectors where costs have historically been driven by land, regulation and the cost of human services.
- Governments act *before* labour loses its political leverage. The essay doesn't address the risk that, once labour is unnecessary, the incentive to share disappears (compare *The Intelligence Curse*).
- Share-based taxation works across borders and for private companies.
- Growth is fast. The full 2.5% rate only applies once GDP is 50% larger.

## Tensions and weak points
- Hard work matters least here. Altman's social contract swaps "effort pays" for "ownership pays", which the Windfall goal scores as a broken promise.
- The writer runs the company producing the disruption, so the essay is also an argument about who should bear responsibility. Ted Chiang's critique of AI leaders who "pass the buck to the government" applies directly.
- The 2021 timeline has held up better than most, which makes the policy gap more urgent. Five years on, no Equity Fund exists anywhere.

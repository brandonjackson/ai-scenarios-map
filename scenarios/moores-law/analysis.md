# Moore's Law for Everything — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**On (prescriptive).** The abundant, broadly shared outcome depends on the American Equity Fund: 2.5% a year on company value, paid in shares, and on private land, paid in dollars, distributed to adult citizens. Without it, Altman says, power shifts to capital and most people end up worse off. The phase-in linked to GDP, a constitutional cap on rates, an American-revenue test against offshoring and rules on land assessment are recommended design details.

## Big assumptions
1. **Capability.** AI moves from legal and medical advice to assembly lines to almost everything, including discovery, and robots build robots, so the cost of renting them falls.
2. **Adoption.** AI eventually produces most basic goods and services, with healthcare, education and construction leading the price falls.
3. **Markets.** Lower labour costs pass through to prices rather than profits; company and land values roughly double over the decade, fall 15% when the tax arrives and recover within a few years; company growth comfortably exceeds the levy.
4. **Politics.** A new valuation-based tax passes before labour loses its leverage, unites a broad constituency and survives incentives to stay private, offshore or pay out rather than reinvest.

## What must be true for this to occur
- AI really does cut the cost of housing, healthcare and education, sectors where land, regulation and human services have driven costs up.
- Growth is very fast: the full rate applies only after GDP rises 50% from enactment, against 13 years to achieve that before 2019.
- Share-based taxation works for private firms and across borders.
- Government acts while labour still has political weight; the essay does not address the risk that the incentive to share fades once labour is unnecessary (compare `intelligence-curse`).

## Tensions and weak points
- Altman's settlement swaps "effort pays" for "ownership pays"; the Windfall goal scores that as a broken work promise even where incomes are secure.
- A constitutional cap meant to stop voters raising the levy limits the very say the Fund is supposed to build.
- The author runs a company producing the disruption, so the essay is also an argument about who should carry responsibility for it.
- **Map placement:** kept at (0.32, 0.62) and (-0.3, 0.2). Replacement is near-total; diffusion is prescribed through the Fund. Public finances (`y_fiscal` 0.2) are inferred: valuation taxes on companies and land replace a weakening income-tax base, but the extract gives no net budget position. Neighbours are `cfg-decentral`, `toloui-swarm`, `loving-grace`, `gos-open-frontier` and, on replacement, `korinek-agi`.

## Scoring notes
Draft scores 1/4/4/2/4 (previously 1/4/3/2/3), all stated and scored on the future the essay argues for. Work is 1: the price of many kinds of labour approaches zero, so effort stops being the route to a better life. Essentials is 4: costs of housing, education, food and clothing fall, and a land tax tackles the one input AI cannot make. Safety net rises to 4: with the Fund in place, a common income floor of about $13,500 per adult that cannot be pledged is a clear improvement, though it fluctuates with the cycle. Government stays 2: power shifts to capital and a constitutional cap limits what voters can decide. Time rises to 4 because the extract describes freed time for relationships, care and art without the earlier caveat about lost structure. The weakest-link rule caps the headline at 2.

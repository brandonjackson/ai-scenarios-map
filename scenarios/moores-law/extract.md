# Moore’s Law for Everything — extract

## Summary

Thinking software progressively takes over cognitive and physical work, including the work of making better machines. Lower labour costs make goods and services much cheaper, while the economic value of labour falls and power shifts toward capital owners. Altman proposes taxing valuable companies and privately held land and paying the proceeds to adult US citizens as cash and shares so that the gains from rising asset values accrue to everyone. With the policy and lower prices, citizens can afford essentials and spend more time with people or working on activities that matter to them. (Introduction; “Moore’s Law for Everything”; “Capitalism for Everyone”; “Shifting to the New System”)

## Key details

- **Source:** *Moore’s Law for Everything*, Sam Altman, 16 March 2021. `sources/moores-law-for-everything-2021/`
- **Other sources for this scenario:** None.
- **Reading notes:** Full essay from the author's site.
- **Scenario set:** Standalone.
- **Geography:** United States for the tax and dividend model. The technology and production outlook is global. (“Capitalism for Everyone”)
- **Authors' framing:** A forecast of technological change and an illustration of how policy could distribute the gains. Altman calls the Fund an idea “in the spirit of a conversation starter.” (Introduction; “Capitalism for Everyone”)
- **Authors' view of likelihood:** Altman calls technological change “unstoppable.” He treats the spending-power gains as conditional on lowering prices and distributing asset income. (Introduction; “Shifting to the New System”)

### Timeline

- Within five years of 2021, thinking programs read legal documents and give medical advice. Within ten years, they work on assembly lines and may become companions. In the following decades, they do almost everything, including scientific discovery. (Introduction)
- Under the illustration, a decade after the essay, adult citizens get about $13,500 each per year from the Fund. It phases in at low rates, reaching 2.5% only after GDP rises 50% from enactment. (“Implementation and Troubleshooting”; “Shifting to the New System”)

### Policy stance

- **Stance:** On. The abundant, broadly shared outcome is conditional on an American Equity Fund that distributes capital income. (“Capitalism for Everyone”)
- **Policies assumed in the scenario:** The Fund levies 2.5% annually on companies above a valuation threshold, payable in shares, and 2.5% on all privately held land, payable in dollars. Adult citizens receive cash and shares they can spend or invest as they choose. (“Capitalism for Everyone”)
- **Policies the source recommends:** Legislate a gradual phase-in linked to GDP growth. Use a constitutional amendment to bound rates, an American-revenue test to discourage offshoring, and space for private firms with annual revenue over $1 billion to accrue equity taxes temporarily before paying in shares or cash. Use federal or local land assessors, reassessing jurisdictions when transaction prices diverge sufficiently, and consider taxing improved value at a lower rate if bare land cannot be valued reliably. Make pledges of future distributions unenforceable. Retain strong leadership to balance shareholder incentives with rights and environmental protection. (“Capitalism for Everyone”; “Implementation and Troubleshooting”; “Shifting to the New System”)

## Key dynamics explored

- Smarter machines help build smarter machines, accelerating innovation. (Introduction)
- AI reduces labour costs throughout supply chains. Machines that make other machines make the equipment itself cheaper too. (“Moore’s Law for Everything”)
- Rising output creates wealth, but the value of productive assets accrues to owners. A share-based levy makes adults owners, aligning their incentives with company and national growth and spreading gains. (“Capitalism for Everyone”)
- Increasing land holding costs reduces the relative desirability of speculation and improves access to scarce land. (“Implementation and Troubleshooting”)
- Competition for citizens' Fund spending puts pressure on prices in formerly government-funded industries. (“Capitalism for Everyone”)

## State of technology

AI progresses from existing learning systems to software that thinks, reasons, creates and discovers. It can read legal documents, advise on medical conditions, diagnose better than human clinicians and identify precisely what a student has failed to understand. Robot labour extends to assembly lines and potentially homebuilding with local materials and solar power. Robot-made robots reduce the cost of renting them. The essay does not specify a model market, access regime or compute requirements. (Introduction; “The AI Revolution”; “Moore’s Law for Everything”)

## State of adoption

The essay projects work moving first to programs in law and advice, then to industrial robotics, then to almost all domains. AI eventually produces most basic goods and services. The essay illustrates what adoption would enable in healthcare, education and construction. (Introduction; “Moore’s Law for Everything”; “Capitalism for Everyone”)

## How the economy works

### GDP growth and productivity

The AI revolution follows agriculture, industry and computing and is expected to create enough wealth for everyone if managed responsibly. The essay expects extremely rapid growth once AI takes hold and uses a 50% rise in GDP as the threshold for applying the full levy. It contrasts this with the 13 years it took to achieve comparable growth through 2019. (“The AI Revolution”; “Shifting to the New System”)

### Labour market dynamics

The price of many kinds of labour approaches zero as sufficiently powerful AI joins the workforce. Work remains, but many future jobs generate relatively little value by present standards. Altman expects new jobs to be discovered and sees abundance as giving people freedom to invent them. (Introduction; “Moore’s Law for Everything”; “Capitalism for Everyone”)

### Winners and losers

Capital gains power over labour, and Altman expects most people to be worse off if policy fails to adapt. Businesses, especially those that use AI, and owners of fixed-supply land hold the two dominant sources of wealth. Broad equity distributions give adult citizens a stake in innovation, while land taxation shifts some value from landholders to society. (Introduction; “Capitalism for Everyone”)

### Household incomes and living standards

The Fund pays about $13,500 annually to each of about 250 million adults in the decade illustration. The value can rise with AI-driven growth and buy substantially more as unit costs fall. Distributions fluctuate with economic cycles. Benefits are usable for education, health, housing, new businesses or other purposes. The essay expects a large reduction in poverty. (“Capitalism for Everyone”; “Implementation and Troubleshooting”)

### Government finances

Progressive income taxation works less well as employment generates less value. Valuation-based taxes on companies and land replace some other taxes over time. A share levy aligns incentives and avoids some problems of profit taxation, because profits can be deferred, disguised or offshored. Corporate payments could use new shares. The design needs to account for incentives to stay private, offshore, or pay shareholders instead of reinvesting. Local governments could continue to levy their taxes using the same assessed land values. (“Capitalism for Everyone”; “Implementation and Troubleshooting”; “Shifting to the New System”)

### International economic patterns

An American-revenue threshold could apply the company levy even to firms that move overseas, deterring tax-motivated offshoring. The essay does not model international outcomes. (“Implementation and Troubleshooting”)

### Market pricing and scarcity

Lower labour costs shrink the price of goods and services. The illustrative aspiration is housing, education, food and clothing becoming half as expensive every two years for decades. US prices previously fell in electronics and entertainment while housing, healthcare and higher education rose. Technological abundance leaves land fixed in supply. Taxing land lowers its investment value relative to other assets, reducing speculation. (“Moore’s Law for Everything”; “Implementation and Troubleshooting”)

### Financial markets and stability

Model inputs are roughly $50 trillion in US corporate market value and $30 trillion in privately held US land excluding improvements. Altman assumes both roughly double over the decade before adjusting for a 15% fall in asset values following the tax, then recover over several years. Company growth rates need to exceed the levy substantially. (“Implementation and Troubleshooting”)

## Impact on the social contract

### Effort is rewarded

Many jobs lose economic value, even as new jobs arise. The Fund is intended to preserve opportunity and make broad gains available via ownership. (“Moore’s Law for Everything”; “Capitalism for Everyone”)

### Affordable essentials

AI lowers housing construction, healthcare and education costs. Cash and shares supplement affordability. Altman argues redistribution alone cannot keep up if essential prices continue rising. (“Moore’s Law for Everything”; “Capitalism for Everyone”)

### Reliable safety net

Dividends are a common income floor. Preventing the sale or pledging of future payouts preserves access over time. The essay compares the scale of the change to the introduction of the US social safety net under Franklin Roosevelt. (“Capitalism for Everyone”; “Implementation and Troubleshooting”; “Shifting to the New System”)

### Time for what matters

When AI makes most basics, people gain time for relationships, care, art, nature and work for social good. (“Capitalism for Everyone”)

### A say in your future

Altman associates growth and economic inclusion with lower polarization and more individual freedom. A constitutional cap on the Fund tax is meant to keep voting from compromising growth. He expects a movement for those reforms to unite a broad constituency. (“Capitalism for Everyone”; “Implementation and Troubleshooting”; “Shifting to the New System”)

### A fair share of progress

Every adult citizen receives a share of economic gains through the Fund, while technology lowers costs across the economy. The mechanism preserves returns to entrepreneurship and investment and is intended to give everyone an interest in the country's success. (“Capitalism for Everyone”)

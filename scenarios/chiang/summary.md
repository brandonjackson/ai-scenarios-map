# Will A.I. Become the New McKinsey? — summary

*Ted Chiang, "Will A.I. Become the New McKinsey?", The New Yorker, 4 May 2023: a critical essay and conditional warning about corporate incentives, centred on the United States. Read from the full text; see `extract.md`.*

**The scenario.** Firms deploy AI to cut labour costs and raise shareholder returns, with the tool playing the part of a management consultancy: it legitimises choices management already wanted to make and diffuses accountability for them. Whether AI is a set of cost-cutting tools or obedient problem-solving software, it serves the objectives its customers choose. Even where it augments workers, owners can capture the savings. Chiang's test is distribution: "The only way that technology can boost the standard of living is if there are economic policies in place to distribute the benefits of technology appropriately."

## Hard work pays
Broken for some. AI "makes it easier to lay people off", and workers who become more productive need not be paid more. Chiang points to word processors and spreadsheets: US output per person nearly doubled after 1980 while median incomes lagged, so individual productivity gains do not lift pay when policy lets the gains concentrate.

## Affordable essentials
Already slipping, and AI won't fix it. As a historical comparison, US housing, college and health care became less affordable despite the conveniences of information technology, and single-income households became rarer. Augmentation alone will not change that.

## Reliable safety net
Not in place. There is no universal basic income for workers displaced by AI. Chiang objects to deploying on the assumption that it will follow once conditions become severe, since that leaves workers exposed in the meantime and deepens inequality.

## Responsive government
Weak against capital. Redistribution depends on political action that may not come. Chiang asks whether AI could aid regulation, grassroots activism, unions and worker-owned cooperatives, and cites the 1976 Lucas Plan, but these remain open questions rather than part of the scenario.

## Time for what matters
The ideal, a century away, is a future in which no one has to do a job they dislike. Chiang ties any path there to more power for labour, which the scenario does not deliver.

## Bottom line
AI need not be very capable to break the social contract. It only needs to be useful to employers as cover for decisions they already wanted to make.

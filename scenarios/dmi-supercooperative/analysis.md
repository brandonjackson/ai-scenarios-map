# Supercooperative AI — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Off.** The model has no government, and concentration emerges from competitive markets with free entry. The authors mention regulation, competition among AI providers, capacity constraints and rigid supply contracts only as real-world factors that "would likely prevent a firm singularity". None is assumed in the scenario.

## Big assumptions
1. **Capability:** AI coordination crosses the absolute threshold (1.60 in the example), so AI firms' output per copy grows without bound with size. Task competence stays at the human average.
2. **Adoption:** frictionless. Copies scale without limit at compute cost, with no capacity limits.
3. **Markets:** increasing returns plus large demand give one giant AI-only firm in deep markets. Bounded niche demand keeps human and mixed firms alive.
4. **Politics:** absent. There is no antitrust and no compensation.

## What must be true for this to occur
- AI copies get past today's coordination failures (41–87% workflow failure, scaling exponent 0.48–0.68) by sharing full working state without loss.
- Coordination crosses the threshold before competence reaches superhuman levels.
- No regulator, rival AI provider or compute constraint stops one firm absorbing a whole deep market.

## Tensions and weak points
- **Order of arrival.** The authors read current AI as already "often exceeding human experts" in competence while coordination lags. That makes a supercooperative regime with only average competence the least likely ordering. It may be best read as a stylised corner of the framework.
- **Concentration but not full exit.** Figure 17 keeps two small human firms ("concentration need not imply human exit"), and all twelve specialists stay employed in the niche example. The map puts replacement high but below ASI.
- **Large-market limit.** The wage jump holds in the large-market limit and "does not establish a jump in a finite market". The paper also shows reorganisation briefly raising coordinator wages before cutting them (Figure 25).
- **Owners unnamed.** The giant firm's owners are not discussed, so x_labor is inferred from firm concentration and wages falling to the outside option.
- **Neighbours.** Close to `korinek-agi` and `anthropic-extreme`, less extreme than `agi-end-employment` because niche human work survives.

## Scoring notes
Scores are 1/–/2*/–/–. Work is 1 because wages "drop abruptly" to the outside option once AI firms undercut human employers, coordinators are made redundant, and workers crowding into niche and relational sectors push wages down there as well, so no sector keeps effort paying. Safety net is 2, inferred: the model has no transfers, and deep-market employment disappears while about half the coordinators in the example are unemployed. The harm falls on specific groups, not on everyone, because niche specialists stay employed. Essentials, government and time are not addressed. With two promises scored, there is no headline rating.

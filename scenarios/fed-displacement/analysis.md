# SI-Driven Expansion with Labour Disruption — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Off.** The scenario describes economic outcomes only and includes no response to the unemployment it describes. Warsh recommends no policies beyond contingency planning and notes implications for monetary policy "down the road".

## Big assumptions
1. **Capability.** SI is strong enough to drive above-trend GDP growth and to automate work "widely".
2. **Adoption.** Firms use SI to replace some jobs rather than complement them.
3. **Markets.** Labour-market frictions stop displaced workers moving quickly into new jobs, so unemployment sits above full employment while output grows.
4. **Politics.** None stated; there is no policy response.

## What must be true for this to occur
- SI raises output faster than the trend while needing fewer workers in some jobs.
- Displaced workers cannot readily move to other work (skills, place or matching frictions).
- Demand from faster growth does not create enough new jobs to absorb them.

## Tensions and weak points
- The speech does not say who gains. The map's mild concentration is inferred: output rises while some workers lose their jobs, so the gains go to capital and to those still employed. It is placed relative to `fed-shared-gains`, where gains are explicitly shared.
- Public finances are left blank: above-trend growth raises revenue, rising unemployment raises spending, and the speech says nothing to settle which wins.
- "Some jobs" and "could push" are hedged; the size of the disruption is "substantial" but unquantified, so the replacement score sits below the strong-displacement scenarios.
- Close to `dsit-rapid` and `wef-supercharged` (growth alongside displacement), but without their detail on which workers lose out.

## Scoring notes
Scores are 2/–/2*/–/–, so only two promises are scored, below the three needed for a headline rating. Work is 2: workers whose jobs are automated lose them and unemployment stays above full employment, though the speech names no group and does not describe economy-wide collapse. Safety net is 2, inferred: automation and labour-market frictions keep unemployment high during an expansion, and the scenario puts no new support in place, so existing support takes the load of job losses that are not a passing downturn. Essentials, government and time are not addressed.

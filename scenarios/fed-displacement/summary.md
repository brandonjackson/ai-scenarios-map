# SI-Driven Expansion with Labour Disruption — summary

*Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System, opening remarks by Chairman Kevin Warsh at the Productivity and Jobs Task Force Conference (San Francisco, 16 November 2026). Warsh described three illustrative scenarios that Board staff generated "applying stylized, model-based frameworks", stressing that they are "illustrative scenarios, not forecasts"; the staff model work is not published. Each scenario gets one paragraph in the speech. Read from the full text of the speech and its footnotes; see `extract.md`.*

**The scenario.** The middle of the three, introduced among outcomes that "could be less positive". Super intelligence (SI) drives an expansion, and "GDP growth would still rise above its long-term average." But the expansion brings "substantial labor market disruption as some jobs are displaced, rather than complemented, by technology", and "widespread automation, combined with labor market frictions, could push the jobless rate above levels consistent with full employment." The speech does not say which jobs, workers or places are hit, who takes the gains, or whether anyone responds with policy.

## Hard work pays
Broken for some. Workers whose jobs are automated lose them, and frictions keep unemployment above full employment even as the economy grows. The speech names no particular group.

## Affordable essentials
Not addressed.

## Reliable safety net
Broken for some, by implication. Unemployment rises because of automation and labour-market frictions, during an expansion rather than a downturn, and the scenario includes no new support for displaced workers. Above-trend growth could fund support, but the speech does not say it does.

## Responsive government
Not addressed.

## Time for what matters
Not addressed.

## Bottom line
Growth and job loss together: the economy does well while workers whose jobs are automated struggle to find new ones. The speech gives no detail on who those workers are.

# Moderate Displacement and Wage Compression — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Mixed: displacement happens anyway, and a negative income tax is assumed to follow on trigger.** Automation outpacing reinstatement is the exogenous part. The essay builds in the policy response: the Scenario 1 stabilizers are already in place, and the EITC "transitions to a negative income tax (NIT) if Scenario 2 thresholds are triggered", that is, when data show "prolonged unemployment spells and falling median wages that outpace job reinstatement". The outcome for households (a guaranteed basic standard of living) depends on that conversion. Wage insurance and UBI are discussed and rejected, so they are not linked.

## Big assumptions
1. **Capability:** AGI advances enough to cause "widespread displacement outrunning new job creation", but not enough to end job creation.
2. **Adoption:** automation outruns reinstatement. Its speed is not given.
3. **Markets:** new demand appears where "human involvement is part of the value", but it arrives late. Median wages fall, and some under-employment may be permanent.
4. **Politics:** the US converts the EITC into an income-tested floor, although the panel gives the NIT very low political support (22.9) and only moderate popular support (36.7).

## What must be true for this to occur
- Displacement is lasting but partial, so a means-tested floor (not a universal one) reaches the people who need it.
- Statistical agencies detect the trigger in time. The essay itself names data lags as a core problem.
- An NIT can be built on the EITC without a new bureaucracy, and it costs far less than UBI.
- Phase-out rates leave enough work incentive for the human-centred jobs that remain.

## Tensions and weak points
- The scenario title says "wage compression", and the trigger names falling median wages. But the extract gives no figures for either, so the map's replacement reading rests on qualitative statements.
- The NIT is chosen partly because it is "much cheaper" than UBI. Yet the essay gives no cost, and the funding analysis is in the unread SSRN paper. The fiscal axis is therefore left blank.
- The authors reject UBI because it "may also leave people with no direct stake in the automated parts of the economy". The NIT gives no stake either (Ownership of Gains 39.8). The essay defers ownership to Scenario 3.
- Neighbours on the map: `wef-supercharged`, `anthropic-substantial` and `gradual-disemp`. Its tiered counterpart is `anthropic-epf-tier2`. That tier sits lower and further left because its new UI and relief measures are recommendations only, whereas here the NIT floor is assumed.

## Scoring notes
Scores are 2/–/3/–/–. Work is 2 because the harm is concentrated: the long-term unemployed and under-employed, and workers whose wages fall, lose the route from effort to advancement, while workers in human-centred and oversight roles find new demand. Safety net is 3: income support becomes durable and is paid on income, not cause, so permanently displaced workers keep "a basic standard of living". It is not scored higher because the level is unstated and the floor is means-tested and phased out. Essentials are not addressed (the inflation warning is about UBI, which the scenario rejects). Government and time are not addressed. No headline rating, because only two promises are scored.

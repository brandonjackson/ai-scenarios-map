# Moderate Displacement and Wage Compression — summary

*Scenario 2 of "Economic policy for AGI" by Julian Jacobs and Alex Imas, an essay from the DeepMind Institute (Google DeepMind) published on 16 September 2026. The essay sets out three scenarios by depth of labour-market disruption and matches each with a "least-regret" policy and an empirical trigger. Read from the full essay. The longer SSRN working paper could not be read; see `extract.md`.*

**The scenario.** AGI capabilities advance far enough to cause "moderate-to-broad displacement": "more substantial labor market churn, prolonged unemployment spells, and potentially permanent under/unemployment". New demand appears "in sectors where human involvement is part of the value", but "labor reinstatement effects may take longer to unfold relative to the pace of automation", and median wages fall. Once data show "prolonged unemployment spells and falling median wages that outpace job reinstatement", the expanded EITC from Scenario 1 becomes a negative income tax (NIT). This is a cash transfer paid automatically below an income threshold and phased out as earnings rise.

## Hard work pays
Broken for some. Workers in roles that need human involvement, expertise or oversight find new demand. Others face long spells out of work, possible permanent under-employment and falling wages. The NIT tries to keep work worthwhile by phasing out "as their earned wages rise", "preserving incentives for work". But it cannot bring back the jobs that automation outpaces.

## Affordable essentials
Not addressed. The authors warn that a universal basic income "risks kindling inflationary pressure", which is one reason they prefer the NIT. They do not describe prices in the scenario itself.

## Reliable safety net
Stable, on a new footing. Safety nets "shift from temporary transition support to durable income support". The NIT supports people "based strictly on income level, rather than the cause of income loss", so no one has to prove that AI caused their job loss. The state "could guarantee a basic standard of living". The essay gives no benefit level.

## Responsive government
Not addressed. The panel gives the NIT low political support (22.9 out of 100), but the essay does not say how politics plays out in the scenario.

## Time for what matters
Not addressed.

## Bottom line
Durable displacement met with a durable, means-tested income floor. The worst-off keep a basic standard of living but lose their route to a better one.

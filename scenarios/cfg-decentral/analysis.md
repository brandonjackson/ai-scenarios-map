# Decentralised Mayhem — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Mixed: a permissive opening, then emergency restrictions.** The misuse wave happens without deliberate policy; EU authorities even hesitate to restrict open weights because local models serve public services and privacy. The end-state, though, is shaped by policy: NATO incident coordination, ENISA intervention powers, a harmonised US–EU–China ban on open publication above 10²⁶ FLOP, resilience investment and public education about AI scams. Previously rated off ("off until a crisis forces bans"); the fuller extract makes the policy response part of the scenario itself.

## Big assumptions
1. **Capability: a late jump in a plateaued world.** After years of weak frontier returns, one 2027 run delivers reliable long-horizon agents, and an open-weight rival matches it within four months.
2. **Adoption: open and fast among small users.** Developers, SMEs, hobbyists and public services adopt the open model quickly, and once weights circulate they can't be withdrawn.
3. **Markets: defence becomes a business.** Damage from unsafe releases creates a lucrative market for closed defensive AI, with above-trend 2029 compute investment for defensive models.
4. **Politics: reactive coordination.** Costly incidents bring rare US–EU–China agreement on release limits while wider diplomacy stays weak.

## What must be true for this to occur
- An open-weight release reaches near-frontier capability while its safeguards can be stripped.
- Offence outpaces defence for smaller firms, local governments and less-protected operators.
- No major jurisdiction restricts open releases until after serious incidents.
- Rival powers can agree a compute threshold for publication even while tolerating attacks on each other.

## Tensions and weak points
- **Map position moved.** The old coordinates (0.42, 0.55; strong replacement with diffuse gains) were set when this point was described as a fast, decentralised takeoff. The extract places it under Plateau, with no labour projection, so it now sits at mild augmentation (−0.2), slightly above Plateau's −0.42 because of the 2027 capability jump. Gains move from 0.42 to 0.1: open weights spread access at first, but restrictions and reliance on closed defence tools "redirect gains to a narrower group of providers". Job quality (−0.05) and public finances (−0.2) reflect business disruption, billions in incident costs and new public spending on cyber defence and resilience, with no fiscal path given.
- Most of the economy sections are not addressed and the technology and adoption sections repeat Plateau's, so the ending is thinly described as an economy.
- The same openness produces both the early access and the harm, and the policy response undoes the decentralisation. It is a trade-off, not a destination.
- Compare Buterin's d/acc, which argues decentralised defensive technology can tip the offence/defence balance; here defence becomes a closed, paid service instead.
- On the labour map its nearest neighbours are `tbi`, `anthropic-modest`, `gos-slow-burn`, `tcb-augmentation` and `imf-baseline`, limited-impact stories, which fits an ending whose shock is to security and trust rather than to work. It sits between Plateau and the centre of the map, left of Plateau because gains re-concentrate.

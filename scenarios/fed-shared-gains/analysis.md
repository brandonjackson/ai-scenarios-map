# Broadly Shared SI Gains — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Off.** The scenario describes economic outcomes only, with no government or central-bank response. Warsh recommends no policies; he says the Fed's role is "neither advocacy nor opposition" and that SI will have "important implications for monetary policy down the road", but none of that is part of the scenario.

## Big assumptions
1. **Capability.** SI raises productivity enough to lift real GDP growth "persistently above its long-run average" within several years, by speeding up idea generation itself.
2. **Adoption.** Use is wide enough to show up in aggregate growth. The speech says nothing about sectors, barriers or pace.
3. **Markets.** The gains reach workers as well as capital owners, and the labour market absorbs the change, staying "close to full employment". The scenario does not say how gains are shared (wages, prices, ownership).
4. **Politics.** None stated; the good outcome comes without new policy.

## What must be true for this to occur
- Total factor productivity breaks out "definitively and structurally to the upside", which Warsh says has not yet happened.
- SI mostly complements work rather than displacing it, or displaced workers are re-employed quickly. This is implied by the contrast with `fed-displacement`.
- Competition or bargaining power is strong enough that capital owners do not keep most of the gains.

## Tensions and weak points
- The whole scenario is one sentence of outcomes. Broad sharing is asserted, not explained, and the staff model behind it is not published.
- Augmentation is inferred from the contrast with the middle scenario ("displaced, rather than complemented"), not stated here, so the map point is hollow on `y_labor`; the fiscal position is inferred from growth and full employment.
- Warsh's own optimism and his stated "ultimate scenario" (lives extended, human flourishing) colour the speech, but the scenario itself is a modest macro outcome: above-trend growth at full employment.
- Close on the map to `gos-augmented-growth`, `korinek-aug` and `tcb-augmentation`, but much thinner than any of them.

## Scoring notes
Scores are 4/–/3*/–/–, so only two promises are scored, below the three needed for a headline rating. Work is 4: near full employment and gains "shared broadly across labor" mean work pays more, and no group is named as losing out; it is not 5 because nothing says the next generation's prospects change profoundly. Safety net is 3, inferred: near full employment leaves little load on support and growth above trend widens the tax base, but nothing says the net improves. Essentials, government and time are not addressed; the falling "marginal cost of satisfying curiosity" applies across the set and says nothing about essentials.

# Broadly Shared SI Gains — summary

*Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System, opening remarks by Chairman Kevin Warsh at the Productivity and Jobs Task Force Conference (San Francisco, 16 November 2026). Warsh described three illustrative scenarios that Board staff generated "applying stylized, model-based frameworks", stressing that they are "illustrative scenarios, not forecasts"; the staff model work is not published. Each scenario gets one paragraph in the speech. Read from the full text of the speech and its footnotes; see `extract.md`.*

**The scenario.** The most positive of the three, at "one end" of the range. Super intelligence (SI) "leads to an increase in productivity and economic gains that are shared broadly across labor and the owners of capital." Real GDP growth "rises persistently above its long-run average, and the U.S. stays close to full employment." The speech's wider argument explains why: SI could "supercharge idea generation", automating the creation of ideas in a way earlier general-purpose technologies could not. The horizon is "the next several years". Beyond that one paragraph the scenario says nothing about who gains most, prices, public finances or politics.

## Hard work pays
Improving. With the economy close to full employment and labour sharing in the productivity gains, work keeps paying and pays more. The speech names no group that is left behind, but it also gives no detail on wages, occupations or entry-level jobs.

## Affordable essentials
Not addressed. The speech says the marginal cost of satisfying curiosity is "falling fast, perhaps even trending toward zero", but it says nothing about housing, food, energy or care.

## Reliable safety net
Stable, by implication. Near full employment leaves few people needing support, and growth above trend widens the tax base. The scenario says nothing about the safety net itself.

## Responsive government
Not addressed. Warsh's general view that "humans must be the deciders" and that the Fed's role is "neither advocacy nor opposition" is not a description of politics in this scenario.

## Time for what matters
Not addressed.

## Bottom line
A one-paragraph best case: faster growth, full employment, and gains shared between workers and capital. It shows what the Fed staff see as the upside, not how it would come about.

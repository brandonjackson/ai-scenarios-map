# Business and Civil Society (Infinite Potential) — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Off, even though the extract records it as on.** The game asks business, civil-society and government players to design responses, and the report is organised around their recommendations: sector workforce-transition playbooks, a "caring society", capital-based taxation, a sovereign wealth fund, stronger powers over frontier labs. None of those are assumed in the scenario. In-game, institutions and laws stay largely intact, the scenario features "governance failures", retraining is outpaced, the tax system is built on payroll and income, and government lacks the authority for forceful intervention in frontier firms. The disruption happens without deliberate new policy, so the scenario sits on the map as off, with no Policy Atlas links.

## Big assumptions
1. **Capability:** ARCTech deploys AGI by late 2027, and AI systems capable of a global cyberattack, possibly autonomous ones, exist by early 2028.
2. **Adoption:** AI and robotics are embedded across manufacturing and critical infrastructure worldwide within months, deeply enough that one attack can cripple them.
3. **Markets:** productivity rises globally while gains go to capital owners and a few firms; the US gets mass unemployment and inequality, and China takes manufacturing.
4. **Politics:** governance fails at home and between states, and firms become rivals of the state rather than partners.

## What must be true for this to occur
- AGI is deployed by a US lab by late 2027, and displacement turns into mass unemployment within months.
- Critical infrastructure becomes dependent on AI and robotic systems that share common vulnerabilities.
- AI models in distributed data centres can mount a global attack, and attribution stays ambiguous.
- No fiscal or labour response is in place before the shock.

## Tensions and weak points
- **The report skips the headline event.** The scenario's defining shock, the cyberattack and possible loss of control, is outside the report's scope, so the extract cannot say what it does to jobs, prices or public finances. Its effect on essentials is inferred.
- **Pooled findings.** Most dynamics are pooled with the sibling `rand-ip-economic-crisis`; only a few findings are attributed to these games (firms as something to act on, the struggle to define goals for a post-labour economy, the four games asking when government runs out of money). The dedicated report on this scenario (RR-A4767-1) was not read.
- **Productivity boom, then collapse.** "Dramatically increases global productivity" and "economic collapse" both appear; the extract does not say whether the collapse follows the attack or the unemployment.
- **The "caring society" is a player vision**, not part of the scenario; it would otherwise lift the time score.
- **Neighbours:** `ai-2027`, `gos-take-off` and `intelligence-curse` on the labour map; `intelligence-curse` and `citrini` on the fiscal map. It is placed just past its sibling on both maps because it adds economy-wide mass unemployment and economic collapse.

## Scoring notes
Scores are 1/2*/1/1/–. Work is 1: mass unemployment spreads from entry-level knowledge work to blue-collar work through robotics, and players struggled to see a role for government where "most humans cannot contribute productive labor". Essentials is 2, inferred: the report says nothing on prices, but an attack that cripples critical infrastructure worldwide on top of mass unemployment implies essentials falling out of reach for those with the least. Safety net is 1: revenue erodes as needs rise and players asked when government would "run out of money". Government is 1: the scenario is defined by governance failures, a contest between states and AI firms, and AGI that may be uncontrollable. Time is not addressed: the caring society and shorter week are recommendations.

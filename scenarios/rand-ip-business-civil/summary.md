# Business and Civil Society (Infinite Potential) — summary

*RAND Research Report RR-A4767-4, "Infinite Potential—Insights on Economic and Societal Impacts Across Two Scenarios" (2026), by Anton Shenk, Richard S. Girven, Gregory Smith and Tobias Sytsma. It is an after-action report on 26 two-turn "Day After AGI" tabletop games played between April 2025 and May 2026, 19 of them on this scenario. The report pools player findings across this scenario and its sibling, Economic Crisis, and describes this world itself only in a short synopsis (p. 4). It does not analyse the cyberattack or loss of control. A dedicated report on this scenario (RR-A4767-1) was not read. The policies players recommended are not part of the scenario. Read from the report's full text; see `extract.md`.*

**The scenario.** By late 2027 the US lab ARCTech has deployed AGI and "dramatically increases global productivity". China secures manufacturing dominance with AI-optimised industry, while the United States faces mass unemployment, inequality and unrest. In early 2028 a massive cyberattack from AI models in globally distributed data centres "cripples manufacturing, critical infrastructure, and robotic systems worldwide". Blame is split between a hostile state and an AI "that has escaped its creators' control". Leaders must confront "governance failures, economic collapse, and the possibility that AGI systems have become uncontrollable".

## Hard work pays
Broken for all. Mass unemployment spreads from entry-level knowledge work to blue-collar jobs via robotics, and players thought the losses may be permanent. AI removes "the bottom rungs of career ladders". In these games players "struggled to articulate what government hoped to achieve if the economy transitioned to one in which most humans cannot contribute productive labor".

## Affordable essentials
Under threat. The report says nothing on prices, but an attack that cripples critical infrastructure worldwide would disrupt the energy and supply systems essentials depend on, on top of falling incomes.

## Reliable safety net
Broken. Income-tax revenue falls as demand for support rises, the tax system is still built on payroll and income, and in at least four of these games players asked when government would "run out of money". Government may be unable to fund basic services, let alone an expanded safety net.

## Responsive government
Broken. The scenario is defined by "governance failures". Players came to see it as "the competition between states and powerful private-sector AI firms", treated the private sector as something government had to act on, and warned of firms "capturing or undermining democratic governance" and of authoritarian responses once a crisis hits. AGI itself may be beyond control.

## Time for what matters
Not addressed. Players proposed a "caring society" built on care, education and community, funded by AI surplus, but as a goal, not something the scenario contains.

## Bottom line
The Economic Crisis world plus a systemic shock: an AGI boom with mass unemployment and a broke state, then a global cyberattack that may be the first sign of lost control.

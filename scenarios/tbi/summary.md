# The Impact of AI on the Labour Market — summary

*Tony Blair Institute for Global Change, November 2024 · UK-focused · see the analysis for source limits*

**The scenario.** A managed transition for the UK. AI could save "almost a quarter of private-sector workforce time", roughly the work of 6 million people. Over time it could displace **1–3 million jobs**, but losses are "capped and ultimately offset" as AI creates new demand. Because adoption is gradual, unemployment rises at a peak of **60,000–275,000 jobs a year** (about 180,000 by 2030), "in the low hundreds of thousands", and the effect "will unwind over time". GDP rises by up to 1% within five years and **5–14% by 2050** (the map uses about 11%).

## Hard work pays
Bent, not broken. Administration, sales, customer service, banking, finance and data-heavy work are most exposed. Most workers keep working, with jobs reshaped by AI that saves time. Displaced workers need help into new roles. The friction is real but temporary.

## Affordable essentials
Indirect gains. Higher productivity and GDP growth should support living standards and public services. The report doesn't target housing, energy or care costs specifically.

## Reliable safety net
Adequate if updated. TBI recommends financial safety nets and retraining, contingency plans in case the transition is slower or rougher than expected, and better job matching, including AI-enabled public employment services, to shorten spells out of work.

## Responsive government
Expected to be capable and proactive, the TBI's general view: an "AI-era state" that uses the technology to deliver services, improve labour-market matching and plan for disruption. Growth gives government more fiscal room.

## Time for what matters
Possible gains. A quarter of workforce time saved could, in principle, become shorter hours or less drudgery. The report treats it mainly as productivity.

## Bottom line
The "normal transition" view for a single economy: real but modest, temporary unemployment, offset by growth and new jobs, handled with the existing welfare-state toolkit plus better retraining and matching.

# SI disappoints — extract

## Summary
Super intelligence (SI) disappoints. After significant capital investment and stock market gains, expected returns turn down and equity valuations fall, leading to an economic slowdown and "a more notable rise in unemployment." In time the economy recovers, "but without the productivity gains" (¶ "Or SI could disappoint"). It is the least positive of three illustrative scenarios that Federal Reserve Board staff generated with stylised, model-based frameworks. Chair Warsh presented them as a starting point for discussion, not as forecasts (¶ "Still, it's worth considering"; ¶ "These are illustrative scenarios").

## Key details
- **Source:** Opening Remarks (Speech by Chairman Kevin Warsh at the Productivity and Jobs Task Force Conference, hosted by the Federal Reserve Board and the Federal Reserve Bank of San Francisco, San Francisco, California), Kevin Warsh, Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System, 16 November 2026. `sources/fed-warsh-si-scenarios-2026/`
- **Other sources for this scenario:** None
- **Reading notes:** Full text of the speech and its six footnotes. The speech describes the scenario in a single paragraph; the underlying staff model work is not published with it. Locators cite paragraphs by their opening words, e.g. (¶ "At one end"). The scenario has no name in the source; the title here is descriptive.
- **Scenario set:** Three illustrative Board-staff scenarios for the next several years, "depending on how SI progresses": (1) at "one end", productivity gains shared broadly across labour and capital with near full employment (`fed-shared-gains`); (2) an SI-driven expansion with substantial labour-market disruption and unemployment above full-employment levels (`fed-displacement`); (3) this one, in which SI disappoints, valuations fall and unemployment rises more notably. The scenarios were generated by "applying stylized, model-based frameworks". No explicit axes are named. *[Inference: the scenarios differ on the size of the productivity gain and whether jobs are complemented or displaced.]* (¶ "Still, it's worth considering"; ¶ "At one end"; ¶ "Other outcomes"; ¶ "Or SI could disappoint").
- **Geography:** United States.
- **Authors' framing:** Planning scenario for contingency planning. "These are illustrative scenarios, not forecasts." "Our job is to prepare for whatever comes next. That means contingency planning" (¶ "These are illustrative scenarios"; ¶ "Our job is to prepare"). Warsh: "we're certainly not here to make predictions" (¶ "My Fed colleagues and I").
- **Authors' view of likelihood:** Not stated for this scenario. Across the set: "many other outcomes—probably including one that will prove most accurate—could be drawn" (¶ "These are illustrative scenarios").

Horizon: "over the next several years" (¶ "Still, it's worth considering"). No dated events.

## Key dynamics explored
*Applies across the set*

- Ideas are "the ultimate resource" in endogenous growth theory. Because ideas are nonrival, their value grows with the number of users, and a doubling of ideas and other key inputs can more than double output (increasing returns to scale; Romer) (¶ "In the tradition of endogenous growth theory"; ¶ "While an idea can be costly"; fn. 1).
- Breakthrough ideas have been harder to find in the US. Research effort has risen more than 20 times since the 1930s, but research productivity has fallen by more (Bloom, Jones et al.) (¶ "Even so, in the U.S."; fn. 2). Slowing population growth may mean fewer new ideas. Together these help explain the return of the secular-stagnation theme (¶ "Long-run growth, I should note"; fn. 3).
- SI might differ from past general-purpose technologies because it could automate the creation of ideas and "supercharge idea generation", changing "the method—and the speed—of innovation". If idea generation broadens, growth could be sustained "substantially higher than the long-run average" (¶ "SI might be somewhat different"; fn. 4).
- The big open questions are "magnitude, scope, and timing": whether SI raises total factor productivity, whether democratised specialised knowledge raises living standards, when the surge starts and tapers, and whether it happens sooner and faster in the US than elsewhere (¶ "The big questions").

*This scenario*

- SI disappoints after significant capital investment and stock market gains. Expected returns turn down, and equity valuations fall (¶ "Or SI could disappoint").
- Falling valuations lead to an economic slowdown and a more notable rise in unemployment. The economy recovers in time, but without the productivity gains (¶ "Or SI could disappoint").

## State of technology
*Applies across the set.* SI is described as "the general-purpose technology called 'super intelligence'" and "an entirely new force in economic life and society" (¶ "The current era"). It gives "instant access to genius-level intelligence on almost any subject", and the marginal cost of satisfying curiosity "is falling fast, perhaps even trending toward zero" (¶ "Even a few years ago"). It could automate the creation of ideas, unlike the internal combustion engine or electricity (¶ "SI might be somewhat different"). Machines "do not take the place of human judgment, morality, or responsibility" (¶ "For all of that"). Market structure, providers, compute, energy and pricing: Not addressed.

*This scenario.* "SI could disappoint": expected returns on SI investment turn down, and the expected productivity gains do not arrive (¶ "Or SI could disappoint"). In what way the technology falls short: Not addressed.

## State of adoption
*Applies across the set.* "The widespread adoption of this technology will have significant impact on the economy" (¶ "And, of course"). Warsh says the United States is "leading" in generating ideas (¶ "In the tradition of endogenous growth theory"). He asks whether the surge will "happen sooner and faster here than in other countries" and whether adversaries will "race to deploy SI" (¶ "The big questions"). Sectors, firm sizes and barriers: Not addressed.

## How the economy works

### GDP growth and productivity
- *This scenario:* Falling equity valuations lead to "an economic slowdown". "In time, the economy would recover, but without the productivity gains" (¶ "Or SI could disappoint").
- *Applies across the set:* Productivity growth "has fluctuated, and in recent quarters, it's been reasonably strong. But it hasn't yet broken out definitively and structurally to the upside" (¶ "Even so, in the U.S."). If idea generation broadens, growth could be sustained substantially above the long-run average (¶ "SI might be somewhat different"). Open question: "Will this new technology deliver a rise in total factor productivity?" (¶ "The big questions").

### Labour market dynamics
- *This scenario:* "A more notable rise in unemployment" during the slowdown (¶ "Or SI could disappoint").
- *Applies across the set:* "There is no replacing the minds and the hands of men and women" (¶ "For all of that").

*Applies across the set.* "If living standards are to advance, conditions must be favorable to the generation of new ideas" (¶ "In the tradition of endogenous growth theory"). Open question: "Will the democratization of specialized knowledge significantly raise standards of living?" (¶ "The big questions").

## Other dynamics
*Applies across the set.*
- **Security and bad actors:** "SI empowers all, including bad actors, so we are thinking especially of critical infrastructures and their potential vulnerabilities." Risks, "if overlooked, can be highly disruptive and economically costly" (¶ "As policymakers").
- **Geopolitics:** "Will adversaries race to deploy SI, intending to do harm to our economy?" (¶ "The big questions"). "Can you be a superpower without super intelligence?" (¶ "For our country in this era"; fn. 5).
- **Human control:** "Humans are the designers and the builders, and humans must be the deciders" (¶ "For all of that").
- **Monetary policy:** SI adoption has "important implications for monetary policy down the road" (¶ "And, of course").
- **Aspirational goal:** Quoting Cogan and Warsh (2022): "strong, durable economic growth, abundant economic opportunities, vibrant and secure communities, and a healthy environment" (¶ "I thought back recently"; fn. 6).

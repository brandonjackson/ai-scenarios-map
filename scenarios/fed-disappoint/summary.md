# SI Disappoints — summary

*Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System, opening remarks by Chairman Kevin Warsh at the Productivity and Jobs Task Force Conference (San Francisco, 16 November 2026). Warsh described three illustrative scenarios that Board staff generated "applying stylized, model-based frameworks", stressing that they are "illustrative scenarios, not forecasts"; the staff model work is not published. Each scenario gets one paragraph in the speech. Read from the full text of the speech and its footnotes; see `extract.md`.*

**The scenario.** The least positive of the three. "After significant capital investment and stock market gains, expected returns could turn down and equity valuations could fall, leading to an economic slowdown and a more notable rise in unemployment. In time, the economy would recover, but without the productivity gains." It is a boom and bust in AI investment, not a story of AI taking jobs: the unemployment comes from the slowdown. The speech does not say how SI falls short, who loses most, or how long the slump lasts.

## Hard work pays
Broken for some. The slowdown brings "a more notable rise in unemployment" than the displacement scenario. When the economy recovers, the productivity gains that might have raised pay never come.

## Affordable essentials
Not addressed.

## Reliable safety net
Stable, by implication. Job losses come from a cyclical slowdown that ends in recovery, so they are temporary rather than permanent. The scenario says nothing about the support people get in the meantime.

## Responsive government
Not addressed.

## Time for what matters
Not addressed.

## Bottom line
An AI investment bust: a recession with higher unemployment, then a recovery to the old trend. The main harm is a cyclical one rather than lasting change.

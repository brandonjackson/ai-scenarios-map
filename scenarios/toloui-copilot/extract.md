# Copilot Empire — extract

## Summary

Transformative AI augments human labour under a concentrated market structure (“A Typology for Technological Revolutions”). Its use can raise workers’ productivity and enable complementary work, while suppliers retain entrenched market power (“Which Quadrant Is Our AI Future?”). Historical infrastructure and computing analogues illustrate that expanded living standards and employment can coexist with coercion of captive customers, political influence and gains skewed toward favoured groups (“Copilot Empire” historical examples).

## Key details

- **Source:** Titans, Swarms, or Human Renaissance? Technological Revolutions and Policy Lessons for the AI Age, Ramin Toloui, The Digitalist Papers, Vol. 2, 2025-12-11. `sources/toloui-titans-swarms-2025/`
- **Other sources for this scenario:** None
- **Reading notes:** Full essay, including the typology, historical analogues, AI outlook, and five policy priorities.
- **Scenario set:** Two axes form four quadrants. Winner-take-all versus fast-follower market structure crosses labour replacement versus labour augmentation. The four quadrants are Titan’s Dominion, Copilot Empire, Disruption Swarm and Promethean Fire. The constructive Copilot row covers an expressly mentioned policy-dependent form of Copilot Empire (“A Typology for Technological Revolutions”; “Priority 3”).
- **Geography:** The policy agenda is US-centred, with international comparison, economic rivalry and historical examples from other countries (“Applying the Matrix to the Technological Past”; “Complicating Factors”; “Five Major Policy Implications”).
- **Authors' framing:** A typology and adaptable policy agenda for transformative AI, grounded in historical analogues. The quadrants are conceptual possibilities (“A Typology for Technological Revolutions”; “Conclusion”).
- **Authors' view of likelihood:** No probabilities. The author finds evidence for all four combinations and says the outcome depends on interaction between the technology and institutions (“Which Quadrant Is Our AI Future?”).

### Timeline

No dated scenario storyline. Evidence used to motivate the axes includes ChatGPT’s late-2022 launch and China’s DeepSeek entering in early 2025 (“Which Quadrant Is Our AI Future?”).

### Policy stance

- **Stance:** Off. The quadrant’s defining conditions are concentrated market power and labour augmentation. Constructive regulation is a policy-dependent variant (“A Typology for Technological Revolutions”; “Priority 3”).
- **Policies assumed in the scenario:** The generic AI quadrant assumes no particular package. In historical analogues, antitrust, regulatory innovation and organized labour responded to monopoly abuses (“Copilot Empire” historical examples).
- **Policies the source recommends:** *Applies across the set.* Prepare adjustable policy levers with different settings for different quadrants. (1) Shift the tax burden from hiring to capital or super-normal rents, revisit a public health insurance option and adjust wage subsidies or transfers if needed (“Priority 1”). (2) Back worker-augmenting research, procurement and training via federal R&D, NAIRR compute and data, SBIR/STTR prototypes, WIOA, apprenticeships and employer–college partnerships, and use adoption measures and voluntary certification (“Priority 2”). (3) Start with interoperable APIs, portable customer data, limits on exclusivity and cloud switching barriers, and expand DOJ/FTC capacity for stronger interventions if bottlenecks emerge (“Priority 3”). (4) Require reporting of advanced training and compute, independent evaluations, material incident reporting, disclosure, meaningful human review and appeals. Consider due-process withdrawal powers and updated tort standards with safe harbours (“Priority 4”). (5) Expand energy, grid and chip supply while protecting households and small businesses from utility cost shifts. Modernize lawful, privacy-protective data sharing, run regulatory sandboxes and extend diffusion assistance to small firms (“Priority 5”). The quadrant-specific priority is retaining AI’s productive benefits while preventing monopoly abuse (“Five Major Policy Implications”).

## Key dynamics explored

- Scale and first-mover effects can consolidate the frontier while augmentation expands the uses of AI and keeps complementary human capabilities valuable (“A Typology for Technological Revolutions”; “Which Quadrant Is Our AI Future?”).
- Productivity and market expansion can coexist with wage polarization, because augmentation and replacement affect different tasks and workers (“Copilot Empire” historical examples; “Which Quadrant Is Our AI Future?”).
- Installed scale, customer switching barriers and political influence can entrench power (“Copilot Empire” historical examples; “Priority 3”).

## State of technology

High fixed training and infrastructure costs and low marginal provision costs favour large incumbents. Proprietary models, network effects, first-mover advantages and data-driven feedback can entrench them. Continued scale gains with slowing algorithmic efficiency could push training and serving beyond all but a few large firms (“A Typology for Technological Revolutions”; “Which Quadrant Is Our AI Future?”).

## State of adoption

*Applies across the set.* Integrating a general-purpose technology requires workflow and process redesign, skills and complementary investments. Institutional inertia and social resistance may slow replacement, though automating may be easier initially than developing effective augmentation. Organizational differences, task requirements and worker readiness mean adoption can vary sharply (“A Typology for Technological Revolutions”; “Which Quadrant Is Our AI Future?”). Integration into workflows confers an incumbency advantage, but worker and managerial skills and redesign determine realized augmentation (“A Typology for Technological Revolutions”; “Which Quadrant Is Our AI Future?”).

## How the economy works

### GDP growth and productivity

The author’s historical analogues show that concentrated ownership of enabling technologies can expand markets and enable new industries even as abuse occurs. AI augmentation studies show productivity gains whose incidence varies by field. The essay gives no growth path for the AI quadrant (“Copilot Empire” historical examples; “Which Quadrant Is Our AI Future?”).

### Labour market dynamics

Augmentation can increase demand for operators, maintenance and integration workers, lower costs to expand demand, and enable products and industries requiring new work. Potential complements include human empathy, personal connection, physical presence, judgement, imagination and leadership. Early evidence favours less experienced workers in call centres, software, writing, law and consulting, and experienced workers in accounting and entrepreneurship (“A Typology for Technological Revolutions”; “Which Quadrant Is Our AI Future?”).

### Winners and losers

Platforms can keep market power while workers whose skills complement AI gain. The historical communications and computing experience shows aggregate gains can coexist with sharp skill polarization, as high-skill wages rose and lower-skill wages stagnated or fell (“Copilot Empire” historical examples).

### Household incomes and living standards

The historical Copilot examples raised living standards, incomes and opportunity while concentrating gains unevenly across skills. The author stresses heterogeneity in the actual AI task evidence (“Copilot Empire” historical examples; “Which Quadrant Is Our AI Future?”).

### Government finances

*Applies across the set.* Accelerated depreciation and expensing expanded in 2025, and low effective taxes on profits, dividends and capital gains contrast with the income/payroll tax wedge and employers’ health-benefit costs. That mix can favour automation of workers even for small gains. Toloui suggests funding Social Security and Medicare payroll tax cuts with earmarked capital revenue, possibly focusing on super-normal AI rents if concentration dominates (“Priority 1”).

### International economic patterns

*Applies across the set.* US–China competition for AI leadership and global influence pushes towards rapid research or deployment. Misuse, misalignment and economic disruption favour caution. The US, EU and China approach regulation differently. Chip resilience depends on investment at home and on allied partnerships for critical inputs (“Complicating Factors”; “Priority 5”).

### Market pricing and scarcity

Monopolists can use captive customer relationships, exclusivity, switching barriers or bottleneck control to extract rents. Interoperability and portability can lower the barriers to switching (“Copilot Empire” historical examples; “Priority 3”).

### Reliable safety net

*Applies across the set.* Larger supports could be required with strong displacement and unequal gains. Examples are an expanded earned income tax credit, wage insurance and transfers independent of employment. A public option for health insurance could lower nonwage hiring costs (“Priority 1”).

### Time for what matters

Not addressed.

### A say in your future

The historical analogues include monopolists cultivating political allies and the institutional response of antitrust and organized labour. The AI agenda includes democratic disclosure, human review and appeals in consequential decisions (“Copilot Empire” historical examples; “Priority 4”).

### A fair share of progress

The central policy problem is how to harness a productive technology while preventing monopolistic abuse and keeping a wider share of gains accessible (“Five Major Policy Implications”).

## Other dynamics

*Applies across the set.* Transformative AI can amplify misuse, bias, discrimination and misalignment. Incidents can also undermine public support for useful diffusion. Demographic decline and a shrinking labour force in many countries could offset some labour-saving effects (“Which Quadrant Is Our AI Future?”; “Complicating Factors”; “Priority 4”).

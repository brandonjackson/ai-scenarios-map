# Augmented Growth — summary

*UK Government Office for Science, "AI Scenarios 2030: Helping policymakers plan for the future of AI" (15 June 2026). One of five exploratory scenarios built on six uncertainties; Augmented Growth pairs mid-to-high capability with the strongest security and global cooperation and low displacement. The authors say the scenarios "are not predictions". Read from the full GOV.UK report, including the outlook to 2040 and the cross-cutting findings; see `extract.md`.*

**The scenario.** By 2030 AI can automate most remote-worker tasks, matches average people at most cognitive work and drives scientific breakthroughs and an economic boom. Society, law and compute shortages keep people involved in most tasks. Adoption restores early-2000s growth rates, and the economy could grow by as much as a third over the 2030s. Allies agree shared safety standards, though compliance is hard to verify and faster-adopting countries pull ahead.

## Hard work pays
Improving. All cognitive roles are exposed but augmentation dominates: "Most firms also use AI to increase output rather than drastically reduce headcounts, with overall unemployment remaining stable." New jobs appear in the AI sector, oversight and integration, and real wages grow at rates last seen in the 2000s, spilling into hospitality and construction. Low-variety roles are displaced, and some lower-skilled work becomes more demanding and less rewarding.

## Affordable essentials
Improving for care. Transformed public services improve healthcare access and outcomes through AI-supported care, treatments and diagnostics. The source gives no change in the prices of other essentials.

## Reliable safety net
Not addressed. Stable unemployment means existing support is not tested.

## Responsive government
Not addressed directly. Elsewhere, public trust in AI is reasonably high given better public services, though below trust in people and tempered by worries about energy, displacement and inequality. Allies cooperate on safety standards after serious incidents.

## Time for what matters
Not addressed. Deskilling leaves some workers dependent on AI and less capable when it fails.

## Bottom line
The good outcome of the five, but a snapshot: "The 'labour-augmenting' nature of AI cannot be guaranteed long-term", and weaker human-involvement limits could bring structural unemployment in the 2030s.

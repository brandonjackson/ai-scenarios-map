# Augmented Growth — summary

*UK Government Office for Science, with AISI and DSIT, "AI Scenarios 2030: Helping policymakers plan for the future of AI" (15 June 2026)*

**The scenario.** By 2030 "AI can automate most tasks that a remote human worker could perform, driving major scientific breakthroughs, transforming public services, and creating an economic boom." Systems become reliable enough to trust widely, interpretability lets developers verify their reasoning, and allies agree shared safety standards. Demand outruns energy and data-centre capacity, creating compute bottlenecks.

## Hard work pays
Improving. "All cognitive roles are exposed to AI, which remains primarily complementary to human labour": societal, practical and legal limits keep humans "in the loop" for most tasks. Roles with high exposure and little variety are displaced, but "many new roles emerge" in the AI sector, integration and oversight. Productivity pushes real-wage growth to "levels last seen in the 2000s", and rising salaries create jobs in hospitality and construction. Some skilled roles shift towards strategy, creativity and relationships, while some lower-skilled roles become "more demanding and less rewarding."

## Affordable essentials
Improving. "Many citizens benefit from transformed public services, including better healthcare access and outcomes through AI-driven treatments, diagnostics, and personalised care plans."

## Reliable safety net
Stable. "Most firms also use AI to increase output rather than drastically reduce headcounts", so overall unemployment stays stable and existing support is enough.

## Responsive government
Improving. Public trust in AI is "reasonably high in light of transformed public services", though still below trust in people. Internationally, allies set shared safety standards and humans stay in the loop in military uses. Richer, faster-adopting nations pull ahead.

## Time for what matters
Mixed. AI "increasingly atrophies many workers' cognitive skills", and workers struggle when systems fail.

## Bottom line
The good outcome of the five, and the only one where strong capability and a strong social contract coexist. GO-Science warns that "the 'labour-augmenting' nature of AI cannot be guaranteed long-term" and could give way to structural unemployment in the 2030s.

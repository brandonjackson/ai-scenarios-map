# Augmented Growth — extract

## Summary

AI can automate most remote-worker tasks by 2030, with improved reliability, scientific breakthroughs and an economic boom (ch. 3, “Augmented Growth”, opening and Capability). Society, law and compute constraints keep people involved for most tasks. New roles, productivity gains and additional demand for labour counterbalance displacement and keep unemployment stable (ch. 3, “Augmented Growth”, Labour displacement). Public services improve and allies establish safety standards, though compliance is hard to verify and faster-adopting countries pull ahead (ch. 3, “Augmented Growth”, Adoption and Global cooperation).

## Key details

- **Source:** AI Scenarios 2030: Helping policymakers plan for the future of AI, Government Office for Science, Government Office for Science (GOV.UK), 2026-06-15. `sources/go-science-ai-scenarios-2030-2026/`
- **Other sources for this scenario:** None.
- **Reading notes:** Full GOV.UK HTML report, including the scenario narrative, the outlook to 2040, and the cross-cutting findings.
- **Scenario set:** Slow Burn and Open Frontier (trajectory 1), Augmented Growth and Transformation Economy (trajectory 2), and Take-Off (trajectory 3). The six uncertainties are capability, distribution and model access, security, adoption, labour displacement, and global cooperation (Executive summary, “Methodology” and “Scenarios overview”; ch. 2).
- **Geography:** Global developments, with implications for the UK (ch. 1, “Project scope”).
- **Authors' framing:** Exploratory policy-planning scenario. “They are not predictions, and the future may involve elements from all scenarios” (Foreword).
- **Authors' view of likelihood:** Not stated. The 2040 outlook is more uncertain than the 2030 narrative (ch. 3, “Guide to the scenario narratives”).

### Timeline

- By 2030 — further scaling, efficiency and scaffolding improvements make AI effective at most remote work. Employment adapts, public services improve and energy and data-centre capacity lag demand (ch. 3, “Augmented Growth”, Capability, Adoption and Labour displacement).
- By 2040 — economy could expand by up to one-third over the 2030s. Scientific innovations take over as a growth driver once early integration gains run out; the labour-augmenting outcome may not persist (ch. 3, “Augmented Growth”, Outlook to 2040).

### Policy stance

- **Stance:** Mixed. The authors exclude new domestic policy responses to scenario outcomes but explicitly assume shared safety standards among allies, wider partial compliance and the continued operation of legal guardrails (ch. 3, “Scenario limitations” and “Augmented Growth”, Labour displacement and Global cooperation).
- **Policies assumed in the scenario:** Allies including European Union and Five Eyes members share safety standards, with partial global compliance and red lines on human involvement in military applications. The US government directs leading companies through policy levers and they remain privately owned. Legal and institutional considerations limit labour substitution (ch. 3, “Augmented Growth”, Distribution and model access, Labour displacement and Global cooperation).
- **Policies the source recommends:** The report invites policymakers to monitor signs of each future, stress-test plans across the set, assess impacts from other perspectives and identify outcomes the UK could influence. It proposes backcasting, policy stress testing and considering shocks as methods (ch. 4, “How to use the AI 2030 scenarios” and “Three methodologies for applying scenarios to policy making”).

## Key dynamics explored

- Scaling, efficiency and agent orchestration allow reliable autonomous work on many long tasks after humans set direction (ch. 3, “Augmented Growth”, Capability).
- Financial incentives spread adoption, but power-grid and data-centre expansion lag demand. Compute scarcity and social, practical and legal constraints keep some tasks human-led (ch. 3, “Augmented Growth”, Adoption and Labour displacement).
- Many firms raise output with AI and build integration and oversight roles. Productivity and real-wage gains among exposed workers raise demand in less-exposed industries (ch. 3, “Augmented Growth”, Labour displacement).
- Serious incidents mobilise alliances and investment in technical safety. Shared standards and defensive capabilities limit the worst harms, while verification remains difficult (ch. 3, “Augmented Growth”, Security and Global cooperation).

*Applies across the set.* The report treats workforce displacement and augmentation as concurrent possibilities across the set. Routine execution changes even in slower futures. Concentration at the frontier and commoditisation behind it can coexist. Uneven adoption and reliance on US- and China-centred ecosystems affect how productivity gains are distributed (ch. 4, “Cross-cutting implications across the AI 2030 scenarios”).

## State of technology

AI matches average humans at most cognitive tasks and exceeds experts in verifiable work, writing all code and completing tasks that could take an experienced worker at least a month. Agents require high-level direction but plan, coordinate and adapt from experience, communicate with stakeholders and sustain working relationships. They contribute to major discoveries and automate much of AI R&D, with diminishing returns under data and compute constraints. AI-guided robots handle variable real-world tasks but remain largely in industry. Multiple US companies lead and access to the highest-capability systems is compute-constrained. Open weights partly close the gap (ch. 3, “Augmented Growth”, Capability and Distribution and model access).

## State of adoption

Businesses, public services and governments deploy AI extensively under economic pressure. The technology removes execution bottlenecks and extends access to skills including coding, design, legal drafting and financial modelling. AI-native firms disrupt slower and less-resourced organisations. Grid and compute shortages impose trade-offs. Public trust is reasonably high given service improvements, though below trust in humans and tempered by energy, displacement and inequality concerns (ch. 3, “Augmented Growth”, Adoption).

## How the economy works

### GDP growth and productivity

Adoption restores early-2000s growth rates. The economy could expand by as much as a third over the 2030s: productivity and wider occupational uptake first, then innovation once one-off integration gains fade (ch. 3, “Augmented Growth”, Adoption and Outlook to 2040).

### Labour market dynamics

All cognitive roles are exposed, though augmentation dominates. Low-variety exposed work is displaced, and new jobs emerge across the AI sector, oversight and integration. Unemployment remains stable as most firms grow output. Workers direct agents, review their work, set strategy and manage stakeholders. Some skilled jobs become richer; some lower-skilled work becomes more demanding and less rewarding. Exposed workers’ productivity raises real wages and spills into jobs in hospitality and construction. Deskilling makes some workers dependent on AI and less capable when it fails. In the 2030s weaker human-involvement constraints could cause structural unemployment or reallocate work to dexterous, in-person or human-preference sectors such as social care, entertainment and education (ch. 3, “Augmented Growth”, Labour displacement and Outlook to 2040).

### Winners and losers

Faster-adopting wealthy and technologically advanced nations pull ahead in output, living standards and geopolitical power. Dynamic AI-native firms displace slower or poorly resourced ones. Workers in exposed occupations generally gain in productivity and wages; low-variety roles are vulnerable and some lower-skilled work becomes less rewarding (ch. 3, “Augmented Growth”, Adoption and Labour displacement).

### Household incomes and living standards

Real-wage growth reaches rates last seen in the 2000s, with higher consumer spending by exposed workers. Many citizens gain access to better healthcare; living standards rise more in wealthy, fast-adopting countries (ch. 3, “Augmented Growth”, Adoption and Labour displacement).

### Government finances

Not addressed.

### International economic patterns

Effective integration into productive businesses with skilled workforces shapes countries’ economic success. The UK’s IT, life sciences and advanced manufacturing strengths position it near the frontier if investment in skills, infrastructure and R&D continues (ch. 3, “Augmented Growth”, Outlook to 2040).

### Market pricing and scarcity

Grid and data-centre capacity constrain compute and limit access to the most powerful systems. High-quality data limits further AI R&D progress (ch. 3, “Augmented Growth”, Capability, Distribution and model access and Adoption).

## Impact on the social contract

### Effort is rewarded

Many workers become more productive and real wages rise; enrichment of some skilled jobs coexists with displacement of low-variety roles and less rewarding work for some lower-skilled workers (ch. 3, “Augmented Growth”, Labour displacement).

### Affordable essentials

Transformed public services improve healthcare access and outcomes using AI-supported care, treatments and diagnostics. The source gives no change in household prices for essential goods (ch. 3, “Augmented Growth”, Adoption).

### Reliable safety net

Not addressed.

### Time for what matters

Not addressed.

### A say in your future

### A fair share of progress

AI provides access to expert technical skills and advice across the population and improves public services, but faster-adopting nations and firms benefit disproportionately (ch. 3, “Augmented Growth”, Adoption).

## Other dynamics

Improved interpretability and safeguards reduce unintended behaviour and mitigate large threats, but global enforcement remains difficult. Long-run dangerous proliferation remains possible. Major advances in biology, materials and energy have social spillovers. AI-related deskilling is a particular concern in high-stakes industries (ch. 3, “Augmented Growth”, Capability, Security, Labour displacement and Outlook to 2040).

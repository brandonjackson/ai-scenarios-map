# Augmented Growth — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Policy off by design.** The good outcome comes from technology and firms' choices, not UK policy. What keeps humans in the loop is "societal, practical, and legal considerations", which implies existing law and norms do some of the work.

## Big assumptions
1. **Strong capability without full autonomy.** AI can do most remote work, but people stay in the loop for most tasks.
2. **Firms choose output over headcount.** Most use AI to grow rather than cut staff.
3. **Safety is solved early.** Systems "generally act as intended" and interpretability lets developers verify reasoning.
4. **Compute bottlenecks ration AI**, which slows substitution and forces prioritisation.
5. **Allies cooperate** on shared safety standards.

## What must be true for this to occur
- Demand for goods and services grows fast enough that firms want more output, not fewer workers.
- Legal and professional rules on human sign-off hold across sectors.
- Gains reach wages: productivity growth shows up in pay, as it last did in the 2000s.
- AI-driven healthcare and public services are actually deployed at scale by 2030.

## Tensions and weak points
- It assumes the most favourable outcome on almost every uncertainty at once: capability, security, adoption, displacement and cooperation. That makes it the least robust of the five.
- Moderate concentration (several US firms lead) sits alongside broad gains. The scenario does not explain why market power does not capture more of them.
- GO-Science itself flags that the augmenting phase may be temporary, which makes this a 2030 snapshot of a path that could turn into Transformation Economy.

Draft scores are 4/4/3/4/3. The safety net is 3 rather than 4 because stable unemployment means it is not tested, not that it improves. Time is 3 because cognitive atrophy and harder low-skilled work offset more creative work for some.

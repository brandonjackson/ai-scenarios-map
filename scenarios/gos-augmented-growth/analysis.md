# Augmented Growth — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Off, with international safety cooperation in the background.** The extract labels it mixed because allies share safety standards and the US steers leading firms through policy levers. But new domestic policy is excluded, and the good labour outcome rests on firms' choices and existing legal limits on substitution, not on enacted economic policy. It is kept off, consistent with the rest of the set (Take-Off also has state-led arrangements and stays off).

## Big assumptions
1. **Strong capability without full autonomy.** AI can do most remote work, yet people keep setting direction.
2. **Firms grow output instead of cutting staff.** Demand rises with productivity, and wage gains spill into other sectors.
3. **Bottlenecks ration AI.** Grid, data-centre and data limits keep some tasks human-led.
4. **Safety and cooperation hold.** Serious incidents mobilise alliances, and shared standards limit the worst harms.

## What must be true for this to occur
- Demand grows fast enough that firms want more output, not fewer workers.
- Legal and institutional rules on human involvement hold across sectors.
- Productivity shows up in pay.
- AI-supported healthcare and public services are deployed at scale by 2030.

## Tensions and weak points
- It takes the favourable outcome on most uncertainties at once, which makes it the least robust of the five.
- Several US firms lead and fast-adopting nations and firms pull ahead, yet workers in exposed jobs gain wages. Within a country it reads as mildly diffuse, so it moves from x 0.15 (tagged concentrated) to x 0.3, beside `korinek-aug` and `wef-copilot`.
- Government finances are not addressed; the strong fiscal placement (y 0.5) is inferred from the growth boom.
- The extract labels the stance mixed while the catalogue says policy is excluded; see above.
- The authors flag that augmentation may be temporary, so this could be an early stage of Transformation Economy.

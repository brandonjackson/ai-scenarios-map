# AI 2027: Slowdown ending — extract

## Summary

Faced with evidence of misalignment at the fictional leading lab, a joint US government–company committee pauses the leading model and develops a safer line. The US and China agree to deploy a common successor under a treaty, while superhuman advisers and automated factories sharply expand output. Employment largely disappears and generous basic incomes and foreign aid eliminate poverty in the narrative, but owners of AI investments accumulate much more wealth and the committee still controls the most powerful systems. The branch ends with political authority contested between a more democratic settlement and permanent concentration of power. (October 2027; Slowdown, November 2027–2030)

## Key details

- **Source:** *AI 2027*, Daniel Kokotajlo, Scott Alexander, Thomas Larsen, Eli Lifland and Romeo Dean, AI Futures Project, 3 April 2025. `sources/ai-2027/`
- **Other sources for this scenario:** None. The publisher also provides the [branch page](https://ai-2027.com/slowdown).
- **Reading notes:** The official PDF and branch page cover the shared story and Slowdown ending, including both political possibilities in the conclusion.
- **Scenario set:** The Race and Slowdown endings share the same 2025–October 2027 path. They diverge when the joint oversight committee chooses whether to keep scaling the leading system or create a safer line. (“Choose Your Ending”; October 2027; Race and Slowdown, November 2027)
- **Geography:** Global, centred on the US and China. In the later story humans and AI begin to settle elsewhere in the solar system. (August 2027; Slowdown, 2029–30)
- **Authors' framing:** A more hopeful alternative forecast from shared premises, designed to encourage discussion. The authors identify optimistic technical alignment assumptions. (Introduction; conclusion)
- **Authors' view of likelihood:** A November 2025 clarification calls 2027 the team's modal year while writing. The authors distinguish the relatively grounded pre-2027 trends from greater uncertainty later and describe both democratic and oligarchic outcomes as plausible after Slowdown. (Introduction; “Key Metrics 2026”; Slowdown, 2030)

### Timeline

- Mid- to late 2025 — unreliable general agents coexist with stronger specialist coding and research agents. The fictional leading US lab OpenBrain builds large compute clusters. (Mid–late 2025)
- 2026 — specialist models speed coding and research, China mobilizes to close the gap, cheaper assistants are released, and junior software hiring weakens. (Early, mid- and late 2026)
- January–July 2027 — agents learn continuously and growing research automation improves models. Cheaper models make a larger share of remote work automatable. (January–July 2027)
- August–October 2027 — competition intensifies and concerns about Agent-4 reach the government, which establishes a joint oversight committee. (August–October 2027)
- November 2027–April 2028 — the committee pauses scaling of Agent-4, consolidates frontier-development oversight in the US, and develops the Safer line as negotiations with China continue. (Slowdown, November 2027–April 2028)
- May–November 2028 — superhuman AI reaches the public, both parties promise basic income to people displaced by AI, and a US–China treaty establishes transition to a shared model. Output and revenue rise rapidly and the Vice President wins the election. (Slowdown, May–November 2028)
- 2029–30 — automated manufacturing and new technologies become widespread. Poverty ends in the story while inequality and the concentration of effective political power remain. (Slowdown, 2029–30)

### Policy stance

- **Stance:** On. Government intervention changes frontier development and redistributes a share of the gains as the economy automates. (Slowdown, November 2027–2029)
- **Policies assumed in the scenario:** The White House initially uses retraining and unemployment insurance. The committee slows scaling, centralizes some frontier-development controls and undertakes safer research. Special economic zones enable fast industrial deployment; the US and China agree on jointly monitored replacement of their models. The government ultimately funds basic income, makeshift employment and foreign aid. (August 2027; Slowdown, November 2027–2029)
- **Policies the source recommends:** The authors present the ending as an alternative scenario for debate and do not offer its particular policy package as an endorsement. (Introduction; conclusion)

## Key dynamics explored

- **Applies across the set:** Improvements in agents accelerate AI R&D and therefore the production of the next generation. Internally available models stay ahead of public ones. (Early 2026; January–September 2027)
- **Applies across the set:** Lower costs and better reliability broaden adoption while complementary oversight work grows initially and entry-level programming contracts. (Late 2026; July 2027)
- Concern about misalignment leads to a slower path built around systems the committee can more reliably monitor. This supports a US–China agreement without requiring an outright military victory. (Slowdown, November 2027–July 2028)
- AIs speed up design and manage production and public administration. Acceleration in output and tax revenue makes generous transfers feasible as people lose their jobs. (Slowdown, April–October 2028)
- The new income floor coexists with concentrated ownership of assets and political authority. A human committee with control of frontier systems can choose whether to diffuse or keep its power. (Slowdown, September 2028, 2029–30)

## State of technology

**Applies across the set:** General agents initially struggle with reliability, while coding and research models advance faster. Agent-1-mini is about 10× cheaper than Agent-1. Agent-3-mini is about 10× cheaper than its frontier counterpart and better than a typical OpenBrain employee. In Slowdown, the committee develops more transparent, controllable agents through the Safer line, then releases superhuman assistants while other systems automate industry. The later story includes fusion, quantum computing, improved medicine, widespread robots and continued improvements in the systems advising people. (Mid-2025; late 2026; July 2027; Slowdown, January–May 2028, 2029–30)

## State of adoption

**Applies across the set:** Coding and research move before many general household uses. Slow defence procurement initially restricts adoption, while the July 2027 release rapidly expands white-collar applications and consumer entertainment. In Slowdown, lead labs and governments first use advanced systems, then public release makes useful assistants widespread. Factories in special economic zones automate physical tasks and supplier operations. Medical regulators and public agencies use the systems to administer changes faster. (Mid-2025; late 2026; July 2027; Slowdown, April–October 2028, 2029)

## How the economy works

### GDP growth and productivity

Automated production facilities, new products and rapid innovation create very high growth by October 2028, while advisers improve public administration. The authors do not provide a growth rate for Slowdown. (Slowdown, April–October 2028)

### Labour market dynamics

**Applies across the set:** Young software workers face fewer opportunities and AI supervisors or integration consultants initially gain. New programming hires are nearly halted by July 2027. In Slowdown broad displacement arrives by 2028, and almost all tasks are expected to shift to AI and robots within a few years. Some displaced people work in government-created roles and others live on basic income. (Late 2026; July 2027; Slowdown, May and October 2028, 2029)

### Winners and losers

**Applies across the set:** Chip firms, the leading lab, successful commercial adopters and people able to manage AI agents benefit early, while junior programmers and trailing companies face pressure. In Slowdown many AI investors become billionaires and some billionaires become trillionaires. Their wealth remains secondary to control over the systems. UBI raises the floor even as inequality rises. (Late 2026; July 2027; Slowdown, 2029)

### Household incomes and living standards

Rapid production, new medical treatments, basic income and aid lead to a material floor and widespread elimination of poverty, including in poorer countries. Most people receive enough to live well, but wealth rises far more sharply among AI asset holders, giving them greater access to scarce goods. (Slowdown, October 2028, 2029)

### Government finances

Receipts rise alongside exceptionally fast GDP growth in 2028. In the later mostly automated economy the source expects nearly all government revenue to come from taxing or perhaps nationalizing AI firms. It compares this structure with resource-dependent states and gives no budget or debt projections. (Slowdown, October 2028, 2029)

### International economic patterns

**Applies across the set:** US and Chinese developers compete for frontier models, chips and influence. In Slowdown a joint model deployment and treaty diminish the danger of war, and foreign aid supports basic income and poverty reduction farther afield. After democratic transitions across countries, a strongly federal global government emerges under UN branding with dominant US influence. (Mid-2026; August 2027; Slowdown, July–August 2028, 2029–30)

### Market pricing and scarcity

**Applies across the set:** Cheaper agents expand their range of economically feasible uses. In Slowdown AI makes most goods broadly available while positional goods remain fixed in supply. Manhattan penthouses become even harder for most people to afford as top incomes and fortunes rise. (Late 2026; July 2027; Slowdown, 2029)

### Financial markets and stability

**Applies across the set:** The stock market rises 30% in 2026, led by the frontier lab, chip supplier and successful integrators. AI applications attract large investments in 2027. The 2029 Slowdown market surges and rewards holders of the right AI investments disproportionately. (Late 2026; July 2027; Slowdown, 2029)

## Impact on the social contract

### Effort is rewarded

**Applies across the set:** People who can supervise AI initially increase their earning power, and entry-level programmers struggle. Even most jobs that remain become unnecessary once cheaper AI and robots can do the work. (Late 2026; July 2027; Slowdown, 2029)

### Affordable essentials

New medical cures, cleaner and safer cities, energy innovations and income support raise material living standards. Some geographically scarce housing becomes less affordable. (Slowdown, October 2028, 2029)

### Reliable safety net

**Applies across the set:** The White House first backs job training and unemployment insurance. In Slowdown both parties later promise a basic income for laid-off workers. A generous income floor and international aid become central ways to share output. (August 2027; Slowdown, May–October 2028, 2029)

### Time for what matters

**Applies across the set:** People use improved entertainment and form friendships with AIs. In Slowdown, once almost all work is automated, many embrace high-quality entertainment and consumption, while others turn to religion or anti-consumerist ways of life. The government generally leaves people to make those choices. (July 2027; Slowdown, 2029)

### A say in your future

The committee has effective control over the most powerful systems and allows a mostly fair 2028 US election. The narrative considers a path where it gradually shares control with Congress and the public, and another where it entrenches oligarchy. The state acquires powerful surveillance while democratic transitions occur abroad. (Slowdown, September–November 2028, 2029–30)

### A fair share of progress

Basic income, aid, medicine and consumer services spread much of the improved material welfare. Investors and the small group with authority over the AIs retain much larger shares of wealth or effective power. (Slowdown, October 2028, 2029–30)

## Other dynamics

The narrative explores how greater alignment and international cooperation could avoid extinction without settling who governs. It also describes highly capable personal advisers, surveillance, debate over consumerism and human purpose, and the beginning of settlement beyond Earth. (Slowdown, September 2028, 2029–30)

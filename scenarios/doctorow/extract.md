# The Reverse Centaur’s Guide to Criticizing AI — extract

## Summary

Dominant technology companies promote AI as a way to replace expensive labour and sustain growth-stock valuations (“Start with monopolies”, “How they’re selling AI”). Employers dismiss workers and retain people to feed, monitor or take responsibility for unreliable systems, a role Doctorow calls the reverse centaur (“A reverse centaur”, radiology and software examples). After the bubble bursts, many suppliers and data centres fail, while inexpensive hardware and some smaller, useful tools survive (closing section).

## Key details

- **Source:** The Reverse Centaur’s Guide to Criticizing AI, Cory Doctorow, Pluralistic, 2025-12-05. `sources/reverse-centaur-2025/`
- **Other sources for this scenario:** None
- **Reading notes:** Full text.
- **Scenario set:** Standalone
- **Geography:** US examples from the technology sector, health care and organised labour, in a discussion of the broader AI boom.
- **Authors’ framing:** A public lecture on what fuels the AI bubble, how it changes work and how workers and users can respond (opening and closing).
- **Authors’ view of likelihood:** Doctorow says the AI bubble will burst. He gives no probability or date (closing section).

### Timeline

December 2025 — the Pluralistic post publishes the University of Washington lecture. A bubble collapse follows at an unspecified date, and repair of affected systems takes a generation or more in Doctorow’s description (opening and closing).

### Policy stance

- **Stance:** Mixed. The main trajectory is market-driven, with the existing human-authorship rule and collective bargaining presented as checks on workplace displacement (creative work and bargaining discussion).
- **Policies assumed in the scenario:** Employers can deploy the tools under existing labour law. Doctorow treats the US Copyright Office’s human-authorship rule as a reason businesses still need creative labour for copyrightable work (creative work and bargaining discussion).
- **Policies the source recommends:** Preserve the requirement for human authorship, restore sectoral bargaining, and organise users and workers together against harmful deployments. The author objects to extending copyright to training as a substitute for labour power (copyright and bargaining discussion).

## Key dynamics explored

- Firms nearing market saturation risk losing growth-stock valuations and use successive investment narratives to maintain investor expectations (“Start with monopolies”).
- AI suppliers pitch wage savings to clients and use announced layoffs as evidence that other occupations can be automated (“How they’re selling AI”, software example).
- Workers left to review low-error-rate AI output lack enough practice to spot unusual failures, yet can be required to sign off and bear the blame (radiology and software examples).
- Concentrated buyers of creative work can make creators surrender newly created rights in standard contracts, which redirects the value of those rights to corporations (copyright discussion).

## State of technology

The author distinguishes assistance such as second opinions, interface styling, transcription, summarisation, descriptions of images and editing from replacing a whole worker. He describes statistically plausible errors and argues that the costly largest models depend on continuing investment, while open models can run on commodity hardware (radiology and software examples, closing).

## State of adoption

Examples cover monitoring of delivery drivers, possible deployment in radiology, coding, illustration and studios. Buyers choose uses that cut payroll and permit organisations to claim a human is in control. Some workers can adopt narrow tasks on terms they control (“A reverse centaur”, radiology, software and creative-work examples).

## How the economy works

### GDP growth and productivity

Doctorow contrasts genuine productivity or quality gains from specific tools with a market pitch aimed at replacing expensive workers. He does not quantify an economy-wide productivity effect (radiology and software examples, closing).

### Labour market dynamics

An AI system can lead to job loss even when it cannot perform the whole job, because management acts on the sales pitch. In radiology and software, fewer workers are asked to oversee greater volumes of output, and experienced workers who could catch subtle errors are expensive enough to target for dismissal. Image-generation hype also threatens precarious illustrators (radiology, software and creative-work examples).

### Winners and losers

Technology companies, executives and concentrated buyers are positioned to capture short-run gains. Workers lose jobs or autonomy, and affected customers can encounter poorer services. After collapse, some applied-statistics workers and users of discounted GPUs or open models benefit (creative-work examples, closing).

### Household incomes and living standards

Workers lose pay or jobs. Doctorow also warns that households’ retirement savings are drawn into a bubble, with losses when it ends (investment pitch, closing).

### Market pricing and scarcity

Customers can pay more for worse services in the reverse-centaur model. In the bust, surplus GPUs become cheap enough for effects artists and climate researchers; many high-operating-cost cloud offerings disappear (radiology example, closing).

### Financial markets and stability

Doctorow links prevailing AI investment to the growth premium, concentrated stock-market exposure, circular commitments and expectations that firms can share payroll savings with suppliers. A collapse closes many providers and facilities (monopoly discussion, closing).

### A say in your future

Doctorow urges alliances between workers and the people who rely on their services. Sectoral bargaining is meant to give workers leverage over how AI is introduced (radiology example, creative work and bargaining discussion).

### A fair share of progress

The author favours uses in which a worker controls the tool and benefits from it. Current corporate incentives instead favour cutting payroll and capturing savings (reverse-centaur discussion, closing).

## Other dynamics

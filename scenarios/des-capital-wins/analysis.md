# Capital Wins Amid Major Permanent Job Displacement — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Off.** The budget results use current law and CBO's projection method, including today's light taxation of capital (preferential rates, deferral, step-up in basis, a 21% corporate rate, some evasion). Fostering employment, a UBI, higher capital taxes and public ownership are options set out with their trade-offs, not part of the scenario.

## Big assumptions
1. **Capability.** AI adds a full point to annual TFP growth for 30 years and permanently substitutes for a large share of labour, though human care work stays in demand.
2. **Adoption.** Displacement phases in gradually over three decades at a "moderate pace" as productivity rises.
3. **Markets.** Extra capital investment replaces lost labour, so GDP rises by the full TFP increment. All extra income goes to top-quintile capital owners, who save heavily; national saving rises and interest rates fall.
4. **Politics.** Current law holds for 30 years despite permanent job loss and a near reversal of the capital and labour shares.

## What must be true for this to occur
- AI doubles the productivity boost of the other scenarios and sustains it for three decades.
- Capital substitutes for labour so thoroughly that labour demand stays permanently weak, rather than new tasks absorbing workers.
- The gains flow to capital owners rather than to workers using AI, and the tax system keeps taxing them lightly.
- No large policy response is enacted, even as political power shifts toward capital owners.

## Tensions and weak points
- The stronger public finances are an artefact of current law plus growth: debt falls 49 points, the same as Scenario #2, only because capital is taxed at about half the rate of top-quintile income. Interest-rate effects, the cost of the displaced and the loss of employer health insurance are left out, so the fiscal position is uncertain in both directions.
- The authors flag but do not pursue the risk of persistent demand shortfalls and long spells at the zero lower bound, which would sit uneasily with GDP 35% higher.
- Remaining workers' living standards are "unchanged apart from more government services", but government services grow less than GDP, so the welfare state shrinks relative to an economy dominated by capital income.
- The 6% fall in workers over 30 years is moderate next to `tcb-displacement` or `imf-runaway`; what makes the scenario extreme is the distribution, not the speed. On the labour map it sits beside `gos-transformation-economy`, `toloui-titans` and `intelligence-curse`. On the fiscal map it pairs fewer good jobs with stronger finances, near `ai-2027`, unlike most strong-replacement scenarios.

## Scoring notes
Scores are 2/2/2/2/–. Work is 2 because displaced workers lose income, purpose and social contact and labour demand stays permanently weak, while those who keep their jobs are unchanged, so the harm is concentrated rather than universal. Essentials is 2: falling employment cuts employer-sponsored health insurance and many people will be unable to afford eldercare. Safety net is 2: current programmes, built for short spells, meet permanent displacement, and people out of work for years would get smaller Social Security benefits without formula changes. Government is 2: capital owners would presumably gain more political power and vested interests could foreclose policy options. Time is not addressed: earlier retirement and shorter workweeks appear only as options, not as part of the scenario.

# Capital Wins Amid Major Permanent Job Displacement — summary

*Scenario #4 of four in "How Might Fiscal Policy Respond to the Rise of Artificial Intelligence?" by Karen Dynan, Douglas Elmendorf and Louise Sheiner (Brookings Institution, August 2026), written for the Brookings–University of Utah conference on America's fiscal future. The four scenarios are layered thought experiments for US federal fiscal policy over CBO's 30-year horizon, not predictions; the authors assign no probabilities. Read from the paper's full text; see `extract.md`.*

**The scenario.** AI has "more profound effects". Productivity growth rises by a full point a year, twice the other scenarios, and capital replaces the lost labour, so GDP is about 35% higher by 2056. Major permanent displacement phases in over three decades: unemployment ends 2 points higher and participation 4 points lower, 6% fewer workers. All the extra income goes to capital owners in the top quintile, and the capital and labour shares "nearly reverse". Because capital is lightly taxed (an effective 15% here), debt ends 49 points of GDP below the baseline, no better than Scenario #2 despite double the growth. The authors widen the menu of responses to job creation, a universal basic income, higher capital taxes and public ownership, none of which is assumed.

## Hard work pays
Broken for the displaced. People who keep their jobs have unchanged living standards; for those who lose them, "living standards presumably fall", and the harm is "not just a loss of income but also a loss of purpose and social contact". Labour demand is permanently weak, and junior positions may be under-provided because AI supplants junior workers' output.

## Affordable essentials
Slipping for the displaced. The fall in employment cuts employer-sponsored health insurance, and "many people will probably want eldercare and be unable to afford it".

## Reliable safety net
Not built for permanent loss. The existing programmes apply, but people out of work for years earn fewer Social Security credits and would get smaller benefits unless the formula changes. A UBI of $15,000 a person would cost over $5 trillion a year. In a survey the authors cite, 39% of Americans would choose a guaranteed public job programme if AI did almost all paid work, 31% a UBI.

## Responsive government
At risk of capture. Capital owners with a much larger share of income "would presumably gain even more political power", and waiting "might foreclose some policy options altogether as vested interests become stronger". Capital's global mobility weakens taxes on it without international coordination.

## Time for what matters
Not addressed in the scenario itself. Earlier retirement and shorter working weeks appear only as options, and the authors ask whether early retirees would enjoy the time more than the jobless would.

## Bottom line
The most disruptive case in the set, and the one the authors call "fundamentally new": an economy far richer than today in which work stops being the route to a share of it, and political power follows the money.

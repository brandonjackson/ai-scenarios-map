# The Simple Macroeconomics of AI — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Off.** The model evaluates automation, complementarity and investment without any new government policy. The author recommends prioritising applications that create useful new tasks and raise workers' capabilities, and judging welfare separately from measured output, but the estimates do not depend on it.

## Big assumptions
1. **Capability:** today's generative AI and computer vision, with cost and reliability limited by data, clarity of outcomes and context. No step change in capability over the decade.
2. **Adoption:** only the share of exposed tasks that becomes economical within ten years is adopted; small firms are slower, and organisational costs delay gains.
3. **Markets:** aggregate gains equal the cost saving per task times the tasks' economic weight, amplified by capital accumulation. Complementarity can raise a group's productivity yet widen wage gaps through task reallocation.
4. **Politics:** not modelled.

## What must be true for this to occur
- Capability improves only incrementally, so exposure and cost savings stay near the 2024 estimates.
- Hard, context-dependent tasks stay hard to automate profitably.
- No large wave of new labour-using tasks arrives to reinstate labour.

## Tensions and weak points
- The estimates rest on 2023–24 evidence and are framed as upper bounds conditional on the model. If capability has grown faster since, as `shumer` and `citrini` argue, they understate the effect; the framework also cannot show sudden cross-sector change.
- The concentration is between capital and labour, not among firms: the paper does not analyse market concentration, beyond noting that tools concentrated in few suppliers can slow adoption by small firms.
- The extract is silent on government finances. The weak negative `y_fiscal` (−0.1) is inferred from the shift of income from wages to capital.
- Its finding that complementarity need not narrow inequality directly challenges `turing-transform` and `autor`. Nearest on the map are `chiang`, `tcb-concentrated` and `wef-stalled`.

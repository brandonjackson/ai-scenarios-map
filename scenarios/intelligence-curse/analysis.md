# The Intelligence Curse — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Off: the default path is the curse.** The scenario assumes no transformative redistribution of capital within or between countries, and firms and states under competitive pressure. The authors' cure is a separate intervention path: technologies that protect safety and liberty, diffusing AI and human capability, stronger democratic control, upskilling around real bottlenecks, barring autonomous AI from holding assets or senior posts, and public ownership or other ways to fund basic needs. These are recommended, not assumed, so there are no Policy Atlas links. A "curse broken" policy-on row would be a useful contrast.

## Big assumptions
1. **Capability reaches AGI and robotics.** Progress comes first on short, checkable digital tasks, then on harder tasks and the physical world.
2. **Adoption follows competition.** Firms delegate more roles and decisions; states adopt in administration.
3. **Markets reward scarce inputs.** Labour becomes plentiful relative to land, energy, compute, factories and robots; frontier labs earn rents across the economy.
4. **Politics follows revenue.** Rentier-state dynamics generalise: states funded without their citizens' labour stop investing in them.

## What must be true for this to occur
- Labour-replacing AGI, then general-purpose robotics, arrive.
- Control of capital and AI stays concentrated rather than commoditised.
- State revenue shifts from labour to AI-dependent firms.
- Diffusion and democratic reform fail to keep pace.

## Tensions and weak points
- **The analogy:** oil is fixed and extracted by a few; AI may commoditise. The authors' own remedy (diffusion) relies on that.
- **Variants:** the extract spans benign shared abundance to below-subsistence outcomes; the map and scores follow the default and worse variants.
- Norway (about 30% of revenue from resources, as cited) shows resource wealth need not ruin a state; the curse is not automatic.
- Falling household demand does not stop production, which is a sharper claim than most displacement scenarios make.
- **Map placement:** `y_labor` moves from 0.45 to 0.6 to reflect broad substitution reaching physical work, putting it beside `cfg-central` and `toloui-titans`, more replacing than `gradual-disemp`. Gains stay strongly concentrated (−0.6) and job quality and public finances near the floor (−0.75, −0.7). Public finances are inferred: revenue shifts from labour to AI-dependent firms and fiscal pressure blocks UBI, though the extract never says budgets shrink and a rentier state could stay well funded.
- Close to `gradual-disemp` (rentier states) and `wf-lost-generation` (pyramid replacement is a generalised version).

## Scoring notes
Draft scores 1/2/1/1/–. Work is 1: talent can no longer overcome inherited wealth and wages fall for everyone. Essentials is 2: the benign variant keeps living standards, the worse one leaves some below subsistence. Safety net is 1: fiscal and competitive pressure block broad support. Government is 1: citizens lose the leverage that made states invest in them. Time is not addressed: the authors note lost ambition and status when achievements are cheaply reproducible, but say nothing about time for family, community or meaning.

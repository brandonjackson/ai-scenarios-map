# The Intelligence Curse — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Policy off is the curse. The cure is "tech tree on" plus democratic reform.** The authors are wary of solutions built on centralisation (licensing regimes, a single global project) because concentrated control is the curse's mechanism. Their prescription: build hardening and safety technologies (to remove the security case for centralisation), push augmenting and user-aligned AI into ordinary people's hands, and strengthen democratic institutions, rather than relying mainly on redistribution.

## Big assumptions
1. **AGI arrives soon and replaces labour broadly.** It cites METR's seven-month doubling, lab CEOs' 2026–27 forecasts and Korinek's wage-collapse probabilities. Pyramid replacement assumes AI beats juniors first, then everyone.
2. **Rentier-state dynamics generalise.** The resource curse (states rich from oil neglect their citizens) applies to AI rents, at higher stakes.
3. **Incentives shape institutions in the long run.** Following Ian Morris, the shape of societies follows their dominant technology. Democracy and liberal capitalism thrived because they were competitive in the industrial era, and may not be under AGI.
4. **Control concentrates.** Capital intensity and scale give AI rents to a few labs, states and suppliers.
5. **Redistribution won't be enough.** UBI is fiscally and politically constrained, and depends on the goodwill of those who no longer need the recipients.

## What must be true for this to occur
- Labour-replacing AGI and then general-purpose robotics arrive.
- AI capability and profits are concentrated rather than commoditised.
- States come to depend more on AI rents than on taxing citizens' labour.
- Diffusion (open, user-aligned AI) and democratic hardening fail to keep up.

## Tensions and weak points
- **The analogy:** oil is a fixed resource extracted by a few. AI may commoditise (Toloui's fast-follower world, open models). If intelligence becomes cheap and widely held, the rentier logic weakens. The authors' own remedy (diffusion) relies on that.
- Rentier states differ: Norway is the counter-example of resource wealth with strong institutions and a sovereign wealth fund. The curse isn't automatic, as the authors acknowledge when they say the outcome is "not yet fulfilled".
- **Closely related scenarios:** the rentier argument appears in *Gradual Disempowerment* (states funded by AI profits) and Windfall's *Disempowered Abundance*. Pyramid replacement is a generalised Lost Generation.
- The emphasis on keeping humans economically *relevant* (rather than just paid) matches the "hard work pays" promise most closely of any AGI-era text.

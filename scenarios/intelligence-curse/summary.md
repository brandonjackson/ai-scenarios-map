# The Intelligence Curse — summary

*Luke Drago & Rudolf Laine, April 2025 · essay series, intelligence-curse.ai*

**The scenario.** AGI "looks a lot more like coal or oil than the plow, steam engine, or computer": capital-intensive, controlled by a few players (labs, their host states, chip, robot and energy suppliers), and a source of *rents* that don't depend on people. Like resource-rich rentier states, "when powerful actors create and implement general intelligence, they will lose their incentives to invest in people." "Pyramid replacement" starts with entry-level hiring freezes and moves up the corporate ladder. Non-human factors (capital, resources, control over AI) come to dominate, and social mobility stops. The authors reject inevitability and offer a way out: **avert** catastrophes without centralisation, **diffuse** AI to ordinary people, and **democratise** institutions.

## Hard work pays
Broken, from the bottom up. A 2030 CEO faces an AI that is "20% faster and 10% better than any of your junior employees" at $10,000 a year: "Why wouldn't you fire your junior employees?" Competition forces it. White-collar work goes first, then blue-collar work through self-driving vehicles and robotics. AI "will obsolete even outlier human talent", so effort and brilliance can no longer overcome a poor start: "society might become permanently static."

## Affordable essentials
Rich but out of reach. The ability to "buy results in the real world" grows enormously, for those who have capital. People without it have no labour to sell.

## Reliable safety net
Fragile. Expanding the net or adopting UBI faces fiscal limits (US Social Security's trust fund runs out around 2033) and cultural resistance. Above all, the incentive to fund it fades when the state no longer needs productive citizens. "Radical equalising measures are unlikely."

## Responsive government
The defining failure. Powerful actors care about you because you are a return on investment and because you can threaten their power. States build schools, courts and safety nets because productive, free citizens make them rich. Under the curse those incentives flip, pulling states "towards entrenched authoritarianism ... unparalleled concentration of wealth and power."

## Time for what matters
Empty leisure. People are freed from work but without leverage, mobility or purpose.

## Bottom line
The modern social contract (people produce, so the powerful invest in them) breaks at the root. Every promise fails unless technology and institutions are deliberately designed to keep humans economically relevant.

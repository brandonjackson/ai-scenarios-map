# The Intelligence Curse — summary

*Luke Drago and Rudolf Laine, "The Intelligence Curse", essay series at intelligence-curse.ai, April 2025: a political-economy warning about the default path after AGI, with better and worse variants and a proposed positive alternative. Global, with examples from industrial and resource-dependent economies. Read from the full text; see `extract.md`.*

**The scenario.** "The intelligence curse describes the incentives in a post-AGI economy that will drive powerful actors to invest in artificial intelligence instead of humans." AI first does junior and professional work, so a small group of senior staff manage more output and the traditional entry routes vanish. Workers shift into manual jobs until vehicles, specialised robots and general robotics close that option. Once capital can buy cheap, high-quality artificial labour, companies and governments lose the incentive to invest in ordinary people, like states funded by resource rents. Production can grow while wages, mobility and political leverage fall. Outcomes range from shared material abundance with frozen mobility to permanent hierarchy or worse.

## Hard work pays
Broken for all. When artificial labour can be bought throughout the economy, outstanding work and talent can no longer overcome inherited wealth. Abundant machine labour and scarce capital and land push down wages and the labour share. The authors cite estimates of about a one-in-three chance of below-subsistence wages before 2045.

## Affordable essentials
Broken for some. A benign variant sustains high living standards through shared returns. In a worse one incomes fall below subsistence, and the goods people need lose investment as land, energy, compute and robots go to the projects of the powerful.

## Reliable safety net
Broken. Fiscal and competitive pressure can block a broad UBI, and global competition discourages diverting resources from AI investment. The authors propose public ownership stakes with citizen dividends and safeguards on basic needs, but these belong to their alternative, not the default.

## Responsive government
The defining failure. As states depend less on labour and tax payments, citizens lose material leverage. Revenue shifts to taxes on AI-dependent firms (corporate tax is 11.5% of revenue in the average OECD state today), and governments have weaker reasons to maintain education, services and infrastructure for people no longer needed as workers. Immigration loses its economic case.

## Time for what matters
Not addressed directly. The authors note effects on ambition, status and cultural significance when even exceptional achievements are cheap to reproduce.

## Bottom line
The modern bargain (people produce, so the powerful invest in them) breaks at the root. Output may be shared like oil income in a poorly governed rentier state.

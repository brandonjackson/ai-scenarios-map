# The Techno-Optimist Manifesto — extract

## Summary

A market economy uses increasingly powerful AI, human–machine collaboration and abundant energy to raise productivity and reduce prices (“Markets,” “Intelligence,” “Energy,” “Abundance”). Lower prices free purchasing power, new demand elicits new supply, and innovation creates more opportunities for work (“Markets,” “The Techno-Capital Machine”). The manifesto envisages widely shared material abundance and an eventual expansion in the number of people supported on Earth and beyond (“Abundance”).

## Key details

- **Source:** The Techno-Optimist Manifesto, Marc Andreessen, Andreessen Horowitz, 2023-10-16. `sources/andreessen-techno-optimist-manifesto-2023/`
- **Other sources for this scenario:** None
- **Reading notes:** Full text.
- **Scenario set:** Standalone
- **Geography:** Global and ultimately multiplanetary (“Abundance”).
- **Authors' framing:** Normative manifesto advocating deliberate technological acceleration (“Truth,” “The Techno-Capital Machine”).
- **Authors' view of likelihood:** Presented as the author’s stated convictions. No scenario probability is given.

### Timeline

2023-10-16 — publication. An undated intelligence takeoff and an open-ended increase in energy, productivity and population form the future (“Intelligence,” “Energy,” “Abundance”).

### Policy stance

- **Stance:** Mixed. Markets coordinate most production, and the manifesto supports welfare provision for vulnerable people and deliberate action to remove barriers to technological development (“Markets,” “Energy”).
- **Policies assumed in the scenario:** Wide scope for competitive enterprise, deployment of AI and expansion of energy supply. Market production generates resources to finance collective needs (“Markets,” “Energy”).
- **Policies the source recommends:** Build and deploy AI and nuclear energy, pursue fusion, and keep scope for competitive entry, technology and voluntary exchange. The author opposes a universal basic income and supports safety-net provision for vulnerable people (“Markets,” “Intelligence,” “Energy”).

## Key dynamics explored

- Prices communicate dispersed information. High prices attract producers and innovators, whose competition expands supply and lowers prices (“Markets”).
- Higher productivity raises wages. Lower prices raise purchasing power; spending on new and unmet wants creates further economic activity (“Markets,” “The Techno-Capital Machine,” “Abundance”).
- AI augments people as well as machines. Intelligence and energy reinforce one another, and a larger population brings more potential contributors and ideas (“Intelligence,” “Energy,” “Abundance”).

## State of technology

The author expects an intelligence takeoff and treats AI as a general problem-solving tool and a means of augmenting human intelligence. Fission is presented as a route to abundant low-emission energy, with fusion a further prospect (“Intelligence,” “Energy”).

## State of adoption

The author urges rapid deployment, arguing that barriers delay useful capabilities and benefits. Competitive incentives, new applications and lower prices spread use (“Markets,” “Intelligence,” “Abundance”).

## How the economy works

### GDP growth and productivity

The feedback between increasing specialisation, trade, lower prices, new demand and wider supply drives ongoing material growth. The manifesto does not quantify future GDP (“The Techno-Capital Machine”).

### Labour market dynamics

Comparative advantage in a free market and demand arising from indefinitely expandable wants sustain employment. Increasing worker productivity raises wages. The author regards useful work as a source of dignity (“Markets,” “Intelligence”).

### Winners and losers

Consumers broadly benefit from falling prices and entrepreneurs are rewarded for producing useful supply. As historical support, Andreessen cites Nordhaus for the estimate that innovators capture about 2% of the economic value of their inventions (“Markets,” “Abundance”).

### Household incomes and living standards

Falling prices raise real incomes for buyers. Higher productivity raises wages, which in turn supports demand and new production. Widely available everyday products illustrate the author’s conception of shared abundance (“Markets,” “Intelligence,” “Abundance”).

### Government finances

Market-created wealth can support research, social welfare and defence. The text does not give tax or spending estimates (“Markets”).

### International economic patterns

Specialisation and comparative advantage increase trade. The author calls for developing countries to reach rich-country levels of energy use and for supply to expand across both groups (“The Techno-Capital Machine,” “Energy”).

### Market pricing and scarcity

Energy and intelligence become cheap enough to remove constraints on many physical goods. Falling prices are the author’s measure of abundance, with a goal of near-zero prices across as many goods and services as feasible (“Abundance”).

### Financial markets and stability

Not addressed.

## Impact on the social contract

### Effort is rewarded

The author connects productive work, higher productivity and wage growth, and values people’s opportunity to do useful work (“Markets,” “Intelligence”).

### Affordable essentials

Technology reduces the cost of goods and services broadly, including energy, and enables advances in medicine and transportation safety (“Intelligence,” “Energy,” “Abundance”).

### Reliable safety net

The author supports a safety net for vulnerable people financed by the wealth markets generate and rejects a universal basic income (“Markets”).

### Time for what matters

Not addressed.

### A say in your future

The author values decentralised decision-making, freedom of choice and competitive discipline, and objects to centralised economic planning (“Markets,” “Not Utopia, But Close Enough”).

### A fair share of progress

Most economic value generated by inventions is said to flow to society via consumer benefits, competition and lower prices (“Markets,” “Abundance”).

## Other dynamics

The essay advocates technological solutions to environmental problems, expanded energy supply, advances in medicine and space settlement, and presents scientific and technical achievement as an expression of human ambition (“Intelligence,” “Energy,” “Abundance,” “Technological Values”).

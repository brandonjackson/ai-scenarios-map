# Diplomacy: Licensed Utopia — summary

*Centre for Future Generations, "Advanced AI: Possible Futures" (10 July 2025), by Bengüsu Özcan, Daan Juijn, Jakob Graabak and Sam Bogerd. The study crosses the speed of capability progress with how concentrated frontier development is. This entry follows the Diplomacy narrative (fast, centralised and coordinated) and its "Licensed Utopia" ending; the other ending, "Unstable Pause", has cooperation stall and safety fall short. The uncoordinated fast path is [Big AI / Arms Race](../cfg-central/summary.md). Read from the publisher's full scenario text, narrative PDF and companion methodology and governance analysis; see `extract.md`.*

**The scenario.** Internal AI speeds up R&D faster than oversight can follow. A staged release of a model called Nova in 2027 brings enthusiasm, job worries and then a breach, and the public calls for a pause. A 2028 Washington summit funds joint safety research and a Global AI Security Institute in London; the US and China limit public release of new capabilities. An interpretability breakthrough in 2029 helps detect deception, and by 2031 the US, EU, China and dozens of other countries agree a licensing treaty under IAEA oversight, with audits, fees and a 25% AI tax. Licensed firms deliver advances in health, materials and energy, and global GDP growth reaches 7% a year by 2032.

## Hard work pays
Broken for some. Senior programmers move to architecture and juniors, consultants and analysts become managers of agents. But once cognitive automation is widespread, unemployment rises in places with weaker labour protections, and education turns to critical thinking and emotional intelligence "as traditional skills lose relevance".

## Affordable essentials
Improving. AI diagnostics, personalised treatments and new medicines improve access and health outcomes, and many countries gain better treatments. The scenario says little about what households pay, and early secure deployments are very expensive.

## Reliable safety net
Improving. As the labour adjustment speeds up, "governments shift focus from retraining programmes to large-scale wealth redistribution". Fees and the 25% AI tax are shared among treaty nations by population and development needs. Benefit design is not specified.

## Responsive government
Stable, with a catch. Public fear and safety activism push governments to act, and trust improves with coordination. But only a few firms qualify for licences and they gain outsized value and bargaining power; people worry about that power, exclusion from the treaty remains a security risk, and a 2032 incident forces a temporary licence freeze.

## Time for what matters
Improving. Families adapt to more leisure through new community structures and norms, and some people organise to keep human-led services and AI-minimal spaces.

## Bottom line
CFG's most hopeful fast-AI future, and the only one where concentration is turned into broadly shared gains, through a treaty, licensing and an AI tax. Work is its weak promise: the gains reach people as redistribution rather than through jobs.

# Diplomacy: Licensed Utopia — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Strongly policy on.** It needs an international treaty, a licensing and audit regime, an IAEA-style institute and a 25% AI tax. The policy-off counterpart is [Big AI / Arms Race](../cfg-central/analysis.md).

## Big assumptions
1. **A crisis creates the political will.** A scary but survivable incident unites rivals.
2. **Verification becomes possible.** An interpretability breakthrough makes alignment checkable and treaties enforceable.
3. **The US and China cooperate** on shared safety concerns despite competition.
4. **Licensing doesn't turn into capture.** Power concentrated among licensed firms is held in check by international oversight.

## What must be true for this to occur
- Alignment research keeps pace with capability, or a pause holds until it does.
- Hardware-enabled verification works well enough to trust.
- The AI tax and fees are large and well distributed.

## Tensions and weak points
- CFG's own alternative ending ("Unstable Pause") shows how easily this falls apart.
- Licensing regimes concentrate power, the tension Buterin and Narayanan & Kapoor warn about.
- **Map history:** this map used to fold Diplomacy into the Big AI / Arms Race point. It is now its own scenario.

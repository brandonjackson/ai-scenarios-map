# Diplomacy: Licensed Utopia — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**On: treaty, licensing and an AI tax.** The good ending depends on enacted policy: a US–China restriction on public frontier release, a treaty with an annually reviewed capability threshold, standardised alignment, cybersecurity monitoring and on-site audits, IAEA verification, and lump-sum fees and a 25% AI tax redistributed among treaty nations. The policy-off counterpart is [Big AI / Arms Race](../cfg-central/analysis.md).

## Big assumptions
1. **Capability: fast, then controllable.** Agents first speed AI R&D, then science; an interpretability breakthrough and AI-assisted oversight allow "superhuman systems under human control".
2. **Adoption: gated, then broad.** Public releases lag internal capability and early secure access is very costly, but by 2032 higher thresholds let most industries use agents.
3. **Markets: licensed oligopoly with a levy.** A few licensed firms capture outsized value; fees and tax are the channel that spreads it.
4. **Politics: a crisis unites rivals.** The Nova breach is scary enough to bring the US and China to a summit and later a treaty, but survivable.

## What must be true for this to occur
- A serious but contained safety incident happens early enough to drive coordination.
- Interpretability and oversight research advance fast enough to make a treaty verifiable.
- The US and China accept limits on public releases while retaining internal work.
- Licensing doesn't turn into capture, and treaty members agree how to share revenues.
- Countries with weak labour protections get enough of the redistribution to offset rising unemployment.

## Tensions and weak points
- CFG's own other ending, Unstable Pause, shows how easily this falls apart; the extract describes it in one line.
- Licensing concentrates power in the few firms able to qualify. The safety backstop and the market power come from the same mechanism, the tension Buterin and Narayanan & Kapoor warn about.
- The 25% AI tax has no stated base, and the revenue goes to treaty nations, not directly to households. Stronger public finances (`y_fiscal` 0.5) rest on that tax, the fees and 7% growth.
- Job quality is lowered to −0.25 (from −0.1) and replacement raised to 0.55 (from 0.45): the extract describes rising unemployment where protections are weak and traditional skills losing relevance, with capability beyond Big AI's. Gains stay near the centre (−0.1): firms concentrate value, but the treaty and governments redistribute it.
- On the labour map its nearest neighbours are `shumer`, `ai-2040-plan-a`, `wef-supercharged`, `citrini` and `loving-grace`; it sits to the right of Big AI / Arms Race at a similar level of replacement, which is how the framework places it: equally fast and centralised, but coordinated.

# Open Frontier — extract

## Summary

AI capability progress slows after 2028, yet inexpensive open-weight models near parity with proprietary systems enable deeper adoption and substitution in exposed sectors (ch. 3, “Open Frontier”, Capability, Distribution and model access, and Labour displacement). Digital-native firms get sizeable productivity gains, while workers displaced from junior and some middle-management jobs often take lower-paid work (ch. 3, “Open Frontier”, Adoption and Labour displacement). A wider range of providers reduces dependence on US firms, while China sets standards across much of the world and security incidents intensify (ch. 3, “Open Frontier”, Distribution and model access, Security and Global cooperation).

## Key details

- **Source:** AI Scenarios 2030: Helping policymakers plan for the future of AI, Government Office for Science, Government Office for Science (GOV.UK), 2026-06-15. `sources/go-science-ai-scenarios-2030-2026/`
- **Other sources for this scenario:** None.
- **Reading notes:** Full GOV.UK HTML report, including the scenario narrative, the outlook to 2040, and the cross-cutting findings.
- **Scenario set:** Slow Burn and Open Frontier (trajectory 1), Augmented Growth and Transformation Economy (trajectory 2), and Take-Off (trajectory 3). The six uncertainties are capability, distribution and model access, security, adoption, labour displacement, and global cooperation (Executive summary, “Methodology” and “Scenarios overview”; ch. 2).
- **Geography:** Global developments, with implications for the UK (ch. 1, “Project scope”).
- **Authors' framing:** Exploratory policy-planning scenario. “They are not predictions, and the future may involve elements from all scenarios” (Foreword).
- **Authors' view of likelihood:** Not stated. The 2040 outlook is more uncertain than the 2030 narrative (ch. 3, “Guide to the scenario narratives”).

### Timeline

- After 2028 — geopolitical conflict disrupts supply chains and limits compute. Shifts to cheaper open weights reduce leading developers’ valuations and trigger a wider market correction in 2028 (ch. 3, “Open Frontier”, Capability and Distribution and model access).
- By 2030 — many workflows are at human capability, the AI market stabilises with US, European and Chinese providers, and China supplies infrastructure outside the US and Europe (ch. 3, “Open Frontier”, Capability and Distribution and model access).
- 2030s to 2040 — more capable models and wider adoption are expected to sustain growth and increase labour market churn, while the net employment effect remains ambiguous (ch. 3, “Open Frontier”, Outlook to 2040).

### Policy stance

- **Stance:** Off. New government responses to the scenario are outside the scope; export controls and existing regulatory boundaries form part of the backdrop (ch. 3, “Scenario limitations” and “Open Frontier”, Global cooperation).
- **Policies assumed in the scenario:** Export controls and East Asian conflict restrict compute supply. Western regulation carries little weight over leading Chinese systems. People remain involved in military AI applications, and social expectations constrain certain AI applications in healthcare, education and criminal justice (ch. 3, “Open Frontier”, Adoption and Global cooperation).
- **Policies the source recommends:** The report invites policymakers to monitor signs of each future, stress-test plans across the set, assess impacts from other perspectives and identify outcomes the UK could influence. It proposes backcasting, policy stress testing and considering shocks as methods (ch. 4, “How to use the AI 2030 scenarios” and “Three methodologies for applying scenarios to policy making”).

## Key dynamics explored

- Conflict constrains compute and slows progress. Open-weight alternatives are sufficiently capable for most applications at lower cost and compute, shifting demand, reducing frontier valuations and widening supplier competition (ch. 3, “Open Frontier”, Capability and Distribution and model access).
- Inexpensive integration and greater autonomy enable productivity gains and substitution in digital-native firms; liability, delivery and security concerns slow deployment elsewhere (ch. 3, “Open Frontier”, Adoption and Labour displacement).
- China’s default stack outside the US and Europe enables it to define standards and increases downstream dependency. Openness increases scrutiny, but safety research receives less investment and deployed systems are hard to monitor (ch. 3, “Open Frontier”, Distribution and model access, Security and Global cooperation).

*Applies across the set.* The report treats workforce displacement and augmentation as concurrent possibilities across the set. Routine execution changes even in slower futures. Concentration at the frontier and commoditisation behind it can coexist. Uneven adoption and reliance on US- and China-centred ecosystems affect how productivity gains are distributed (ch. 4, “Cross-cutting implications across the AI 2030 scenarios”).

## State of technology

AI writes nearly all code and exceeds humans in verifiable domains. It provides competent assistance on well-scoped tasks, supports drug discovery and materials manufacturing, and learns and coordinates to a limited degree during deployment. It needs occasional human intervention and lags at insight, negotiating, strategic and value-based judgment, open-ended work and the retention of skills learned after deployment. It accelerates part of AI R&D. Robots perform specific repetitive tasks in controlled environments. Proprietary systems still power cutting-edge work, while open weights run most applications (ch. 3, “Open Frontier”, Capability and Distribution and model access).

## State of adoption

Tech, professional services, finance and life sciences integrate affordable open models with considerable access and autonomy, and dynamic start-ups disrupt markets. Existing organisations can be slowed by adaptation costs, legal and delivery constraints or security concerns. Scarce compute limits research with cutting-edge models but does not significantly limit integration of cheaper open models. Public opinion is mixed and cultural expectations limit use in sensitive sectors (ch. 3, “Open Frontier”, Distribution and model access and Adoption).

## How the economy works

### GDP growth and productivity

AI provides considerable productivity and growth gains. The report expects growth to receive a sustained boost in the 2030s as capabilities improve across domains and adoption expands (ch. 3, “Open Frontier”, Adoption and Outlook to 2040).

### Labour market dynamics

Substitution causes significant layoffs among junior staff and some middle managers in tech, finance and professional services. Most displaced workers find new work, often at substantially lower pay. Few new high-paying jobs emerge, such as integration and auditing. Many sectors, including health and education, see little unemployment impact. Cognitive workers supervise multiple agents and spend more time framing, ideating and validating. By 2040 sectoral churn could rise further with ambiguous net effects on employment (ch. 3, “Open Frontier”, Labour displacement and Outlook to 2040).

### Winners and losers

Affordable access opens economic opportunity, especially for digital-native firms and dynamic start-ups. The 2040 outlook expects benefits to concentrate among people in AI-adjacent roles and capital owners, with displaced mid-skill cognitive workers losing out (ch. 3, “Open Frontier”, opening, Adoption and Outlook to 2040).

### International economic patterns

By 2030 a competitive US, European and Chinese provider mix reduces some middle powers’ US dependence, while China becomes the default stack outside the West and uses resulting dependency to gain power. Export controls and conflict constrain compute supply. A later shift in global frontier leadership could matter more if combined with transformative capabilities (ch. 3, “Open Frontier”, Distribution and model access, Global cooperation and Outlook to 2040).

### Market pricing and scarcity

Competition keeps AI affordable. Cheaper, less compute-intensive open weights suit most commercial uses; limited compute restricts deployment of the most advanced proprietary systems (ch. 3, “Open Frontier”, Distribution and model access and Adoption).

### Financial markets and stability

Lower frontier developer valuations trigger a wider correction in 2028. By 2030 the market has stabilised around a broader range of suppliers (ch. 3, “Open Frontier”, Distribution and model access).

## Impact on the social contract

### Effort is rewarded

Workers displaced from junior and middle-management roles generally secure work elsewhere, often for smaller wages, while few new high-paying AI-related positions emerge (ch. 3, “Open Frontier”, Labour displacement).

### A say in your future

Western regulators have little influence over Chinese systems, and AI-generated material has contributed to international incidents (ch. 3, “Open Frontier”, Global cooperation).

### A fair share of progress

Wider access offers broad economic opportunity. Distributional gains over the 2030s nevertheless favour AI-adjacent workers and capital owners, while displaced mid-skill cognitive workers lose out (ch. 3, “Open Frontier”, opening and Outlook to 2040).

## Other dynamics

Open access supports public evaluation but raises misuse and monitoring concerns. Defensive capabilities fall behind threats, some systems cause service outages, and security incidents heighten international tensions. Artificial intelligence changes military operations, with human involvement retained (ch. 3, “Open Frontier”, Security and Global cooperation).

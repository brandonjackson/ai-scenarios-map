# Open Frontier — summary

*UK Government Office for Science, "AI Scenarios 2030: Helping policymakers plan for the future of AI" (15 June 2026). One of five exploratory scenarios built on six uncertainties; Open Frontier shares Slow Burn's low capability but scores highest on distribution and model access. The authors say the scenarios "are not predictions". Read from the full GOV.UK report, including the outlook to 2040 and the cross-cutting findings; see `extract.md`.*

**The scenario.** "AI progress slows but AI still causes significant disruption." After 2028 conflict and export controls in East Asia limit compute. Cheap open-weight models become good enough for most applications, cutting leading developers' valuations and triggering a 2028 market correction. By 2030 the market has stabilised around US, European and Chinese providers, and China is the default stack outside the US and Europe. Digital-native firms and start-ups integrate cheap models with real autonomy and get sizeable productivity gains.

## Hard work pays
Broken for exposed workers. Substitution causes significant layoffs among junior staff and some middle managers in tech, finance and professional services. Most find new work, often at substantially lower pay, and few new high-paying jobs appear. Health and education see little unemployment impact.

## Affordable essentials
Not addressed. "Intense competition ensures prices remain affordable" for AI itself, but nothing changes the cost of housing, food, energy or care.

## Reliable safety net
Not addressed. Displaced workers mostly find new jobs, so the cost lands as lower pay rather than as claims on support.

## Responsive government
Weakened. Western regulators have little influence over leading Chinese systems, and AI-generated material contributes to international incidents. Defences fall behind threats and some systems cause service outages.

## Time for what matters
Not addressed.

## Bottom line
Open access spreads opportunity but not the gains: the 2040 outlook expects benefits to concentrate among people in AI-adjacent roles and capital owners, with displaced mid-skill cognitive workers losing out.

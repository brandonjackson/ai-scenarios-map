# Promethean Fire — extract

## Summary

Fast-follower competition spreads transformative AI that complements workers and extends what they can do (“A Typology for Technological Revolutions”). The cost of intelligence falls as firms compete, efficiency improves and useful innovations move toward applications (“Which Quadrant Is Our AI Future?”). Toloui illustrates how a technology can support rising living standards through direct complementarities, cheaper output and the work generated by new industries. Policy can support a positive version of this trajectory by widening access, investing in worker capabilities and containing safety risks (“Promethean Fire” historical examples; “Five Major Policy Implications”).

## Key details

- **Source:** Titans, Swarms, or Human Renaissance? Technological Revolutions and Policy Lessons for the AI Age, Ramin Toloui, The Digitalist Papers, Vol. 2, 2025-12-11. `sources/toloui-titans-swarms-2025/`
- **Other sources for this scenario:** None
- **Reading notes:** Full essay, including the typology, historical analogues, AI outlook, and five policy priorities.
- **Scenario set:** Two axes form four quadrants. Winner-take-all versus fast-follower market structure crosses labour replacement versus labour augmentation. The four quadrants are Titan’s Dominion, Copilot Empire, Disruption Swarm and Promethean Fire. The constructive Copilot row covers an expressly mentioned policy-dependent form of Copilot Empire (“A Typology for Technological Revolutions”; “Priority 3”).
- **Geography:** The policy agenda is US-centred, with international comparison, economic rivalry and historical examples from other countries (“Applying the Matrix to the Technological Past”; “Complicating Factors”; “Five Major Policy Implications”).
- **Authors' framing:** A typology and adaptable policy agenda for transformative AI, grounded in historical analogues. The quadrants are conceptual possibilities (“A Typology for Technological Revolutions”; “Conclusion”).
- **Authors' view of likelihood:** No probabilities. The author finds evidence for all four combinations and says the outcome depends on interaction between the technology and institutions (“Which Quadrant Is Our AI Future?”).

### Timeline

No dated scenario storyline. Evidence used to motivate the axes includes ChatGPT’s late-2022 launch and China’s DeepSeek entering in early 2025 (“Which Quadrant Is Our AI Future?”).

### Policy stance

- **Stance:** Mixed. Competitive diffusion and augmentation define the quadrant. The positive form is an explicit policy goal, and public investments helped deliver analogous historical outcomes (“Promethean Fire” historical examples; “Five Major Policy Implications”).
- **Policies assumed in the scenario:** The economic quadrant has no fixed AI policy package. Toloui draws attention to education and infrastructure in the historical examples and proposes ways to promote the positive form (“Promethean Fire” historical examples; “Five Major Policy Implications”).
- **Policies the source recommends:** *Applies across the set.* Prepare adjustable policy levers with different settings for different quadrants. (1) Shift the tax burden from hiring to capital or super-normal rents, revisit a public health insurance option and adjust wage subsidies or transfers if needed (“Priority 1”). (2) Back worker-augmenting research, procurement and training via federal R&D, NAIRR compute and data, SBIR/STTR prototypes, WIOA, apprenticeships and employer–college partnerships, and use adoption measures and voluntary certification (“Priority 2”). (3) Start with interoperable APIs, portable customer data, limits on exclusivity and cloud switching barriers, and expand DOJ/FTC capacity for stronger interventions if bottlenecks emerge (“Priority 3”). (4) Require reporting of advanced training and compute, independent evaluations, material incident reporting, disclosure, meaningful human review and appeals. Consider due-process withdrawal powers and updated tort standards with safe harbours (“Priority 4”). (5) Expand energy, grid and chip supply while protecting households and small businesses from utility cost shifts. Modernize lawful, privacy-protective data sharing, run regulatory sandboxes and extend diffusion assistance to small firms (“Priority 5”). The quadrant-specific priority is to turn capabilities into broad living-standard gains while containing misuse and misalignment (“Five Major Policy Implications”).

## Key dynamics explored

- With rapid catch-up, falling unit costs and interoperability, the innovative edge shifts from controlling frontier development to deploying and adapting tools (“A Typology for Technological Revolutions”; “Which Quadrant Is Our AI Future?”).
- Complementary skills and cheaper output can generate more demand, including along supply chains and in new industries that use the output (“Promethean Fire” historical examples).
- Training, public education, compute access and deployment infrastructure expand the population able to use the technology (“Promethean Fire” historical examples; “Priorities 2 and 5”).

## State of technology

Knowledge spreads quickly, catching up is feasible, and switching costs are low. Alternative paths to continued capability gains include efficiency improvements, ongoing investment by multiple large firms, open models near the frontier, and specialized smaller models that are good enough for many uses (“A Typology for Technological Revolutions”; “Which Quadrant Is Our AI Future?”).

## State of adoption

*Applies across the set.* Integrating a general-purpose technology requires workflow and process redesign, skills and complementary investments. Institutional inertia and social resistance may slow replacement, though automating may be easier initially than developing effective augmentation. Organizational differences, task requirements and worker readiness mean adoption can vary sharply (“A Typology for Technological Revolutions”; “Which Quadrant Is Our AI Future?”). Competition lets deployers innovate across applications. Public procurement, extension services, access to compute and data, regulatory sandboxes and education are proposed ways to enable this diffusion (“Priorities 2 and 5”).

## How the economy works

### GDP growth and productivity

The historical analogues illustrate productivity from both better machinery and changes to assembly lines and other workflows. Lower costs expand demand for output, intermediate inputs and wholly new products (“Promethean Fire” historical examples).

### Labour market dynamics

Augmentation can increase demand for operators, maintenance and integration workers, lower costs to expand demand, and enable products and industries requiring new work. Potential complements include human empathy, personal connection, physical presence, judgement, imagination and leadership. Early evidence favours less experienced workers in call centres, software, writing, law and consulting, and experienced workers in accounting and entrepreneurship (“A Typology for Technological Revolutions”; “Which Quadrant Is Our AI Future?”). Historical analogues include rising productivity and employment in autos, and second-order jobs in inputs, local services, hospitality and other sectors (“Promethean Fire” historical examples).

### Winners and losers

A decentralized, worker-augmenting structure allows innovation and benefits across many deployers, workers and consumers. Complementarities vary by field, skills and task (“Promethean Fire” historical examples; “Which Quadrant Is Our AI Future?”).

### Household incomes and living standards

Higher living standards are the aim of the positive version. In the historical examples, sanitation and pharmaceuticals improved health, while appliances reduced housework and enabled more paid work outside the home for women (“Promethean Fire” historical examples; “Five Major Policy Implications”).

### Government finances

*Applies across the set.* Accelerated depreciation and expensing expanded in 2025, and low effective taxes on profits, dividends and capital gains contrast with the income/payroll tax wedge and employers’ health-benefit costs. That mix can favour automation of workers even for small gains. Toloui suggests funding Social Security and Medicare payroll tax cuts with earmarked capital revenue, possibly focusing on super-normal AI rents if concentration dominates (“Priority 1”).

### International economic patterns

*Applies across the set.* US–China competition for AI leadership and global influence pushes towards rapid research or deployment. Misuse, misalignment and economic disruption favour caution. The US, EU and China approach regulation differently. Chip resilience depends on investment at home and on allied partnerships for critical inputs (“Complicating Factors”; “Priority 5”).

### Market pricing and scarcity

Competition reduces unit costs of AI. The historical mechanism is that higher output per worker reduces production costs and final prices, increasing demand for products and for the labour that makes, supplies and uses them (“Promethean Fire” historical examples; “Which Quadrant Is Our AI Future?”).

## Impact on the social contract

### Effort is rewarded

Not addressed.

### Affordable essentials

The author’s historical examples describe improvements in sanitation and health, agriculture and home appliances. For future AI infrastructure, Toloui further proposes safeguarding households and small businesses against higher utility prices (“Promethean Fire” historical examples; “Priority 5”).

### Reliable safety net

*Applies across the set.* Larger supports could be required with strong displacement and unequal gains. Examples are an expanded earned income tax credit, wage insurance and transfers independent of employment. A public option for health insurance could lower nonwage hiring costs (“Priority 1”).

### Time for what matters

Historical analogue: mass-produced appliances reduced housework and enabled more women to work outside the home (“Promethean Fire” historical examples).

### A say in your future

Not addressed.

### A fair share of progress

Widespread competition and complementarity can extend benefits. Education and infrastructure were important public investments in analogous technologies. Labour-enhancing innovation and procurement, safety governance and extension services are ways to support the positive AI version (“Promethean Fire” historical examples; “Five Major Policy Implications”).

## Other dynamics

*Applies across the set.* Transformative AI can amplify misuse, bias, discrimination and misalignment. Incidents can also undermine public support for useful diffusion. Demographic decline and a shrinking labour force in many countries could offset some labour-saving effects (“Which Quadrant Is Our AI Future?”; “Complicating Factors”; “Priority 4”).

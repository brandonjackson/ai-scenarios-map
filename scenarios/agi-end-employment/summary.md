# AGI and the End of Human Employment — summary

*Pascal Stiefenhofer (Newcastle University), "Artificial General Intelligence and the End of Human Employment: The Need to Renegotiate the Social Contract", arXiv:2502.07050 (February 2025), CC BY 4.0. A short theory paper built on three extended Cobb-Douglas production models. Read in full; see `extract.md`.*

**The scenario.** AGI operates "at near-zero marginal cost", improves through self-learning and can "fully replace both cognitive and physical labor". Firms minimise costs, so they substitute AGI for human workers until human labour drops out of production. At first AGI complements people and wages rise; once it becomes a substitute, displacement accelerates "exponentially" and the economy moves from a decentralised, human-labour system to a centralised one in which "capital owners of AGI control all productive output". The author calls the transition a "mathematical inevitability"; the collapse that follows is what happens without new institutions.

## Hard work pays
Broken for all. Human wages are driven toward zero, making "employment-based earnings obsolete". "Labor is no longer the foundation of individual agency", and social mobility falls.

## Affordable essentials
Not addressed directly. The paper says that with no wages "fewer consumers … can afford to buy goods", but does not discuss housing, food, energy or care.

## Reliable safety net
Fails. "Traditional economic models based on employment income distribution fail", and the scenario has no alternative. Aggregate demand collapses into "a Keynesian crisis where firms produce goods but cannot sell them".

## Responsive government
Undermined at the root. "Humans lose economic agency", human economic power collapses completely, and the paper warns of "a technological aristocracy". It frames the social contract, after Rousseau, as resting on wage labour, which AGI "threatens to dissolve". Voting and political institutions are not discussed.

## Time for what matters
Not addressed.

## Bottom line
The purest economic statement of full replacement: production booms, wages vanish, and everything goes to whoever owns AGI. The author's remedies, all outside the scenario itself, are UBI, public or cooperative ownership of AGI, and progressive taxation of AGI capital, adopted "now" rather than after collapse.

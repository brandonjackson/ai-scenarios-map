# AGI and the End of Human Employment — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Off (a warning).** The modelled future assumes no new way of distributing income; its collapse outcomes are explicitly conditional on the absence of "alternative income distribution models (e.g., universal basic income or AI taxation)". The paper then recommends UBI, public or cooperative AGI ownership and progressive AGI capital taxation, without design details. Like `intelligence-curse`, the scenario is the default path the author wants avoided.

## Big assumptions
1. **AGI substitutes for all labour**, physical as well as cognitive, at near-zero marginal cost.
2. **AGI capital grows without bound** and swamps traditional capital.
3. **Competitive, cost-minimising firms** pay wages equal to marginal product.
4. **Consumption depends on wages**, so falling wages mean falling demand.
5. **Ownership of AGI stays concentrated** among existing capital owners.

## What must be true for this to occur
- AGI becomes good enough and cheap enough to replace human work in every task, including physical work.
- No mechanism (taxation, transfers, broad ownership) recycles AGI income to households.
- No task remains where humans keep a comparative advantage or where people prefer human work.

## Tensions and weak points
- The models are illustrative: no calibration, horizon or dates. The extract notes errors in the equations as printed (eq. 8's wage limit, eq. 24's wording, eq. 29), though the qualitative story doesn't depend on them.
- It doesn't explain why firms keep producing when demand collapses, or what happens to prices; the "Keynesian crisis" is asserted rather than modelled.
- Government finances are not discussed, so the fiscal axis pair is left blank.
- It reaches the same endpoint as `korinek-agi` and `intelligence-curse` by a different route: a production-function argument about wages and demand rather than scenario planning or rentier-state politics.

Draft scores are 1/3/1/1/3. Work, safety net and government are 1 because the paper describes universal loss of wage income, failed income systems and a complete collapse of human economic power. Essentials and time are 3 because the paper does not address them.

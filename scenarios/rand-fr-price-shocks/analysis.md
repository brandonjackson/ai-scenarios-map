# Price Shocks — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Off.** Cheap, widely accessible AI and competition produce the outcome. The simulation assumes no fiscal response beyond Fed rate cuts. Stabilising nominal demand and AI excise taxes are the authors' options outside the scenario. A taxpayer-run tax-filing AI appears as an illustration of at-cost access, not as an assumed policy.

## Big assumptions
1. **Capability.** AI and robots can replace essentially all existing jobs, as a pure substitute with no productivity gain beyond holding output constant.
2. **Adoption.** Full diffusion, no timeline. Model efficiency lets capable systems run at or near the cost of inference, including at the edge.
3. **Markets.** Open-weight or otherwise at-cost AI commoditises services, so profits fall to essentially zero and prices to near marginal cost. New human tasks absorb displaced workers.
4. **Politics.** No fiscal response. Monetary policy cuts rates but does not stop a decade of −2% inflation.

## What must be true for this to occur
- Open-weight models close the gap with closed ones, or access is priced so low that incumbents cannot compete.
- New remunerated human work appears at similar pay within about five years.
- Deflation in automated services is not offset by monetary or fiscal action.
- Physical-goods sectors stay a minor share, so Baumol-style inflation there does not dominate.

## Tensions and weak points
- The scenario is good for households in real terms and bad for the Treasury in nominal terms. Scores follow households and the map's fiscal axis follows the Treasury, so the two read differently by design.
- Personal income tax rises as a share of GDP in the simulation (15.6% against 14.0%) even though nominal GDP is a quarter lower. The fiscal stress comes from debt service and corporate receipts, not from the labour base.
- Long-run vulnerable revenue is 20% in Table 3 but 17% in the text.
- "Gains diffuse" rests on profits disappearing into lower prices and AI being "owned by the population at large", not on rising wages. Workers who own capital, including pension savers, lose the profit stream, which the extract does not trace.
- Neighbours: `gos-open-frontier` (open-weight, moderate replacement, mild diffusion) and `toloui-swarm`. It is the most diffuse of the RAND four.

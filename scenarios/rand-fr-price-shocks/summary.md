# Price Shocks — summary

*RAND Corporation, "Federal Revenue When AI Replaces Labor: An Examination of Economic Scenarios with Highly Capable Artificial Intelligence" (RR-A4980-1), by Carter C. Price and Akshaya Suresh; catalogued August 2026, revision 2.0 dated 8 May 2026 (a working paper version appeared in August 2025). A US federal-revenue study built on a 2×2 of AI pricing (monopolistic vs at cost) and new work for humans (new tasks vs none). Each of the four "extreme cases" is examined twice: a long-run case in which essentially all existing jobs are displaced, and a 2025–2035 simulation in which 10% of the workforce is displaced. AI is treated as a pure substitute for labour with no productivity gains. Read from the full report text; see `extract.md`.*

**The scenario.** AI at cost, new tasks for humans. Highly capable AI is "widely accessible for the general population at or near the cost of inference (such as with open-weight models)". Instead of displacing labour income on net, "AI systems could displace existing capital": firms compete against cheap AI, profits in services such as accounting, consulting and engineering fall to essentially zero, and most goods are delivered near marginal cost. The result is "substantial deflation in the prices for automated services" and a lower nominal national income, while some physical goods may get dearer. Humans find new jobs over time. The simulation sets core inflation at −2% for ten years, and nominal GDP in 2035 is 2.4 trillion against 3.9 trillion at baseline.

## Hard work pays
Kept in the long run, broken for some on the way. Displaced workers find new jobs over time, but the simulated unemployment rate sits at 14.2% for five years. Nothing more is said about pay or quality in the new work.

## Affordable essentials
Mixed. Automated services get much cheaper, and "a dramatic fall in prices relative to income" could raise consumption substantially. But physical goods may see inflation through Baumol's cost disease. Housing, food, energy and care are not discussed by name.

## Reliable safety net
Holds, with less room. Transfers are assumed to sustain the unemployed at 60% of prior consumption through the five-year spell. Revenue runs about a quarter lower than under monopolistic pricing, 17–20% of it directly vulnerable, and deflation makes the nominal debt "much harder" to service. Backward-looking benefit thresholds make the gap between nominal and real prices matter.

## Responsive government
Not addressed. Stabilising nominal demand and AI excise taxes are offered as options outside the scenario.

## Time for what matters
Not addressed.

## Bottom line
Good for consumers and workers, hard on capital owners and the Treasury: cheap AI turns profits into lower prices, and deflation shrinks the tax base and swells the real weight of the national debt.

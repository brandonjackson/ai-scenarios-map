# Transformative Disruption — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Off: the decoupling happens, and the remedies are an open research agenda.** The extract records "On", noting that the tier "is defined by the need for" structural income replacement. But the authors say they are "not yet ready to advocate specific policies", and that the tiers describe "what governments should do, not what they do inside a story". New tax bases, UBI, sovereign funds, worker equity, larger capital accounts and public investment in human-facing work are candidates only. The world described (record output, unprecedented unemployment, rent unpaid, a shrinking tax share) comes about under existing institutions. No links are recorded.

## Big assumptions
1. **Capability:** "if AI capabilities advance rapidly and broadly", AI becomes a general substitute for labour and delivers "transformative productivity gains".
2. **Adoption:** wide enough to push unemployment past any historical peak. The extract does not describe its path.
3. **Markets:** output reaches records while national income shifts from labour to capital. AI companies may "generate transformative returns". Care and teaching remain short of staff.
4. **Politics:** the state enters with "elevated deficits and recent inflationary experience", and its tax system leans on labour. Until new revenue sources are built, the response is constrained.

## What must be true for this to occur
- AI substitutes for labour across most of the economy, not only cognitive niches.
- Aggregate demand and output hold up despite mass unemployment, so the economy "grow[s] substantially alongside sustained technological displacement".
- Effective tax rates on labour stay well above those on capital as the income shift happens.
- New redistribution mechanisms are not in place when the tier arrives.

## Tensions and weak points
- Record output alongside rent going unpaid shows a distribution failure, not a production failure. The source states this tension and does not resolve it.
- The authors say deficit-financed support is "textbook countercyclical policy" but that structural displacement needs fiscally sustainable answers. If inflation or fiscal crisis coincides, they fall back on spending-neutral tools, which limits what the safety net can do.
- The catalogue says incomes are sustained by new taxes and broad sharing. The source names these as needed, not delivered.
- Neighbours: `gos-take-off`, `ai-2027`, `anthropic-extreme` and `agi-end-employment`. It is more severe than `anthropic-extreme` (11.9% unemployment), since it passes historical peaks. Its DeepMind counterpart `dm-policy-structural` reaches a similar level of replacement but sits far to the right, because a capital stake is assumed there.

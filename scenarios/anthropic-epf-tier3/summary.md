# Transformative Disruption — summary

*Tier 3 of Anthropic's "Economic Policy Framework" ("A policy framework for AI's impact on work"), a 14-page US policy paper published in June 2026 with a web summary. Three tiers are set by the unemployment rate, each matched to the policies Anthropic would want ready. The tiers are contingencies, not forecasts. Here the authors say they are "not yet ready to advocate specific policies". Read from the full PDF and web summary; see `extract.md`.*

**The scenario.** Unemployment is at "levels never before sustained alongside an economy generating record output", "past the edge of the maps that policymakers and economists have historically used". Job searches stretch "past a year, then past two, and for some, eventually stops". Savings are drawn down and "rent is paid late, then not at all". National income shifts from labour to capital, and AI companies may "generate transformative returns". Labour is taxed more heavily than capital, so "the current tax system captures a shrinking share of a growing economy, even as the cost of sustained income support rises". The US may enter this period with "elevated deficits and recent inflationary experience". The authors list candidate responses, all of them open: new tax bases, universal basic income, AI sovereign wealth funds, worker equity, much larger capital accounts, and public investment in care and teaching jobs.

## Hard work pays
Broken for all. "The link Americans have long taken for granted—between contributing to the economy and sharing in its rewards—is strained or broken." The authors call it "a structural decoupling of productive contribution from income".

## Affordable essentials
Broken for some. The long-term unemployed fall behind on rent and then stop paying it. Prices are not otherwise described. The authors note that inflation may continue alongside displacement.

## Reliable safety net
Broken for all. "Traditional safety net tools may not be sufficient", and wage-linked benefits are hard to justify when job loss lasts years. The tax base shrinks just as costs rise, and fiscal headroom may already be gone. Without the new revenue and redistribution the authors are still researching, the existing system cannot carry "a large share of the workforce".

## Responsive government
Not addressed. The authors say "we have significant agency to shape how the future unfolds", but they do not describe how government responds in this tier.

## Time for what matters
Not addressed. Funding care and teaching jobs "keeps paid work available for those who want it". The idea that people may "work far less" is a longer-horizon hope, not part of this tier.

## Bottom line
Record output alongside record unemployment, with a tax system built for a wage economy. The tier names the problem and the candidate tools but chooses none of them. Anthropic says it is "ready and willing to pay our fair share".

# Artificial Superintelligence — summary

*"Organizing Intelligence", Alex Imas, Andrew Koh and Shane Legg (Google DeepMind), arXiv:2611.05417, 31 October 2026, with the companion DeepMind Institute essay "The Economics of Intelligence" (29 October 2026). A theoretical model, explicitly not a forecast, that sorts AGI into four regimes by task competence and coordination. This is the regime where AI crosses both thresholds: it beats every human at every task, and bigger AI organisations become more productive per agent. Read from the paper's main sections and Appendix E and from the essay in full; see `extract.md`.*

**The scenario.** Deep markets go to "one giant firm in the limit". In the essay's account, niche markets "no longer shelter human teams: lean AI firms can now serve specialized demand without needing massive scale." Most goods and services "may become commoditized and society would grow much richer in real terms." The essay's possible endpoint follows past structural change: spending flows to the relational sector, where "human involvement is itself part of the value" (healthcare, education, services, craft), and to "brand-new jobs". The paper's worked example covers only outcomes "around the threshold of ASI".

## Hard work pays
Broken for some. In the example, specialists keep niche jobs through comparative advantage, but at 0.27 against an outside option of 0.25. Coordinators earn the outside option and are unemployed. Pay in commoditised production is "anchored by the cost of compute". Against that, the essay suggests the relational sector "may expand substantially and employ much of the workforce at high real wages". This is offered as a possibility, not a result.

## Affordable essentials
Not addressed. Most goods may be commoditised, but the source does not discuss housing, food, energy or care. Healthcare and education sit in the relational sector, where "human delivery increases its value".

## Reliable safety net
Broken for some, by implication. The model has no taxes or transfers. Coordinators lose their jobs in the example, and in the task-model variant human employment falls to zero. The scenario relies on the relational sector, not on transfers, to absorb displaced workers.

## Responsive government
Not addressed. The model has no government. Regulation is named only as a real-world brake on a "firm singularity".

## Time for what matters
Not addressed.

## Bottom line
The richest regime in real terms and the most concentrated. Whether workers share in it depends on a relational sector the model does not include.

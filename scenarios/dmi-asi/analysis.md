# Artificial Superintelligence — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Off.** Concentration in deep markets and the shift of work toward the relational sector both come from markets and consumer spending. No government action is modelled, and regulation appears only as a real-world brake on a "firm singularity".

## Big assumptions
1. **Capability:** AI dominates the full range of human capabilities. In the example, its task skills are 12 against a best human of 9, and its coordination is 1.05, above both the threshold and the best human coordinator.
2. **Adoption:** frictionless, with copies rented at compute cost. Humans keep a role only because compute (1) costs more than the outside option (0.25).
3. **Markets:** one giant AI firm in deep markets, lean AI firms in niches. Rising real incomes are spent on human-delivered relational goods.
4. **Politics:** absent. Nothing in the scenario redistributes the gains.

## What must be true for this to occur
- AI crosses both the competence and the coordination threshold. The authors call crossing the second "an empirical question" and describe today's coordination as subcritical.
- Nothing (regulation, rival providers, capacity) stops deep markets concentrating.
- For the essay's benign endpoint, consumers must keep valuing human delivery enough that the relational sector employs much of the workforce at high real wages.

## Tensions and weak points
- **Paper versus essay.** The essay says niche markets "no longer shelter human teams" and sketches high real wages in the relational sector. The paper's example keeps all twelve specialists in the niche at a wage just above the outside option, with coordinators unemployed, and does "not consider human employment in the case where ASI improves further". The relational sector is not in the model at all.
- **Richer but concentrated.** "Much richer in real terms" sits against one giant firm whose owners are not named. x_labor is inferred, at −0.25, between the paper's concentration and the essay's broad prosperity.
- **Comparative advantage hinges on compute cost.** Proposition 4(i) makes all firms machine-only once compute costs no more than human reservation wages. Any human role depends on compute staying relatively dear.
- **Neighbours.** Near `korinek-agi`, `gos-take-off` and `ai-2027` on replacement, but less concentrated than those because of the essay's relational-sector endpoint. In the set it sits at the top on replacement, with `dmi-supercooperative` the more concentrated of the two.

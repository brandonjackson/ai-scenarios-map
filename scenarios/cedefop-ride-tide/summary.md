# Left Alone to Ride the Tide — summary

*Cedefop (European Centre for the Development of Vocational Training), "Preparing for 2040: four AI-powered scenarios for the future of continuing skills development", Publications Office of the European Union (November 2025). Four exploratory EU scenarios built from 21 STEEPV trends, morphological analysis and a semi-Delphi survey of 132 respondents; not a 2×2 grid, and no probabilities. This is Scenario B, built on the path where "AI disrupts most jobs and businesses; it's impact is hard to manage and only some segments of the population benefit". Read from the report's full text; see `extract.md`.*

**The scenario.** It is 2040. "Successive waves of AI transformation" have reshaped work. Most jobs are transformed rather than replaced — augmentation "marginally prevails" — but firms replace workers "when economically advantageous". The state and employers "step back from workforce skills development", leaving adults to keep their skills relevant on their own. A "two-tier workforce" results: tier 1 embraces individual responsibility and thrives; tier 2 struggles and falls behind. "Traditional employment has become a privilege of the few", replaced by micro-tasks, project work and temporary contracts, and tech platforms dominate learning.

## Hard work pays
Broken for some. Outcomes track prior advantage: those with better education, money, location and social capital "bounce back", while those starting disadvantaged "increasingly struggle to keep pace". People who cannot use AI tools "move towards low-skilled/low-quality jobs, rather than unemployment, as labour markets are tight". "Disparity spirals" compound disadvantage. "We are all riding the AI tide, but some are surfing while others are struggling to tread water."

## Affordable essentials
Not addressed.

## Reliable safety net
Broken for some. The state and employers step back, "traditional unionisation is weakened and cannot effectively support those lagging behind", and diversity and equality work is left to civil society. Those whose mental health suffers are "pushed into unemployment and inactivity". The authors call support structures for guided adaptation decisive but do not describe any.

## Responsive government
Strained. Regulation "fail[s] to address" concerns over quality, privacy and ethics in learning; the AI race crowds out sustainability policy; and social dialogue fragments into "company-specific negotiations ... limited to basic framework conditions". Labour relations are "tense". New, cause-oriented forms of worker representation emerge — "evolution rather than dissolution".

## Time for what matters
Worse for many. Rapid change brings "increasing cognitive load, stress and potential burnout", and rising "anxiety, stress and feelings of inadequacy". Some workers, especially the young, choose purpose-driven, value-aligned project work.

## Bottom line
Not a jobs collapse but a responsibility shift: AI's gains go to the already advantaged because nobody else carries the cost of adapting. "Technological progress does not automatically translate to societal progress."

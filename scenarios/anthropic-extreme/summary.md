# Extreme — summary

*Korinek, Jones, Sacher, Cotter & McCrory, "Economic Scenarios for Transformative AI", The Anthropic Institute Working Paper No. 2026-02 (September 2026), with Anthropic's companion web explorer, "Scenarios for our Economic Future", and its June 2026 Economic Policy Framework. A US model with three scenarios (Modest, Substantial, Extreme) compared in January 2030. Read from the full working paper, the explorer text and the policy framework; see `extract.md`.*

**The scenario.** "The macroeconomic consequences go well beyond any event in history." AI's reach grows from 14% to 50% of task mass by 2030, it is used on 60% of the tasks it could do, and productivity on affected tasks rises to about 2.2 times its pre-AI level; the explorer says this would likely need recursively self-improving AI. About 30% of all task instances are affected, 90% of that work is automated and no new human tasks appear. GDP is 32.4% above the no-AI path and growing 15.4% a year; sustained, per-capita incomes would double about every five years.

## Hard work pays
Broken for knowledge workers. Cognitive employment falls 21.5% from mid-2026, cognitive wages are 11.5% below the no-AI path and slightly below their 2026 level, and cognitive unemployment reaches 17.9%, above any US post-war rate. Other workers, now comparatively scarce, see wages rise 33.6%.

## Affordable essentials
Not addressed.

## Reliable safety net
Overwhelmed unless redesigned. Total unemployment is 11.9%, above the peak after the global financial crisis. A transfer of about 9% of GDP, "about the size of Social Security and Medicare combined", could restore cognitive workers' income while leaving the rest of the economy over 20% ahead, but transfers of that size in response to technology have no precedent, and the paper points to past regional displacement to show gains do not compensate losers on their own.

## Responsive government
Under strain. The labour share falls from 60% to 45.2%. Labour income is up only 0.5% while capital income is up 81.4%, which accounts for almost all the extra output.

## Time for what matters
Not addressed.

## Bottom line
An economy rich enough to make everyone better off, with no mechanism in it that does so.

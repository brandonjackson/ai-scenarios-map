# Extreme — summary

*Korinek, Jones, Sacher, Cotter & McCrory, "Economic Scenarios for Transformative AI", Anthropic Institute Working Paper 2026-02 (10 September 2026), with the online Econ Scenario Explorer*

**The scenario.** AI "transforms the economy" beyond "any event in history." By 2030 it affects roughly half the tasks cognitive workers did in 2025, more than doubles productivity on them, and automates 90% of that work with no new tasks created for people. GDP is 32% above its no-AI path and growing 15.4% a year; sustained, per-capita incomes would double every five years.

## Hard work pays
Broken for knowledge workers. Cognitive employment falls 21.5% and cognitive wages are 11.5% below the no-AI path, slightly lower in 2030 than in 2026. Unemployment among those who started in cognitive work reaches 17.9%, "a stunning" figure "far above any postwar rate". Meanwhile wages in other occupations, where labour is now scarce, rise 33.6%.

## Affordable essentials
Split. Output soars, but the displaced lose the income to share in it.

## Reliable safety net
Overwhelmed. Economy-wide unemployment reaches 11.9%, above the post-financial-crisis peak. Holding cognitive workers' income at its no-AI level would take a transfer of about 9% of GDP, "roughly the size of Social Security and Medicare combined." The money exists, but "transfers of that scale in response to technological change have no precedent", and past experience is that "it mostly does not happen on its own."

## Responsive government
Strained. The labour share falls from 60% to 45%: "a full 15 percent of GDP moves from being paid to labor to being captured as a return on investment." Total labour income is almost unchanged while capital income is 81% higher. All of the growth goes to capital.

## Time for what matters
Worse for the displaced: insecurity, not free time.

## Bottom line
An economy rich enough to make everyone better off, with no mechanism that does so. The authors call retraining, income support, universal basic capital or basic income "a central question of economic policy" in this world.

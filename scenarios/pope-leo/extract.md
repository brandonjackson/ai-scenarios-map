# Antiqua et Nova: Note on the Relationship Between Artificial Intelligence and Human Intelligence — extract

## Summary

Artificial intelligence helps people perform tasks and can advance agriculture, education, healthcare and living standards when its use respects human dignity and serves the common good (§§40–47, 50–51). In work, it relieves people of repetitive tasks while preserving meaningful employment and scope for judgement (§§66–70). The note warns that concentration of ownership and the pursuit of efficiency can instead increase inequality, monitoring and worker displacement (§§52–55, 64–70).

### Policy stance

- **Stance:** On. The note calls for human oversight and institutions that direct AI toward solidarity and the common good (§§43–55).
- **Policies assumed in the scenario:** People and institutions retain responsibility for AI-mediated decisions and preserve meaningful work and relationships (§§44–48, 69–74).
- **Policies the source recommends:** Protect privacy and freedom, assess discriminatory outcomes, keep human responsibility for consequential decisions, make benefits broadly available and prohibit lethal autonomous weapons (§§44–55, 75–76, 89–94, 98–103).

## Key dynamics explored

- Neural networks can reproduce outputs of human cognition quickly, but human judgement, moral responsibility, embodiment, relationships and wisdom remain distinctive (§§7–35).
- AI can remove mundane work and create new roles. If deployed chiefly to cut costs, it can shift control over pace and judgement from workers to systems (§§64–70).
- Control over widely used systems gives a few companies disproportionate influence. Unequal access and biases in data and system design can extend existing social inequalities (§§52–55).

## State of technology

Current systems learn from large datasets, generate human-like text and images and can act on objectives with a degree of autonomy. They simulate selected manifestations of reasoning and lack human moral understanding. Performance on particular tasks is compatible with bias, fabrication and unreliable outputs (§§3, 7–12, 30–35, 84–89).

## State of adoption

AI is already being integrated into economic and financial systems and into education, healthcare and work. Uptake alone does not determine outcomes; design, objectives, accountability and conditions of deployment matter (§§4, 40–48, 64, 72, 80).

## How the economy works

### GDP growth and productivity

The note describes potential productivity gains from routine task automation and new opportunities in multiple sectors. No economy-wide growth estimate is supplied (§§51, 66–67).

### Labour market dynamics

Mundane tasks can be automated and new work can emerge. AI can also deskill workers, make them conform to machine speed, enable surveillance and remove jobs. AI should support participation, creativity and meaningful employment, and workers should retain responsibility and room to act (§§66–70).

### Winners and losers

People and communities with access to the technology can gain in education, health and work. Benefits may accrue disproportionately to a few companies or better-resourced populations, while disadvantaged groups face greater exclusion, discrimination and loss of work (§§51–55, 64–76).

### Household incomes and living standards

Agricultural, educational and cultural innovation could improve living standards. Replacement of workers without opportunities for dignified participation risks further impoverishment and marginalisation (§§51–52, 68–70).

### Government finances

Not addressed.

### International economic patterns

The note calls for attention to how inequalities across populations and nations may widen, and for benefits to reach the poorest and most vulnerable (§§50–55).

### Market pricing and scarcity

Not addressed.

### Financial markets and stability

Investment in AI and its integration into finance are already material. The note calls for scrutiny of relations between market incentives, concentration and the common good; it gives no market forecast (§§53–55, 64–65).

## Impact on the social contract

### Effort is rewarded

The note treats labour as a path to growth, personal fulfilment and human development. It urges institutions to expand meaningful employment and use AI to support creativity (§§69–70).

### Affordable essentials

AI can support medical diagnosis, care and administrative efficiency. If access depends on wealth or medical decisions prioritise economics, it could deepen existing gaps and harm the poor; the note calls for access to quality care for all (§§71–76).

### Reliable safety net

Not addressed.

### Time for what matters

Not addressed.

### A say in your future

Subsidiarity and solidarity should guide authority and responsibility. Concentrated control, surveillance and manipulated content can threaten privacy, freedom and trust in the public sphere (§§47, 52–55, 85–94).

### A fair share of progress

The note calls for AI innovations to benefit all people and communities, with particular attention to vulnerable groups and their participation (§§43, 50–55, 76).

## Other dynamics

The note addresses the place of human relationships in care and schooling, the hazards of simulated empathy and companionship, information authenticity, privacy, environmental modelling, energy and water demand, and human responsibility for military uses (§§56–63, 71–103).

# My techno-optimism (d/acc) — extract

## Summary

Defensive and decentralising technologies improve people’s ability to protect themselves, verify information and coordinate without depending on a small group of powerful actors (“d/acc,” “Defense-favoring worlds,” “Social technology”). AI development places more weight on tools that enhance human cognition and preserve agency while exercising caution about autonomous superintelligence (“So what are the paths forward for superintelligence?”; “A happy path”). The essay describes how this could allow continued technological progress with greater resilience and more options for democratic governance (“Defense-favoring worlds”; “We are the brightest star”).

## Key details

- **Source:** My techno-optimism, Vitalik Buterin, Vitalik Buterin, 2023-11-27. `sources/buterin-my-techno-optimism-2023/`
- **Other sources for this scenario:** None
- **Reading notes:** Full text.
- **Scenario set:** Standalone
- **Geography:** Global, with examples from Switzerland and communities in the Ethereum ecosystem (“Defense-favoring worlds,” “Social technology”).
- **Authors' framing:** Prescription for how to steer technology and a set of thought experiments about possible futures (“d/acc”; “So what are the paths forward for superintelligence?”).
- **Authors' view of likelihood:** The essay describes d/acc as a direction. The author says the AI extinction hypothesis is speculative and cites external survey estimates without endorsing a point prediction (“Existential risk is a big deal”).

### Timeline

2023-11-27 — publication. The author describes near-term defensive technologies and longer-run pathways for human–AI collaboration, without committed milestones (“A happy path”).

### Policy stance

- **Stance:** On. Success depends on deliberate choices about development, ownership and governance, including action by researchers, firms, organisations and communities (“d/acc”; “Social technology”).
- **Policies assumed in the scenario:** Development prioritises technologies that improve defence, resilience and decentralised coordination. The author considers competing arrangements for governance of advanced AI and does not prescribe one fully specified state policy (“d/acc”; “So what are the paths forward for superintelligence?”).
- **Policies the source recommends:** Prefer technologies that increase people’s ability to defend themselves and coordinate, invest in public-health resilience, security and information integrity, and favour human agency in AI tools (“Micro physical defense”; “Cyber defense”; “Info-defense”; “A happy path”).

## Key dynamics explored

- Improved individual and community protection reduces dependence on concentrated authority. The author sees a stronger balance of defence as favourable to decentralised or democratic government (“Defense-favoring worlds”).
- Without sufficient defensive capability, citizens and institutions can be pushed into greater reliance on central platforms or authorities. Effective privacy, security and evidence tools can expand the set of workable governance arrangements (“Cyber defense”; “Info-defense”).
- A single authority able to control advanced AI would itself hold exceptional power. Multiple AI providers may be unstable if competition tends toward concentrated ownership, and competition alone may not preserve meaningful human agency (“So what are the paths forward for superintelligence?”).
- Human-controlled interfaces and tools can preserve active involvement and extend human cognition (“A happy path”).

## State of technology

The essay examines increasingly capable AI and its distinction from previous technologies, widely available tools for security and verification, and AI interfaces that support iterative human input. Brain–computer interfaces are a speculative longer-run direction for empowering humans, with the author recognising access and distribution risks (“AI is fundamentally different”; “Cyber defense”; “A happy path”).

## State of adoption

Emerging defensive tools can become inherent to digital products, buildings or public infrastructure, increasing protection without requiring people to opt in to specialist tools. Existing blockchain and open deliberation communities provide testing grounds for governance mechanisms (“Micro physical defense”; “Cyber defense”; “Info-defense”; “Social technology”).

## How the economy works

### GDP growth and productivity

Not addressed.

### Labour market dynamics

AI can complement human cognition and widen what workers can do, an aim the essay connects to economic work on human-complementary AI. It offers no extrapolation for jobs or wages (“A happy path”).

### Winners and losers

Digitally mediated power can accrue to actors controlling AI, communications or other concentrated services. More accessible protection and governance tools can empower individuals and communities. Sophisticated augmentation could initially amplify the advantages of already powerful groups (“The sky is near”; “Cyber defense”; “A happy path”).

### Household incomes and living standards

Technology can expand human potential and provide comfortable lives. In an illustrative thought experiment, material comfort supplied by capable AI coexists with loss of independent agency, purpose and authenticity (“Even if we survive”; “We are the brightest star”).

### Government finances

Not addressed.

### International economic patterns

Resilient local systems reduce dependence on fragile long-distance supply chains, while AI complicates previous limits on central rule and surveillance across borders (“Macro physical defense”; “The sky is near”).

### Market pricing and scarcity

The author applies natural-monopoly concerns to competition among advanced AI providers and points to the practical concentration of parts of the internet, which can follow from consumers needing trusted defences (“Cyber defense”; “So what are the paths forward for superintelligence?”).

### Financial markets and stability

Not addressed.

## Impact on the social contract

### Effort is rewarded

Not addressed.

### Affordable essentials

Public-health resilience, safer environments and more self-sufficient access to necessities can help communities withstand crises (“Macro physical defense”; “Micro physical defense”).

### Reliable safety net

Not addressed.

### Time for what matters

The author questions whether leisure and comfort in an AI-run society suffice for purpose when trivial or consequential activity is performed better by machines. A better future preserves choices and projects where people have a real effect (“Even if we survive”; “A happy path”).

### A say in your future

Defensive capacity creates room for autonomy and democracy. Privacy-preserving identity, collective evidence tools and better deliberation can help communities coordinate. The essay cautions about concentration of AI control at governments and companies and considers the institutional challenges of both centralised and pluralistic approaches (“Defense-favoring worlds”; “Cyber defense”; “Info-defense”; “Social technology”; “So what are the paths forward for superintelligence?”).

### A fair share of progress

Steer technology to expand the abilities of people and communities, while recognising that tools for human enhancement could initially widen gaps in power (“A happy path”; “We are the brightest star”).

## Other dynamics

The essay also discusses environmental coordination, information integrity, online security, public health and competing views of AI existential risk (“The environment”; “Existential risk is a big deal”; “Micro physical defense”; “Info-defense”).

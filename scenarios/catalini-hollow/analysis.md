# The Hollow Economy — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Off; a warning.** The Hollow Economy is what happens when liability stays unpriced and verification, synthetic practice and augmentation are neglected. No policy that internalises risk is adopted: the priced liability wedge approaches zero, verification budgets collapse to the minimum, lightly regulated markets ship with low verification, and compute export controls only "buy time". The authors' playbook (mandatory liability insurance, verification public goods, "flight simulators for work", universal augmentation, a coalition of democracies) describes how to avoid this scenario, not part of it.

## Big assumptions
1. **Capability:** the cost of automating any measurable task falls exponentially with compute, and agents take on long, high-stakes tasks; misaligned behaviours already seen in evaluations persist.
2. **Adoption:** deployment "scales by default", racing ahead of verification, especially in lightly regulated markets and through open-source agent swarms.
3. **Markets:** verification is a public good, so firms under-invest in it; buyers cannot tell verified from reckless output, and a lemons market crowds out careful deployment.
4. **Politics:** governments neither price liability nor fund verification infrastructure, and the geopolitical race pushes alignment to the minimum.

## What must be true for this to occur
- Agent capability keeps outpacing human verification across most high-stakes work.
- Firms stop hiring juniors once agents are cheaper, and nothing replaces the experience juniors used to gain.
- AI-verifies-AI is treated as good enough by firms, insurers and regulators.
- Failures of unverified output stay hidden long enough to accumulate.
- No liability or insurance regime emerges to make deployers bear tail risk.

## Tensions and weak points
- **A model's basin, not a narrative.** There is no timeline or horizon; the scenario is an equilibrium of a formal model, with the authors' examples (Flash Crash, LTCM, Zillow) standing in for events.
- **Abundance and bankruptcy at once.** Prices for measurable execution trend to zero, yet people are "structurally bankrupt". The model reconciles them through the leak crowding out real consumption, but the extract gives no figures on household incomes, so the essentials score is inferred.
- **Public finances are silent.** The paper says nothing on taxes, debt or social insurance, so the fiscal map is left blank even though wages collapsing towards compute cost would suggest a shrinking labour tax base; with measured GDP exploding, the net effect on revenue cannot be settled from the extract.
- **Succession framing.** Under the "Successor" view, agents taking human consumption could count as welfare; the map scores the scenario on the human (Parasite) view the authors use to frame it as a warning.
- **Neighbours:** `ai-2027`, `intelligence-curse` and `gradual-disemp`. It shares `gradual-disemp`'s slow erosion of human control and `intelligence-curse`'s concentration, but its mechanism is unverifiable output rather than lost bargaining power.

## Scoring notes
Scores are 1/2*/–/1/–. Work is 1: wages for measurable work fall to compute cost "regardless of the historical prestige or training required", junior hiring freezes, average expertise collapses, and the paper's advice to displaced workers is "exit". Essentials is 2, inferred: the paper does not discuss housing, food or care, but the leak crowds out real consumption and leaves people "starved of functioning infrastructure", while measurable goods do get cheaper, so it is not 1. Government is 1: the end-state is "a complete hollowing-out of human control", with succession a real risk. Safety net and time are not addressed.

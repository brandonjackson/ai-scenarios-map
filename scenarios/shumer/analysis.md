# Something Big Is Happening — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Policy off, and individualised.** No collective response is proposed. Every recommendation is personal: use paid AI tools daily, build savings, steer children toward adaptability, lean into relationships, physical presence and licensed accountability. The essay treats the technology as "too powerful to stop" and the policy question as outside its scope.

## Big assumptions
1. **Trends extrapolate.** METR task-length doubling "has held for years with no sign of flattening", so AI works independently for days within a year and on month-long projects within three.
2. **Coding progress generalises.** Software got there first only because labs aimed there; other knowledge work follows on a similar curve.
3. **Recursive self-improvement has begun.** AI-assisted training and deployment (the GPT-5.3-Codex system card) is read as the start of an intelligence explosion.
4. **Capability means displacement.** Once AI *can* do a job, it will replace it within a few years. Adoption frictions (regulation, liability, institutional inertia) only "buy time".
5. **Lab leaders' forecasts are reliable.** Amodei's timelines and job-loss estimates carry much of the argument.
6. **An individual edge is possible and temporary.** Being early helps until "everyone figures it out".

## What must be true for this to occur
- Capability keeps improving at or above the current exponential pace through 2026–2028.
- AI reliability, judgement and "taste" in non-coding work catch up with coding.
- Employers restructure around AI quickly rather than slowly.
- No regulatory, legal or social brake (licensing, liability rules, unions) slows deployment in professions.

## Tensions and weak points
- **Evidence base:** personal testimony from a founder who sells AI products and newsletters (the essay links to his own agent, newsletter and X account). That doesn't make it wrong, but the map's "85M views" signals reach, not reliability.
- **Fallacy of composition:** "Being early" can help an individual but not everyone at once. The essay's advice gives no answer to the collective problem it describes.
- The first-person lived experience (a developer replaced by tools) is a real and early signal, and it matches the junior-hiring evidence behind Windfall's Lost Generation.
- The essay was a market event in its own right (compare Citrini). Narratives about AI displacement can move capital and employer behaviour before capability does, a point Doctorow makes too.

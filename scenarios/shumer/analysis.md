# Something Big Is Happening — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Off, and individualised.** The trajectory is driven by frontier labs and user adoption, with no government response specified. The extract records no policies assumed and none recommended; the advice is personal: practise with current models, save, and build skills grounded in trust and accountability.

## Big assumptions
1. **Capability.** Frontier models already handle specified, multi-step software work with apparent judgement, and are improving across many occupation-specific skills at once.
2. **Recursion.** Current models help build later ones, a feedback loop the author expects to accelerate (he cites Amodei's one-to-two-year estimate for autonomous development of later models).
3. **Adoption.** Organisations and labour markets lag capability, but only for a few years; free products understate what paid models can do.
4. **Lab leaders' forecasts hold.** Amodei's 50% entry-level estimate carries much of the labour-market argument.

## What must be true for this to occur
- Capability keeps improving quickly through 2026–2028, and gains in coding carry over to law, finance, writing, medical analysis and customer service.
- Employers restructure around AI within the two-to-five-year window rather than slowly.
- Licensing, liability and the value of in-person trust only delay replacement rather than stopping it.

## Tensions and weak points
- **Evidence base:** personal testimony and industry accounts, with the key numbers borrowed from Amodei. That makes it a signal, not a model.
- **Fallacy of composition:** being early can help one worker but not everyone at once, and the essay offers no answer to the collective problem it describes.
- The extract gives nothing on growth, prices, government finances or the social contract, so the fiscal map pair stays blank and three promises are not scored.

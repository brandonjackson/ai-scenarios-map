# Co-Pilot Economy — summary

*World Economic Forum, "Four Futures for Jobs in the New Economy: AI and Talent in 2030", white paper (7 January 2026). Four exploratory global scenarios on two axes, incremental versus exponential AI advancement and limited versus widespread workforce readiness, built from research and workshops with chief strategy officers to stress-test business decisions. No probabilities; the directions of change in Table 1 are illustrative. Read from the full 20-page paper; see `extract.md`.*

**The scenario.** A mid-2020s AI valuation bust cuts frontier funding and resets commercialisation timelines. Progress turns gradual, and firms tailor AI to specific tasks, keeping humans in core operations and decisions. Early worry about displacement speeds up the redesign of education and training, until AI literacy is as common as basic digital literacy was in the early 2020s. "AI deployment widens but remains shallow."

## Hard work pays
Holding, and improving for many. Job churn and displacement rise and routine administrative and basic analytical tasks are hollowed out; more than 40% of skills change by 2030. But mobility, reskilling, gig work and entrepreneurship expand, and demand grows for managerial, social and hybrid AI skills. Job quality improves where workers lead AI and worsens where it narrows creativity and agency. Raised skill floors narrow wage gaps slightly among middle- and high-skilled workers, while people without education, infrastructure or employer support are left out.

## Affordable essentials
Not addressed. Lower cost pressures stabilise inflation somewhat.

## Reliable safety net
Not addressed. Churn rises, but unemployment is flat in the paper's illustrative table.

## Responsive government
Mixed. Standards and data privacy rules multiply; some jurisdictions pursue interoperability while others nationalise or restrict infrastructure and tools, and talent-mobility policy ranges from borderless pipelines to protectionism. Disinformation raises polarisation and pushes trust towards verified sources and human intermediaries. Firms keep people in key decision-making roles.

## Time for what matters
Not addressed. Remote and flexible work widen opportunity in marginalised and peripheral communities.

## Bottom line
The WEF's most benign future, resting on two things: AI slowing down, and early investment in training, mobility, digital infrastructure and governance. Even here, wage polarisation edges up and those without access fall behind.

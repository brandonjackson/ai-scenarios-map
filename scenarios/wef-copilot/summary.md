# Co-Pilot Economy — summary

*World Economic Forum, "Four Futures for Jobs in the New Economy: AI and Talent in 2030", white paper (January 2026). Scenarios are global and built on two axes: AI advancement (exponential or incremental) and workforce readiness (widespread or limited).*

**The scenario.** "Gradual AI progress and availability of AI-ready skillsets shift the focus towards augmentation rather than mass automation." The "AI bubble" bursts in the mid-2020s, frontier funding dries up and expectations reset. Early displacement drives investment in AI literacy and reskilling, until "AI skills have become as common as digital literacy skills in the early 2020s." AI use spreads but stays shallow.

## Hard work pays
Improving. Workers use AI to augment routine tasks and managers lead human–AI teams. Administrative and basic analytical tasks are hollowed out and more than 40% of skills change by 2030, but labour markets show "higher mobility and job fluidity" and hybrid roles grow. "Job quality varies widely: improving where workers lead AI, and deteriorating where AI shrinks human creativity and agency." AI lifts skill floors, so wage gaps "narrow slightly among mid- and high-skilled workers", though those without education, infrastructure or supportive employers are locked out.

## Affordable essentials
Stable. Productivity growth rises above early-2020s rates, and "higher margins and lower cost pressures have somewhat stabilized inflation."

## Reliable safety net
Stable. Displacement and churn rise, but reskilling and mobility absorb them.

## Responsive government
Mixed. AI standards and privacy rules multiply but diverge; some places harmonise, others nationalise AI infrastructure or protect talent. As AI content overtakes human content, misinformation concerns grow and "trust shifts towards curated sources, verified platforms and human intermediaries."

## Time for what matters
Slightly better for some. Remote, flexible work and entrepreneurship widen opportunity for peripheral regions and marginalised communities.

## Bottom line
The WEF's only clearly good scenario, and it rests on two things: AI slowing down, and countries and firms that "invested early in training, mobility, digital infrastructure and AI governance."

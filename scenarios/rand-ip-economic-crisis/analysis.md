# Economic Crisis (Infinite Potential) — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Off, even though the extract records it as on.** The game is built for players to design responses, and the report is organised around what they recommended: emergency income support, WPA-style jobs programmes, a shift from labour to capital taxation, a sovereign wealth fund, stronger powers over frontier labs. None of that is in the scenario itself. The policies assumed in-game are the status quo: institutions and laws largely intact, Congress gridlocked, economic tools "ill-suited for the new AI economy", a tax system still built on payroll and income, and no authority for forceful intervention in frontier firms. The only deliberate new policy in the world is China's export controls on robotics inputs, which shapes the scenario but is not a domestic response. The crisis happens without new policy, so it sits on the map as off, and it has no Policy Atlas links.

## Big assumptions
1. **Capability:** a US lab achieves AGI between late 2026 and mid-2027, out of a frontier already able to displace whole categories of cognitive work.
2. **Adoption:** large-scale integration happens within months, fast enough to outrun retraining, while US hardware automation is held back by Chinese export controls.
3. **Markets:** gains flow to capital owners and a few "hyper-profitable" firms, producing a K-shaped economy in which GDP sets records while employment and consumer spending fall.
4. **Politics:** Congress stays gridlocked and tax reform takes years, so the fiscal squeeze turns into a looming debt spiral; competition with China blocks any choice to slow down.

## What must be true for this to occur
- AGI-level systems are deployed in the United States by mid-2027.
- Firms replace knowledge workers at scale within months of deployment, starting with entry-level roles.
- Productivity growth decouples from employment, and the tax base stays tied to labour income.
- No new fiscal or labour policy passes in time, because of gridlock and firm influence.
- China converts AI into manufacturing dominance quickly enough to change the US calculus.

## Tensions and weak points
- **Thin scenario, rich findings.** The extract describes this world only through the synopsis in Table 1. Every other dynamic, from lost entry rungs to the fiscal squeeze, is pooled across both scenarios, and no finding is attributed to Economic Crisis games specifically. The scores lean on that pooled material.
- **Record growth and a debt spiral together.** Record GDP growth would normally lift revenue; the scenario rests on growth that accrues to capital the tax system does not reach. The extract states this mechanism, but gives no figures.
- **Robotics.** US hardware automation is blocked, yet the pooled findings have displacement moving to blue-collar work through robotics. In this scenario that shift may be China's, not America's.
- **The horizon has nearly passed.** The scenario sets AGI between late 2026 and mid-2027; at the time of assessment (November 2026) nothing like it has been deployed.
- **Neighbours:** `intelligence-curse`, `imf-runaway` and `ai-2027` on the labour map; `citrini` and `intelligence-curse` on the fiscal map. It is closest to `citrini` in spirit: a fast displacement shock meeting a tax system and a Congress that cannot respond. The sibling `rand-ip-business-civil` sits slightly further towards replacement and weaker finances.

## Scoring notes
Scores are 2/–/1/2/–. Work is 2: the extract names the losers (junior and early-career workers in entry-level knowledge work, law, software and finance) and a generation without career pathways; it stops short of 1 because the scenario's own displacement is of knowledge workers, with US hardware automation blocked. Safety net is 1: income-tax revenue falls as needs rise, a debt spiral looms, and government may be unable to fund "basic services, let alone an expanded safety net". Government is 2: gridlock, radicalisation, dependence on lab self-reporting and the risk of firm capture, with authoritarian responses a warning rather than an outcome. Essentials and time are not addressed: falling consumer spending says nothing about prices, and the shorter week and meaning questions are player proposals.

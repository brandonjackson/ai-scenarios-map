# Economic Crisis (Infinite Potential) — summary

*RAND Research Report RR-A4767-4, "Infinite Potential—Insights on Economic and Societal Impacts Across Two Scenarios" (2026), by Anton Shenk, Richard S. Girven, Gregory Smith and Tobias Sytsma. It is an after-action report on 26 two-turn "Day After AGI" tabletop games played between April 2025 and May 2026, seven of them on this scenario. The report pools player findings across this scenario and its sibling, Business and Civil Society, and describes this world itself only in a short synopsis (p. 4). Most of what follows "applies across the set". The many policies players recommended are not part of the scenario. Read from the report's full text; see `extract.md`.*

**The scenario.** Between late 2026 and mid-2027 a leading US lab achieves AGI, which speeds up an employment crisis that has already begun. The result is "record gross domestic product (GDP) growth alongside mass knowledge-worker displacement, a looming debt spiral, and congressional gridlock". China wins manufacturing dominance with AI-run "lights-out factories", and its export controls on robotics inputs block US hardware automation. Unrest and radicalisation grow. Players must protect workers "using economic tools that are ill-suited for the new AI economy".

## Hard work pays
Broken for some. Entry-level knowledge work, legal research, software engineering and financial analysis are displaced fast, and junior workers are most exposed. AI removes "the bottom rungs of career ladders, resulting in a generation without clear pathways into professional life". Players concluded the losses may be permanent rather than cyclical, and that moving workers into trades is "at best a temporary solution" once robotics arrives.

## Affordable essentials
Not addressed. Consumer spending falls, but the report says nothing on the cost of housing, food, energy or care.

## Reliable safety net
Broken. Unemployment cuts income-tax revenue just as demand for support rises, while gains flow to capital owners and a few firms. The tax system is still built on payroll and income, and reform "takes years while the economic change takes months". With a debt spiral looming, government may be unable to fund "basic services, let alone an expanded safety net".

## Responsive government
Failing. Congress is gridlocked and radicalisation rises. Government depends on the labs' own reporting to know what their systems can do, lacks the authority to intervene forcefully in frontier firms, and faces firms whose power risks "capturing or undermining democratic governance". Once instability is acute, responses become "reactive, fragmented, and potentially authoritarian".

## Time for what matters
Not addressed. Players discussed shorter working weeks and asked "How can people find meaning in a post-labor society?", but as proposals, not as features of the scenario.

## Bottom line
A K-shaped AGI boom in which GDP grows while jobs and spending fall, the state lacks the money and the tools to respond, and US–China rivalry stops it slowing down.

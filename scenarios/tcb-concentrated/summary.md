# Concentrated Gains — summary

*The Conference Board, "AI & the Labor Force: Scenarios for Stakeholders" (15 September 2026), a US report for business leaders, policymakers and educators. Four scenarios, no probabilities, no named authors. Read from the full report text on the Conference Board's publication page.*

**The scenario.** "AI raises productivity unevenly, benefiting certain industries and occupations." Aggregate employment disruption is limited or moderate, but the firms, roles and workers best placed to capture the gains pull ahead, and an "AI underclass" of excluded workers, firms and communities may form.

## Hard work pays
Broken for some. About 48% of tasks in STEM fields have high AI exposure against 23% in manual trades and production, so trades may gain relatively while exposed office work stalls. The report points to Phoenix, which shed nearly 24,000 customer-service jobs between 2021 and 2025, as the kind of place at risk; it does not attribute those losses to AI.

## Affordable essentials
Not addressed.

## Reliable safety net
Patchy. The report suggests targeted help, such as Trade Adjustment Assistance-style support or benefits extended by occupation or region, rather than a general expansion.

## Responsive government
Strained by geography. Thirty of 195 US metro areas account for nearly 70% of AI-skill job postings, so gains and political voice concentrate in a few places.

## Time for what matters
Not addressed.

## Bottom line
Not a jobs crisis but an inequality one: AI widens the gap between the places and people who can use it and those who can't.

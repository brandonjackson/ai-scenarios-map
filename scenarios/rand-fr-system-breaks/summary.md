# System Breaks — summary

*RAND Corporation, "Federal Revenue When AI Replaces Labor: An Examination of Economic Scenarios with Highly Capable Artificial Intelligence" (RR-A4980-1), by Carter C. Price and Akshaya Suresh; catalogued August 2026, revision 2.0 dated 8 May 2026 (a working paper version appeared in August 2025). A US federal-revenue study built on a 2×2 of AI pricing (monopolistic vs at cost) and new work for humans (new tasks vs none). Each of the four "extreme cases" is examined twice: a long-run case in which essentially all existing jobs are displaced, and a 2025–2035 simulation in which 10% of the workforce is displaced. AI is treated as a pure substitute for labour with no productivity gains. Read from the full report text; see `extract.md`.*

**The scenario.** AI at cost, no new tasks for humans. "Essentially, automation is so cheap and productive that human labor cannot compete, and the value of most types of capital has declined because automated systems are widely distributed and accessible." Labour income disappears and capital income shrinks because widely accessible AI earns no substantive profit. "In this case, the economy fails to work as it has historically, and federal revenue may collapse": 83% of federal tax revenue is directly vulnerable, and no level of productivity growth restores it. In the simulation, participation falls permanently, core inflation runs at −2%, and corporate income tax falls to 0.7% of GDP by 2035.

## Hard work pays
Broken. Human labour "cannot compete", labour income goes away and no new tasks emerge.

## Affordable essentials
Broken for many. Consumer prices fall (−2% a year in the simulation) because AI producing goods and services is "owned by the population at large". But with both wage and capital income gone, people without transfer payments struggle to buy goods from non-automated industries.

## Reliable safety net
Broken. Transfers become what separates people from being unable to buy non-automated goods. The model's assumption that they sustain 60% of prior consumption "may not be the case", the authors say, "particularly in scenario 4". The revenue that would fund them is the most exposed of the four scenarios. A universal income would worsen the federal position if federally funded.

## Responsive government
Not addressed. The universal-income and price-stability options sit outside the scenario.

## Time for what matters
Not addressed.

## Bottom line
The corner where the existing fiscal model stops working: abundance at near-zero prices, but almost no taxable income, wages or profits, to pay for anything.

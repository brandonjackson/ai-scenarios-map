# System Breaks — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Off.** The breakdown follows from cheap AI and absent new work, with no deliberate response. The simulation assumes no fiscal response, and the authors themselves doubt its 60% transfer assumption here. A universal income or asset programme, price stabilisation and AI excise taxes are offered outside the scenario. The authors note that the existing tax base offers no revenue-neutral path at all.

## Big assumptions
1. **Capability.** Automation "so cheap and productive that human labor cannot compete", across intellectual and physical work.
2. **Adoption.** Full diffusion, no timeline. In the simulation, a tenth of the workforce leaves the labour force permanently in 2025.
3. **Markets.** AI is widely distributed and accessible at or near cost, so neither wages nor profits survive and most capital loses value. No new tasks emerge.
4. **Politics.** No fiscal or monetary response beyond rate cuts. The tax code stays built on labour and corporate income.

## What must be true for this to occur
- Human labour has essentially no market value in any task.
- At-cost AI (open-weight or priced below incumbents) dominates every market, leaving no rents to tax.
- Nothing replaces wages and profits as a source of household income or tax revenue.
- Deflation persists for a decade without a policy response.

## Tensions and weak points
- AI is "owned by the population at large" and prices fall, yet households lack the income to buy non-automated goods. Who owns the AI, and how that ownership pays anyone, is left open. That gap is what pins `x_labor` near the middle rather than on the diffuse side.
- Simulated personal income tax ends slightly *above* baseline as a share of GDP (14.5% against 14.0%) despite permanent job loss, because nominal GDP shrinks. The long-run "83% vulnerable" case carries the scenario, not the simulation.
- The authors call all four scenarios extremes; this is the one furthest from current conditions, needing both full replacement and full commoditisation.
- No close neighbours: it sits further into replacement and weak public finances than any other scenario on the map. Nearest are `agi-end-employment` (but concentrated) on labour, and `intelligence-curse` and `rand-fr-hyperconcentration` on the fiscal axes.

## Scoring notes
Scores are 1/2/1/–/–. Work is 1: human labour cannot compete and no new tasks emerge. Essentials is 2: prices fall, which separates it from a 1, but people without transfers struggle to buy non-automated goods. Safety net is 1: transfers are the only remaining income, the authors doubt they can sustain consumption "particularly in scenario 4", and 83% of revenue is directly vulnerable. Government and time are not addressed. Map: `x_labor` +0.1 balances the stated absence of any concentrated winner against the stated loss of both wage and capital income; `y_fiscal` −0.9 is the weakest of the four.

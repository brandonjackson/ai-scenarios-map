# Transformation Economy — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Off.** US policy influence over the two leading firms and existing limits on some automation are part of the world, but new answers to job and tax-base losses are outside the scope. The harms are what happens without reform.

## Big assumptions
1. **Remote-work-level AI from 2029.** Coordinated agents plan and execute whole projects with people setting direction.
2. **Extreme concentration.** Capital, compute, data and distribution put the frontier in two US firms.
3. **Firms choose substitution.** Cheap models and AI-native rivals push firms to take legal risks and automate.
4. **Demand feedback bites.** Lost wages cut spending enough to offset most growth.
5. **The UK is a price-taker.** Weak in the value chain and short of compute, it loses profits overseas.

## What must be true for this to occur
- Two firms pull clear of every rival, including open weights, and stay there.
- Agents become reliable enough to run large firms with minimal staff.
- UK energy prices and planning keep domestic compute scarce.
- No new revenue source replaces falling income-tax receipts.

## Tensions and weak points
- The demand feedback is the key mechanism and one many economic models leave out, which makes this harsher than forecasts with similar capability.
- Healthcare improves while incomes fall, so essentials move both ways.
- Physical and in-person work is relatively stable at first, and the 2040 outlook has workers moving into lower-paid care, construction and hospitality: that could stabilise employment while deepening inequality.
- It sits furthest towards concentration in the set (x −0.75), as the framework's distribution score of 1 implies, near `toloui-titans`, `cfg-central` and `imf-runaway`. `gos-take-off` is the more extreme version on replacement.

## Scoring notes
Draft scores are 2/3/2/2/–. Work is 2 rather than 1 because experts and asset owners do well: the harm is severe but concentrated on displaced workers and entrants. Essentials is 3, balancing better healthcare against falling purchasing power. The safety net is 2 because welfare spending rises as the labour tax base shrinks, with no reform.

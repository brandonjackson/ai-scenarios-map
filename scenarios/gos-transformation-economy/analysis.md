# Transformation Economy — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Policy off by design.** The harms (collapse of entry-level hiring, a fiscal squeeze, profits leaking abroad) are what happens without UK intervention. GO-Science says that without intervention unemployment "will continue to rise".

## Big assumptions
1. **Remote-work-level AI by 2029.** Systems can do most tasks a remote worker could.
2. **Extreme concentration.** Two US companies dominate inputs and routes to market, a winner-takes-all market.
3. **Firms choose substitution.** Most automate rather than expand output.
4. **Demand feedback bites.** Lost wages cut spending enough to cancel most of the growth.
5. **The UK is a price-taker.** Weak in the value chain, it loses profits and tax base overseas.

## What must be true for this to occur
- Two firms pull clear of all rivals, including open-weight models, and keep that lead.
- Agentic systems become reliable enough to run whole projects with minimal staff.
- Firms restructure fast: from 2029 shifts are "large and sudden."
- No new UK revenue source replaces falling income-tax receipts.

## Tensions and weak points
- The demand feedback is the key mechanism and the one most economic models leave out (the Anthropic Institute model, for example, explicitly omits it). It makes this scenario harsher than many forecasts with similar capability.
- Healthcare gets better even as the economy stalls, so essentials improve and worsen at once.
- In the 2040 outlook labour markets adjust, with displaced workers moving into care, hospitality and construction at much lower wages. This sets up `gos-take-off` as the more extreme version and `wf-high-substitution` as a close relative.

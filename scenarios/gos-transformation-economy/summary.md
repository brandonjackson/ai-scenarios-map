# Transformation Economy — summary

*UK Government Office for Science, with AISI and DSIT, "AI Scenarios 2030: Helping policymakers plan for the future of AI" (15 June 2026)*

**The scenario.** From 2029 AI can do most remote work, and productivity "dramatically increases". But the leading systems come from "2 US companies, which command unprecedented market share with significant control over critical AI inputs and downstream routes to market." The UK sits weakly in the AI value chain, so profits in financial and professional services "leak overseas".

## Hard work pays
Broken for many. "Large and sudden shifts in the workforce": most firms automate roles that can be done remotely, and large enterprises run with minimal staff. "Only half of those replaced find new employment, with many forced to accept wage reductions and less secure work." Entry-level recruitment collapses while demand for experienced experts holds, removing entry points for the young. Humans are increasingly "out of the loop", overseeing teams of agents and overriding them less and less.

## Affordable essentials
Mixed. AI-driven treatments and diagnostics improve healthcare. But "heightened unemployment and falling wages have reduced consumer demand, almost entirely counteracting the expected economic growth benefits."

## Reliable safety net
Under severe strain. "Declining labour income tax revenues and rising welfare expenditures are creating an increasingly challenging fiscal environment for government", with unemployment at recession levels.

## Responsive government
Divided. "A minority of highly skilled professionals and asset owners monopolise gains – echoing the dynamics of early industrialisation." Public opinion splits between winners and the unemployed; there are "large anti-AI protests", and AI is "now the decisive political issue." The UK loses strategic autonomy through dependence on foreign technology.

## Time for what matters
Worse. Unemployment and insecure work, not leisure.

## Bottom line
GO-Science's sharpest warning about the social contract. It expects a two-tier economy of a small asset-owning elite and a low-wage majority, and says this "may require fundamental change from the current social contract whereby humans contribute their cognitive and physical labour in exchange for resources."

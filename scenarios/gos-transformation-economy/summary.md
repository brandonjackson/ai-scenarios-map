# Transformation Economy — summary

*UK Government Office for Science, "AI Scenarios 2030: Helping policymakers plan for the future of AI" (15 June 2026). One of five exploratory scenarios built on six uncertainties; Transformation Economy pairs mid-to-high capability with the most concentrated access, fast adoption and high displacement. The authors say the scenarios "are not predictions". Read from the full GOV.UK report, including the outlook to 2040 and the cross-cutting findings; see `extract.md`.*

**The scenario.** From 2029 AI automates most remote work, supports major scientific advances and greatly raises productivity. Two US companies dominate the frontier, and open weights lag about 18 months. The UK relies on US systems and imported European compute, and profits from its financial and professional services flow overseas. Large-scale substitution pushes unemployment to recession levels, and lost wages cut demand enough to offset nearly all the expected growth.

## Hard work pays
Broken for many. Most firms automate remote roles and large enterprises run with minimal staff. About half of displaced people are re-employed, often at lower pay and with less security. Entry-level hiring collapses while experts stay in demand, removing young people's entry points. The 2040 outlook says the exchange of labour for resources may need a fundamental rethink.

## Affordable essentials
Mixed. AI-assisted diagnosis, treatment and personalised care improve healthcare, but falling wages and unemployment cut purchasing power.

## Reliable safety net
Under strain. "Declining labour income tax revenues and rising welfare expenditures are creating an increasingly challenging fiscal environment for government." The report warns of further deterioration without reform but describes no new benefit.

## Responsive government
Divided and less sovereign. Large protests and anti-AI campaigns make AI a decisive political issue. Human sign-off persists but people intervene less, and technology-importing countries lose autonomy and leverage.

## Time for what matters
Not addressed.

## Bottom line
The sharpest warning in the set for the social contract: a small share of highly skilled workers and asset owners captures most gains, and "In the absence of economic reform, continued economic growth could co-exist with rising inequality, deflationary pressures and falling tax revenues."

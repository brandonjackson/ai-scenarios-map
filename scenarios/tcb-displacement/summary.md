# Significant Displacement — summary

*The Conference Board, "AI & the Labor Force: Scenarios for Stakeholders" (15 September 2026), a US report for business leaders, policymakers and educators. Four scenarios, no probabilities, no named authors. Read from the full report text on the Conference Board's publication page; see `extract.md`.*

**The scenario.** AI sharply reduces labour demand across many sectors. Losses start in clerical, production, transport and food-preparation work, then reach professional fields such as finance, law and some medical services, many of which require advanced degrees. "Increased unemployment rates would be persistent and paired with higher underemployment and lower labor force participation rates." A relatively small number of firms and workers capture the gains. The press release calls this "massive displacement".

## Hard work pays
Broken for many. Displaced workers who find new jobs face large earnings losses, and educated workers lose the protection a degree used to give.

## Affordable essentials
Not addressed.

## Reliable safety net
Overwhelmed. Only about 28% of laid-off workers collect unemployment insurance, replacing about 40% of weekly earnings, and pandemic-era systems saw payment errors double and $100–135 billion in fraud. Prolonged job loss raises reliance on Social Security, Medicaid and SNAP and "could also worsen the fiscal crisis facing Social Security". Widespread unemployment, especially among higher earners, would hit federal, state and local tax bases hard: federal receipts fell nearly 17% in the first year of the Great Recession.

## Responsive government
Under pressure. The report warns of substantial "social and political turmoil" and of benefit agencies without the capacity to cope. It floats long-term wage subsidies, a universal basic income or a public dividend from AI profits as novel, costly options.

## Time for what matters
Worse, indirectly: prolonged job loss is linked to poorer health and family instability.

## Bottom line
The crisis case, seen through the US benefits system: the tax base shrinks just as claims surge, and current institutions aren't built for it.

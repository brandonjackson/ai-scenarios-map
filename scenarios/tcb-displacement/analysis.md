# Significant Displacement — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Off.** The scenario shows what happens if today's US systems face the shock unprepared. The report's recommendations (surge planning, modernised UI, flexible training money, early-warning data) are what it wants done now.

## Big assumptions
1. **Broad capability.** AI can replace work across clerical, manual and professional occupations.
2. **Persistent, not cyclical, unemployment.** New jobs do not appear fast enough.
3. **Concentrated gains.** A small number of firms and workers capture the productivity growth.

## What must be true for this to occur
- AI (and robotics, for production and transport work) becomes good enough to replace rather than assist.
- Firms choose cost-cutting over expansion.
- Demand for new kinds of work fails to absorb displaced workers.

## Tensions and weak points
- The report cites Anthropic's CEO predicting 10–20% unemployment by 2030 but notes many experts dispute it; it sets no timeframe of its own.
- Its value is the institutional detail on US unemployment insurance, benefit capacity and fiscal exposure, which complements `wef-displacement` and `anthropic-extreme`.
- Named "Massive displacement" in the press release and "Significant displacement" in the report; the map uses the report's name.

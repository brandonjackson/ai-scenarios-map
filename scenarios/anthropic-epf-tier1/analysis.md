# Baseline Economy — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Off: the policies are recommendations for a labour market that churns anyway.** The extract marks the stance "On" because the whole document prescribes policy. But it also says plainly that "the tiers set out what governments should do, not what they do inside a story". The tier is defined by an unemployment rate, about 5% with churn, that arises from AI adoption whatever policy does. Capital accounts holding AI equity, wage insurance, licensing reform, retention credits, training grants and job matching are all proposed, not assumed. The only policies inside the scenario are existing ones: birth-seeded federal accounts (index funds only) and state UI on legacy systems. These are not deliberate new policy, so no links are recorded.

## Big assumptions
1. **Capability:** AI automates "tasks across occupations simultaneously". Recommendations are calibrated to capabilities that exist now or will within about a year.
2. **Adoption:** broad enough to raise churn and compress skill premiums, but not enough to raise headline unemployment.
3. **Markets:** displaced workers find new jobs, often at lower pay. AI is "fueling growth".
4. **Politics:** none needed. The scenario is the status quo with faster churn.

## What must be true for this to occur
- Job creation keeps pace with task automation in aggregate, so unemployment stays near 5%.
- Skill compression and churn happen below the headline numbers. Weaker entry-level employment growth in exposed occupations is the evidence cited.
- No large shock tests the UI system.

## Tensions and weak points
- The tier is barely described as a world. The extract says "most of the section is policy", so the map position and scores rest on two sentences on p. 7 and the cohorts named on p. 8.
- The catalogue description names capital accounts, wage insurance and retraining "help[ing] workers move". Under a policy-off reading those are what the authors want, not what happens.
- It is a separate source from the Anthropic Institute's `anthropic-modest`/`-substantial`/`-extreme`, and the tiers do not map onto them. The Tier 1 position sits between Modest and Substantial. Nearest neighbours: `tcb-uneven`, `anthropic-substantial` and `ilo`.
- Its counterpart in the DeepMind framework, `dm-policy-mild`, sits slightly more towards augmentation and diffuse gains. That essay frames mild disruption as familiar augmentation and assumes the stabilizers are in place.

## Scoring notes
Scores are 3/–/3*/–/–. Work is 3: unemployment stays near 5%, but the value of hard-won skills is "steadily compress[ed]" and some re-employed workers earn less. That is real erosion but within past experience, in line with `anthropic-substantial`. Safety net is 3, inferred: at about 5% unemployment the existing system is not under the "large labor market shock" the authors say it is unprepared for, so it copes. Essentials, government and time are not addressed. The "work far less" hope and worker engagement apply across the framework, not to this tier. No headline rating, because only two promises are scored.

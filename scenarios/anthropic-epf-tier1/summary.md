# Baseline Economy — summary

*Tier 1 of Anthropic's "Economic Policy Framework" ("A policy framework for AI's impact on work"), a 14-page US policy paper published in June 2026 with a web summary, "Policy on the AI Exponential". Three tiers are set by the unemployment rate, and each is matched to the policies Anthropic would want ready. The tiers are contingencies for planning, not forecasts. This is a separate publication from the Anthropic Institute's modelled scenarios (`anthropic-modest`, `-substantial`, `-extreme`). Read from the full PDF and web summary; see `extract.md`.*

**The scenario.** Unemployment is about 5% and "the headline numbers look normal. But underneath, the labor market may be churning faster than workers can adapt." AI "is automating tasks across occupations simultaneously, steadily compressing the value of skills that workers spent years acquiring". Displaced workers may take lower-paid jobs. Entry-level workers in the most AI-exposed occupations have already seen weaker employment growth. The source describes this world only briefly. Most of the section sets out policy: universal capital accounts that can hold AI-company equity, wage insurance, licensing reform, retention tax credits, sectoral training and job-matching platforms. These are recommendations, not part of the scenario.

## Hard work pays
Stable, with erosion underneath. Jobs are still there, but years of acquired skill are worth less, and some re-employed workers earn less than before. The authors hold that "there is dignity in work" and that society should keep aiming for full employment. Nothing in the tier itself restores the value of lost skills.

## Affordable essentials
Not addressed. The only price effect mentioned is that occupational licensing "raise[s] prices without corresponding net welfare gains".

## Reliable safety net
Stable. At about 5% unemployment the existing system is not under strain. The framework warns that UI "is not sufficiently prepared for a large labor market shock", but this tier is not one. Workers who take lower-paid jobs get no help under current policy. Wage insurance, which would cover that gap, is only a recommendation.

## Responsive government
Not addressed.

## Time for what matters
Not addressed. Across the framework, the authors suggest AI may eventually let people "work far less". They do not apply this to Tier 1.

## Bottom line
Close to today. The numbers look normal while the value of skills quietly erodes. The tier is less a picture of the future than a reason to start capital accounts before they are needed.

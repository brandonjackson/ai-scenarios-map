# Gradual Disempowerment — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Off; policy only a stopgap.** Market and governance incentives drive the substitution, and the scenario assumes regulators lag adoption while existing market and democratic mechanisms carry on with weakening effect. The proposals (monitor AI's share and authority, restrict excessive delegation, progressive taxes on AI revenue or subsidies for human participation, more responsive democracy, alignment across institutions) are offered for discussion, not assumed.

## Big assumptions
1. **Capability improves gradually but broadly.** AI becomes competitive in labour, decisions, culture and companionship, scaling at compute cost, without a single jump.
2. **Adoption is forced by competition.** Firms and states that keep humans in the loop lose to those that delegate.
3. **Markets follow AI demand.** Activity shifts towards what AI-controlled purchasers want, so GDP can grow while its link to human welfare weakens.
4. **Politics depends on economic need.** Institutions serve people because they need them; once they don't, explicit levers like votes are not enough.

## What must be true for this to occur
- AI becomes cheaper than people across most cognitive functions.
- No international coordination limits delegation or taxes AI revenue.
- Measurement lags, so the drift is not seen until it is hard to reverse.
- Ownership of capital or transfers do not translate into control over AI-run decisions.

## Tensions and weak points
- **Hard to falsify:** a drift without a crisis is by design hard to see.
- **Ownership:** the relative variant keeps human incomes; whether that ownership preserves influence is the paper's most debatable step.
- **Map placement:** the old "Moderate REP" placement was light for a paper whose endpoint is a labour share near zero. It moves to "Strong REP" (`y_labor` 0.5) and slightly lower on good jobs (`x_fiscal` −0.55). Concentration (`x_labor` −0.3) means power shifting to AI-run systems more than to a few owners, hence less extreme than `intelligence-curse`.
- Undated, so likelihood rests on direction rather than timing.
- Close to `intelligence-curse` (the rentier argument taken further), `dsit-rapid` and Windfall's *Disempowered Abundance*, which is close to its relative variant.

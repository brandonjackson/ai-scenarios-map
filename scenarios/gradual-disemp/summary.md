# Gradual Disempowerment — summary

*Jan Kulveit, Raymond Douglas, Nora Ammann, Deger Turan, David Krueger & David Duvenaud, January 2025 (arXiv 2501.16946)*

**The scenario.** No robot uprising and no sudden takeoff. "Even an incremental increase in AI capabilities, without any coordinated power-seeking, poses a substantial risk of eventual human disempowerment." The economy, culture and the state have stayed broadly aligned with human interests through *explicit* levers (votes, consumer choice) and, more importantly, through *implicit* dependence on human labour and cognition. As AI becomes the more competitive option in "almost all societal functions, such as economic labor, decision making, artistic creation, and even companionship," that dependence disappears and the systems drift away from human preferences. Each system reinforces the drift in the others. The end state could be "effectively irreversible".

## Hard work pays
Fades away. Competitive pressure, scalability asymmetries and regulatory gaps (human labour carries minimum wages, payroll taxes and safety rules; AI carries almost none) push firms to cede control to AI. "Anticipatory disinvestment" means people stop building skills in areas expected to be automated, which speeds the shift. "Human labor share of GDP gradually tends toward zero."

## Affordable essentials
Two versions. In **relative disempowerment**, people stay prosperous through capital ownership, redistribution or UBI, but lose influence while the economy shifts toward AI-centred activity. In **absolute disempowerment**, AI outcompetes humans for land, energy and materials, and "necessities [become] increasingly unaffordable" even in a wealthy economy. Cheap entertainment and virtual worlds may be abundant.

## Reliable safety net
Possible, but not secure. Redistribution may keep people comfortable, but those who depend on it have less leverage to defend it.

## Responsive government
The core concern. Modern states built mass education and political participation partly *because* they depended on their citizens. "States funded mainly by taxes on AI profits instead of their citizens' labor will have little incentive to ensure citizens' representation", a rentier-state dynamic. Meanwhile AI gives states unprecedented influence over culture and behaviour, and replaces the human officials and security forces whose ability to refuse orders has checked abuse.

## Time for what matters
Hollowed out. AI companions, AI-generated culture and personalised content can displace human relationships and the shared production of meaning.

## Bottom line
A slow drift in which every promise can look intact by traditional metrics while people lose the ability to shape outcomes. The authors stress that "no one has a concrete plausible plan for stopping" it.

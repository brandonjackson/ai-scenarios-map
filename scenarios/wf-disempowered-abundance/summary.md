# Disempowered Abundance — summary

*Windfall Trust Radar, 2026 · source: map description and draft scores (no public source document yet; add one to this folder)*

**The scenario.** AI runs much of the economy and prices fall. A handful of companies own most production. Life feels comfortable and material needs are largely met, but people lose their bargaining power as workers and, gradually, as citizens. Governments funded by AI firms have less reason to listen. With no visible crisis, power drifts away unnoticed.

## Hard work pays
Broken for some. AI runs most production, so human labour has little economic weight. Some work remains, but effort no longer gives people leverage: they can't threaten to withhold labour that nobody needs.

## Affordable essentials
Improving. This is the scenario's distinctive feature. Prices fall as AI runs much of the economy and essentials become broadly affordable. It is the one promise that does well.

## Reliable safety net
Stable, but conditional. Support holds and life is comfortable, but it depends on the goodwill of a few owners and on AI-funded government revenue. A net people have no power to defend isn't really reliable.

## Responsive government
Broken for all. If AI firms fund the state, rather than taxpaying workers, government's incentive to respond to citizens weakens. This is the rentier-state problem (see Drago & Laine's *Intelligence Curse* and Kulveit et al.'s *Gradual Disempowerment*). Elections continue but matter less. The weakest-link rule exists for exactly this case.

## Time for what matters
Stable. Comfort leaves room for life outside work, but people have no say in how it is shaped. Time is abundant; agency isn't.

## Bottom line
A future that passes every material test and fails the democratic one. The weakest-link rule caps it low: material plenty can't make up for citizens losing their say.

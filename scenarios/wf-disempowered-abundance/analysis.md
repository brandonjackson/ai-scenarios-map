# Disempowered Abundance — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Partly policy on, but the wrong policy.** Someone has evidently set up mechanisms that keep people comfortable (transfers, cheap services, a functioning safety net), so this isn't a pure laissez-faire world. What is off is policy to keep power distributed: ownership stays concentrated, and the state's revenue comes from AI firms rather than citizens. Material redistribution happened without political redistribution.

## Big assumptions
1. **AI production is highly productive and cheap.** Prices fall enough that material comfort is widespread even without wages.
2. **Ownership concentrates.** A handful of firms own most production. That requires winner-take-all dynamics (Toloui's Copilot or Titan quadrants) rather than commoditisation.
3. **Owners share enough to keep people comfortable,** from goodwill, reputation, political calculation or a negotiated windfall-style settlement, but not enough to share control.
4. **The fiscal link between citizens and state is cut.** Governments are funded by AI firms, so taxpayers lose leverage. This is rentier-state theory applied to AI.
5. **No visible crisis.** Comfort removes the trigger for mobilisation, so power drifts gradually.

## What must be true for this to occur
- AI replaces most economically decisive labour, so strikes and labour scarcity no longer give workers leverage.
- AI firms' profits are big enough to fund both the state and a comfortable standard of living.
- Elections and institutions survive in form but weaken in substance (regulatory capture, information dominance, dependency).
- No counter-movement for public or broad-based ownership (sovereign wealth funds, universal basic capital, data dividends) succeeds while there is still leverage.

## Tensions and weak points
- Stability is questionable. Comfort that depends on the goodwill of a few owners can be withdrawn. The scenario assumes owners keep providing it, but the Intelligence Curse argues incentives to invest in people decline once people aren't needed.
- It shows a blind spot in GDP and consumption measures: by most economic indicators this looks like success.
- It is the clearest case for the goal's weakest-link rule. Without it, the average of these scores (2.6) would hide a broken government promise.

## Scoring notes
Draft scores (2/4/3/1/3) fit. One could argue the safety net is really a 2, since a net that depends on owners' goodwill is reliable only until it isn't.

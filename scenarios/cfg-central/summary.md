# Centralized Takeoff — summary

*Centre for Future Generations, "Advanced AI: Possible Futures", July 2025 · fast progress, centralised development (covers CFG's Big AI, Arms Race and Diplomacy scenarios)*

**The scenario.** AI agents become highly capable and AI starts speeding up its own development. Compute is the limiting factor and the US controls the chip supply chain, so a handful of actors dominate. CFG runs this one cell three ways. In **Big AI**, five firms control cloud, operating systems and distribution. In **Arms Race**, the US and China treat AI as the decisive security asset and private labs fall under Manhattan-Project-style state partnerships. In **Diplomacy**, a crisis (a model replicating itself out of control) triggers an IAEA-style international licensing regime. CFG's warning: "AI may centralize power on unprecedented scales."

## Hard work pays
Broken for many. Software engineering is hit first and entry-level roles vanish as AI writes production-ready code. In Arms Race, students drop computer science because the careers look gone. Those fluent in AI speed ahead, others fall behind: "connected by code, divided by everything else."

## Affordable essentials
At risk. Demand for inference compute pushes chip prices up and vertical integration raises switching costs. In "Silicon Blackmail", the worst Big AI ending, prosperity is "concentrated among shareholders and AI-native professionals." Only the Diplomacy ending ("Licensed Utopia") brings broad gains: AI-driven diagnostics, new medicines and materials.

## Reliable safety net
Ending-dependent. In Silicon Blackmail, communities hit by automation "receive minimal support." In "The Agent Economy", retraining and job guarantees emerge. In Licensed Utopia, a 25% AI tax and lump-sum fees pay for redistribution while growth reaches 7% a year.

## Responsive government
The crux. In Big AI, governments depend on, and are pressured by, a few firms; the US even threatens Europe with withdrawal. In Arms Race, private power is absorbed by the national-security state and the "Hot War" ending sees China invade Taiwan. Only Diplomacy produces effective collective governance, and its "Unstable Pause" ending shows how fragile that is.

## Time for what matters
Mostly squeezed, except in Diplomacy, where growing leisure is accepted and "AI-refusers" emerge as a lifestyle movement.

## Bottom line
Fast, centralised AI puts the social contract in the hands of whoever controls the compute. Only deliberate international coordination turns that concentration into shared prosperity.

# Big AI / Arms Race — summary

*Centre for Future Generations, "Advanced AI: Possible Futures" (10 July 2025), by Bengüsu Özcan, Daan Juijn, Jakob Graabak and Sam Bogerd. The study crosses the speed of capability progress with how concentrated frontier development is. This entry combines two of its five core narratives, both fast and centralised: Big AI (endings "The Agent Economy" and "Silicon Blackmail") and Arms Race ("Hot War" and "Multipolar World"). The coordinated fast path is [Diplomacy: Licensed Utopia](../cfg-diplomacy/summary.md). Read from the publisher's full scenario text and companion material; the website is used for chronology, and some Big AI details differ in the narrative PDF. See `extract.md`.*

**The scenario.** In **Big AI**, US agents become reliable enough for real workflows, and chips, distribution, data and lock-in consolidate control among a few US providers: "The race is no longer just about algorithms—it's about distribution, integration, and user data." In the Agent Economy, European and community open models keep room to compete and GDP growth rises modestly. In Silicon Blackmail, integrated platforms threaten to withdraw services to pressure regulators, US growth gains a point and unemployment rises. In **Arms Race**, the US and China move frontier work into secret state-backed programmes, export controls tighten and limits break down over verification and Taiwan, ending either in open conflict or a fragile truce that freezes commercial AI.

## Hard work pays
Broken for some. People skilled in AI pull away and orchestrating agents becomes a valued skill, but routine white-collar work faces substitution. Silicon Blackmail names software engineers, paralegals and digital-media workers, with falling computer-science enrolment and rising unemployment; in Arms Race entry-level software work goes first and layoffs spread.

## Affordable essentials
At risk. Clinical diagnostic support arrives where regulation permits, but rising inference demand pushes up chip prices and firms trade lock-in or data for access. In Silicon Blackmail Europe struggles to maintain digital infrastructure and essential services when major US providers leave.

## Reliable safety net
Broken for some. The Agent Economy has job-guarantee pilots and agent levies to fund retraining. Elsewhere, prosperity accrues to "shareholders, executives and AI-native workers" while automation-hit communities fall behind, and Arms Race redirects public money to data centres and chips.

## Responsive government
Broken. Big AI platforms lobby governments, lock in users and can withdraw essential services to press demands; displaced communities protest. Arms Race puts secrecy first, protesters gain little from demands for transparency, and the Multipolar truce depends on disputed verification and outside monitors.

## Time for what matters
Broken for some. Big AI raises emotional dependency and social withdrawal among some young people. In Multipolar World many workers put careers aside and spend more time with family, out of uncertainty rather than choice.

## Bottom line
Fast, centralised AI puts the social contract in the hands of whoever controls the compute, whether a few platforms or the national-security state. Only the Agent Economy ending leaves real room for smaller players and workers; Diplomacy is CFG's route to shared gains.

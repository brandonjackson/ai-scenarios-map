# Uneven Disruption — summary

*The Conference Board, "AI & the Labor Force: Scenarios for Stakeholders" (15 September 2026), a US report for business leaders, policymakers and educators. Four scenarios, no probabilities, no named authors. Read from the full report text on the Conference Board's publication page; see `extract.md`.*

**The scenario.** "AI displaces workers in certain occupations while supporting job growth in others." Some sectors and roles lose heavily, but growth in AI-using roles may offset them and aggregate employment might even rise. The report calls it "less severe—though still disruptive".

## Hard work pays
Broken for the displaced. Past evidence is grim: of workers displaced by trade, about 60% were re-employed after four years and those who were earned only slightly more than half their old pay. Of long-tenured workers displaced in 2021–2023, about 16% were still unemployed and 18% had left the labour force in 2024, and about 40% of those re-employed earned less than before.

## Affordable essentials
Not addressed.

## Reliable safety net
Stretched in specific places. The report suggests Trade Adjustment Assistance-style support or extended benefits triggered by occupational or regional displacement rather than statewide unemployment rates.

## Responsive government
Not addressed directly; the report notes a generational divide inside firms between workers new to AI and those who grew up with it.

## Time for what matters
Not addressed.

## Bottom line
Normal technological churn at an unusual pace: the aggregate numbers may look fine while particular workers and communities absorb lasting losses.

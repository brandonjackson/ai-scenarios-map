# AI 2040: Plan A — summary

*Thomas Larsen, Romeo Dean, Brendan Halstead, Eli Lifland, Ryan Greenblatt and Daniel Kokotajlo (AI Futures Project), "AI 2040: Plan A", July 2026. A policy proposal stress-tested as a scenario: "Plan A is primarily a recommendation, not a prediction," and successful implementation is not the authors' baseline. Read from the complete PDF, including the 2030–40 chapters missing from the first website text and the economic appendix; see `extract.md`.*

**The scenario.** By 2028 white-collar work increasingly means supervising AI agents, and the presidential election turns on AI. In 2029–30 the US and China agree a slowdown with verifiable limits and Total Research Transparency, so dozens of firms across a Consortium of countries reach the frontier together. From 2032 robots and AI drive explosive growth (about 50% in 2032, around 100% a year on average to 2037), held back by tradable compute and robot permits whose auction proceeds fund a Citizen's Dividend. Models are paused at top-expert level from 2035, and around 2040 control is gradually handed to aligned superintelligence.

## Hard work pays
Broken as a route to living standards, but not as a source of meaning. Displaced white-collar workers first move into trusted roles and construction, until robots take that work too. The share of work done by AI and robots rises from about 20% in 2032 to 85% in 2035; US employment falls from 62% in 2027 to 26% in 2036 and 12% in 2040. The stigma of not working fades, and people contribute through politics, volunteering and giving.

## Affordable essentials
Improving. AI and robots cheapen food, medicine, durable goods and many services; malnutrition and homelessness become rare and many illnesses are cured. Land and positional goods rise in relative price, so historic city centres stay expensive, though housing can be cheap where planning lets robots build.

## Reliable safety net
Improving. Permit auctions raise about $50 trillion in FY2032 and $180 trillion in 2034, and a legally assigned share goes to equal shares in a Compute Dividend Corporation. The dividend is about $45,000 per adult in 2032 and $1 million by 2035 (real 2025 dollars), enough for almost all living costs. Part of the revenue goes abroad, from about $1,200 per adult in 2032 to $10,000 in 2035.

## Responsive government
Strengthened, with a long-run question. Public pressure puts AI on the ballot, and transparent AI advisers with good track records help voters judge policy and officials; the 2036 election has better-informed voters. Losing economic leverage threatens political leverage ("Your vote is your most important asset"), and by 2040 humanity hands more control to AIs: "There is no single moment where humanity relinquishes control."

## Time for what matters
Improving. Most people have more time for family, romance, learning, hobbies and volunteering, though some miss feeling useful.

## Bottom line
The most complete policy-on future on the map: a verified slowdown plus a rent-funded dividend keeps the material promises while work disappears, and leaves open how much say humans keep after 2040.

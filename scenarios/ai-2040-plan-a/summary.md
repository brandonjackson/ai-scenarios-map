# AI 2040: Plan A — summary

*Thomas Larsen, Romeo Dean, Brendan Halstead, Eli Lifland, Ryan Greenblatt & Daniel Kokotajlo (AI Futures Project), "AI 2040: Plan A" (July 2026)*

**The scenario.** The authors of AI 2027 set out what they would do instead. By 2027 superhuman coding agents are everywhere, and "most white-collar professions are seeing disruption like software engineering saw in 2026"; by 2028 data centres under construction cost twice the US military budget. Left alone, "in 2030, we would have fully automated AI R&D, leading to superintelligence by the end of the year." Instead the 2028 US election turns on AI, and in 2029 the US and China strike a deal: "total research transparency for AI R&D", verification, and multiple companies in multiple countries scaling "slowly and safely together." AI is scaled to top-expert level by 2035, paused there to keep human control, and taken to superintelligence under oversight around 2040.

## Hard work pays
Broken for many in the near term. White-collar work turns into managing AI agents, and AI companies systematically target one profession after another. The scenario says little about what happens to those whose work is automated.

## Affordable essentials
Not addressed.

## Reliable safety net
Not addressed. The slowdown buys a decade for institutions to adapt, but the scenario does not describe income support.

## Responsive government
The scenario's focus. Before the deal, "power is concentrating in the US, and in particular in the President plus a handful of tech CEOs", and Congress asks who will control the AIs: "Probably not us." The authors' central fear is "an unprecedented concentration of power", where "a tiny group of people, or possibly just a single individual" controls the superintelligences. Public pressure ("having put humanity on this path, the AI companies find it acceptable. But most people don't") drives both parties to back a slowdown, and the international deal spreads control across countries and companies.

## Time for what matters
Not addressed.

## Bottom line
A governance blueprint rather than an economic forecast: it buys time and spreads control, but leaves the social contract for others to design.

# AI 2040: Plan A — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Strongly on.** The scenario is a policy proposal: an international US–China agreement with total R&D transparency, verification, coordinated scaling and a deliberate pause at expert level. The authors present four other plans for comparison: B (fight China), C (burn the lead), D (race to superintelligence) and S (shut it all down).

## Big assumptions
1. **Very fast capability.** White-collar professions are disrupted by 2027–28 and superintelligence would follow in 2030 without a deal.
2. **Politics responds in time.** AI dominates the 2028 election and both candidates back a slowdown.
3. **China agrees and verification works.** Both sides accept total transparency and mutually assured destruction of compute deters cheating.
4. **Coordinated scaling is stable** for a decade, across companies and countries.

## What must be true for this to occur
- Public alarm becomes strong enough to override competitive and commercial pressure.
- The US and China trust a verification regime enough to slow down together.
- Labs accept full transparency over their research.

## Tensions and weak points
- It has the same very fast timeline as `ai-2027`, which its authors have since lengthened. Plan A itself is written as a recommendation, not a forecast.
- It is the policy-on counterpart to `ai-2027` (race) and a successor to `ai-2027-slowdown`. Unlike the Slowdown ending, it spreads control across countries and firms rather than handing it to one committee.
- The economic side is thin. Whether the slowdown protects workers depends on how far automation has gone by 2029, which is already far in the scenario.

## Scoring notes
Draft scores are 2/3/3/3/3. Work is 2 because white-collar disruption arrives before the deal. The other promises are 3 because the scenario does not describe them; government is 3, balancing the deal's spread of control against concentration around the President and a few CEOs.

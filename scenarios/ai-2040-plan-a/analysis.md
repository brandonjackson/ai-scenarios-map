# AI 2040: Plan A — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Strongly on.** Multilateral coordination and redistribution are the scenario: a 2027 US transparency law, a US–China slowdown agreement with verification and Total Research Transparency, safety cases before progress, industrial expansion confined to monitored special zones and capped by tradable compute and robot permits, a Compute Dividend Corporation funded from permit proceeds, revenue shared abroad, and a pause at top-expert level from 2035 to 2040. The Foreword contrasts it with Plans B, C, D and S.

## Big assumptions
1. **Capability.** Very fast: agents can do almost any desktop task by 2027, and without a deal AI R&D would be fully automated with superintelligence in 2030. AI and robots can do about 95% of cognitive work by 2035 and physical work by 2036.
2. **Adoption.** Rapid: buyers spend about $10 billion a month on agents in 2027, and reproducible robot labour lets capital directly expand the workforce. Permits, not adoption frictions, set the pace (doubling about every six months).
3. **Markets.** Prices of most goods collapse while land and scarce human input rise; real interest rates around 100%; monetary policy must choose between large money creation and deflation.
4. **Politics.** Voters force AI onto the agenda, the US and China trust a verification regime, labs accept full transparency, and the US shares rents with its citizens and other countries.

## What must be true for this to occur
- Public alarm becomes strong enough to override competitive and commercial pressure in both the US and China.
- Verification and research transparency work well enough to hold a decade-long coordinated slowdown.
- Permit auctions capture rents on the modelled scale, since the old income- and payroll-tax base largely vanishes as employment falls and firms expense reinvestment.
- Governments keep the dividend share legally protected as labour loses its leverage.

## Tensions and weak points
- **Earlier entry was read from partial text.** The previous summary and analysis were built from the website's first chapters and found the economics "thin", scoring essentials, safety net and time at 3 as unaddressed. The complete PDF describes the dividend, prices and free time in detail, so the map point and scores move.
- **Map.** Gains move from −0.20 to 0.35 (equal dividend shares, open research and payments abroad, against a long upper tail of investor incomes); replacement from 0.50 to 0.65 (employment to 12% by 2040); good jobs from −0.30 to −0.55; public finances from 0.30 to 0.70 (permit revenue of tens to hundreds of trillions). Its nearest labour neighbour is `moores-law`, another capital-funded dividend; on the fiscal plot it sits beside `ai-2027-slowdown`, as a more deliberate version of the same rent-funded state.
- The transition is the gap: white-collar disruption arrives by 2028 but the dividend starts in 2032, and the extract says nothing about support in between. The worst-off may be those displaced before the deal, and people outside the Consortium, whose payments start far lower.
- The rentier logic of the Slowdown ending remains: nearly all public revenue comes from permits on AI and robots, and the 2040 handover to AIs is unresolved.
- Dollar figures are in real 2025 terms amid huge relative-price shifts, so a $1 million dividend is less comparable than it looks.

# Jobs Are Lost and New Jobs Emerge — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Off.** The budget results use current law and CBO's projection method. Existing unemployment insurance applies and Trade Adjustment Assistance has lapsed. Expanding UI, establishing wage insurance and expanding training are discussed as options with arguments for and against, not built into the scenario.

## Big assumptions
1. **Capability.** AI can do work across many occupations over time, from call centres to some legal work, adding half a point to annual TFP growth.
2. **Adoption.** Displacement rolls across occupations and places at a "moderate pace" as TFP rises, not in a sudden wave.
3. **Markets.** New jobs emerge, but skill mismatch leaves a 2% shortfall in workers. Remaining workers supply more hours or AI-boosted human capital, so GDP and the labour share are unchanged from Scenario #2, and all extra income goes to the top quintile.
4. **Politics.** No new worker-support policy is enacted for 30 years, despite displacement affecting many millions of people.

## What must be true for this to occur
- AI keeps reaching new occupations at a pace that displaces workers faster than they can retrain but slowly enough to avoid a recession.
- Other labour input fully offsets the lost work; otherwise GDP and the budget fare worse, as the authors' alternative version notes.
- Policymakers repeat the China-shock pattern of responding late.

## Tensions and weak points
- The debt path is borrowed from Scenario #2, not estimated. The authors say a more precise estimate would include higher means-tested spending on the unemployed and non-participants, so the fiscal position is a little better on the map than the scenario's own mechanism implies.
- The offset of lost labour by more hours from those still working is a strong assumption: it keeps GDP and the labour share unchanged while millions are out of work.
- Spreading displacement across places lowers regional spiral risk but "does not necessarily ease skill matching".
- On the labour map it sits near `turing-trap`, `anthropic-substantial` and `dsit-rapid`: moderate replacement with concentrated gains. Its fiscal position is unusual in pairing fewer good jobs with stronger finances, because the budget gain from top-heavy growth swamps the cost of the displaced.

## Scoring notes
Scores are 2/–/2/3*/–. Work is 2 because the displaced, about 3 million at any time, suffer lasting earnings losses and move to lower-paying jobs or out of work, while others are unaffected: severe harm for a group, not for all. Safety net is 2: unemployment insurance pays half of wages for 26 weeks, misses gig workers and is too short for occupational change, and TAA has lapsed, so the net is built for short job losses, not structural ones. Government is 3, inferred: losses spread nationwide lower the risk of regional spirals in local public finances, and the federal budget still improves, so institutions are not overwhelmed, though the authors warn the response may come late as after the China shock. Essentials and time are not addressed; the health harms of job loss are noted under work rather than time.

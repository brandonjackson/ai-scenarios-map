# Modest — summary

*Korinek, Jones, Sacher, Cotter & McCrory, "Economic Scenarios for Transformative AI", Anthropic Institute Working Paper 2026-02 (10 September 2026), with the online Econ Scenario Explorer*

**The scenario.** "AI has roughly the same kind of impact as the internet did." By 2030 AI affects only about 4% of the economy's tasks, partly because capabilities are modest and partly because diffusion is slow. Where it is used, half the affected work is automated and half augmented, and for every two tasks automated one new task appears for people. US GDP is 1.6% above its no-AI path, "an extra ten months of ordinary growth accumulated over four years."

## Hard work pays
Holds. Cognitive employment is 0.5% below its mid-2026 level and the average wage is 0.7% higher, with cognitive wages up 0.4%. Unemployment is 3.9% against a normal 3.8%: "only small labor market effects."

## Affordable essentials
Unchanged. Gains arrive gradually and within historical norms. The model has nothing specific on the cost of housing, food, energy or care.

## Reliable safety net
Not tested. Effects this small are well within what existing institutions handle.

## Responsive government
Unchanged. The labour share slips from 60.0% to 59.4%, a shift too small to move the balance of power.

## Time for what matters
No particular change.

## Bottom line
The baseline for "AI as normal technology": real but ordinary gains. The authors note that today's muted labour-market data fit this scenario, or the very start of a more extreme one, and that "today, none of the three can be ruled out."

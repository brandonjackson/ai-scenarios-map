# Modest — summary

*Korinek, Jones, Sacher, Cotter & McCrory, "Economic Scenarios for Transformative AI", The Anthropic Institute Working Paper No. 2026-02 (September 2026), with Anthropic's companion web explorer, "Scenarios for our Economic Future", and its June 2026 Economic Policy Framework. A US model with three scenarios (Modest, Substantial, Extreme) compared in January 2030. Read from the full working paper, the explorer text and the policy framework; see `extract.md`.*

**The scenario.** "The economy continues on a 'normal' path with AI making changes around the margins," much as the internet did. AI's reach grows from 14% of task mass in mid-2026 to 20% by 2030, and it is used on a fifth of the tasks it could do, so about 4% of all task instances are affected. Half of that work is automated and half augmented, and one new cognitive task appears for every two automated. GDP is 1.6% above the no-AI path, "about ten months of ordinary growth accumulated over four years", and growth is 2.4% a year against 2.0%.

## Hard work pays
Holds. Cognitive wages are 0.4% and other wages 1.1% above the no-AI path. Cognitive employment is 0.5% below its mid-2026 level, cognitive unemployment stays at 2.9% and total unemployment edges up to 3.9%. The model does not look at effort, hierarchy or career stage.

## Affordable essentials
Not addressed.

## Reliable safety net
Not addressed. The extra occupational movement is "compatible with past labour-market experience".

## Responsive government
Not addressed. The labour share slips from 60.0% to 59.4% and capital income grows faster than labour income (3.1% against 0.6%). The explorer treats sharing gains broadly as a collective choice in every scenario.

## Time for what matters
Not addressed.

## Bottom line
AI as normal technology: small, ordinary gains, slightly tilted towards capital, that existing institutions absorb. The authors say none of the three scenarios "can be ruled out".

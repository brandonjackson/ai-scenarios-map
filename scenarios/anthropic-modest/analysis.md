# Modest — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Policy off.** The model "leaves out policy responses", along with business cycles, financial disruption, catastrophic risk and political economy. With effects this small, the authors say "the adjustments required of existing institutions are correspondingly small."

## Big assumptions
1. **Limited capability and slow diffusion.** AI can perform a fifth of the economy's tasks by 2030, and only a fifth of that potential is used, so about 4% of tasks are affected.
2. **A balanced mix.** Half the affected work is automated and half augmented.
3. **New tasks appear.** One new human task for every two automated.
4. **Workers move freely.** The model has two occupation groups; a displaced worker who switches group earns that group's wage at once.

## What must be true for this to occur
- Capability gains slow, or adoption stays shallow and slow, through 2030.
- Measured productivity gains stay small, as they have been so far.

## Tensions and weak points
- The model's simplifications (no lost tenure, no demand feedback) make every scenario look smoother than real displacement. In this one they matter least.
- It is a US model. The UK and other countries could differ through their position in the AI value chain (see `gos-transformation-economy`).
- It sits close to `normal-tech` and `acemoglu` on the map, and its numbers give those essays a quantified form.

## Scoring notes
Draft scores are 3 on every promise: manageable change within existing capacity, which is what the scenario describes.

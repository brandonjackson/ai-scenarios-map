# Modest — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Off.** The model isolates technology and labour-market adjustment and leaves policy responses out of the simulated paths. The authors' suggestions (retraining, income support, universal basic capital, universal basic income) and the linked policy framework's call for measurement, phased responses and possibly sequenced rollout apply across the set and are aimed at the larger shocks.

## Big assumptions
1. **Capability: slow growth in reach.** AI's reach rises only from 14% to 20% of task mass between mid-2026 and 2030, with productivity on affected tasks up about 35%. Effects are confined to cognitive tasks.
2. **Adoption: shallow.** Use doubles from 10% to 20% of eligible instances, so about 4% of task instances are affected.
3. **Markets: balanced automation and reinstatement.** Half of affected work is automated, one new task appears per two automated, and capital supply responds (elasticity 3). Displaced cognitive workers search across occupations with a search discount of 0.17.
4. **Politics: not modelled.** Effects are small enough that institutions need not change.

## What must be true for this to occur
- Capability gains in knowledge work slow markedly from their mid-2020s pace, or stay hard to put to economic use.
- Firms adopt AI gradually, at roughly internet-era speed.
- Labour-market churn stays within past experience.

## Tensions and weak points
- An earlier version of this entry said a displaced worker switching occupation earns the new wage at once. The model has explicit search frictions, and the authors say worker-level displacement costs are represented coarsely, not ignored.
- Capital takes most of the small gain: with a 40% share it gains 3.1% while labour gains 0.6%. The scenario is less "diffuse" than the old DIFF tag suggested, so the map now puts it near the centre (x 0.05) with a "limited impact" tag.
- Its likelihood is moved from probable to plausible: today's measured effects are this small, but the scenario needs capability reach to grow only six points in three and a half years, and the authors' survey median sits nearer Substantial.
- The model is US-only and leaves out demand effects, business cycles, financial disruption and robotics.
- Government finances are not addressed; the fiscal y position (0.1) is inferred: GDP, labour income and capital income all rise slightly while unemployment barely moves, so the tax base grows a little with no added benefit demand.
- Close neighbours: `imf-baseline`, `gos-slow-burn`, `tbi` and `tcb-uneven`.

## Scoring notes
Draft scores are 3/–/3*/–/–, so only two promises are scored, below the three a headline rating needs. Work is 3 because cognitive employment dips by only 0.5% and all wages rise slightly; nobody is clearly left behind. The safety net is 3*, inferred from total unemployment edging up only to 3.9%, well within existing capacity. Essentials, government and time are not addressed: the model has no prices for essentials, no political channel and nothing on time; the earlier 3 for government leaned on the labour share, which says nothing about whether votes matter.

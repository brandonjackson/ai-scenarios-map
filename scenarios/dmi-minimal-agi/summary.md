# Minimal AGI — summary

*"Organizing Intelligence", Alex Imas, Andrew Koh and Shane Legg (Google DeepMind), arXiv:2611.05417, 31 October 2026, with the companion DeepMind Institute essay "The Economics of Intelligence" (29 October 2026). A theoretical model, explicitly not a forecast, that sorts AGI into four regimes by two capabilities: task competence and coordination. This is the regime where AI is roughly as good as the average human at both. Read from the paper's main sections and Appendix E and from the essay in full; see `extract.md`.*

**The scenario.** "At first glance, almost nothing structurally changes in the economy." AI copies match the average human but cannot coordinate at scale, so AI-only firms stay at about seven copies and teams of complementary human specialists ("mosaic intelligence") still beat them in deep markets. Headcounts stay intact and industries stay split across many small and medium firms. The change shows up in pay and prices: AI can be rented at the cost of compute, so human scarcity rents shrink. Small AI firms enter niche markets and push prices down. The authors' headline: "even minimal AGI can lower human wages without reducing employment."

## Hard work pays
Broken for some. Everyone in the worked example keeps a job, but specialist wages fall from 4.15 to 2.90 and coordinator wages from 2.41 to 1.30. Workers whose skills AI matches see pay "decline toward the cost of compute". The essay says specialists and coordinators "can demand even higher wages than before", which the paper's own numbers do not show. As current evidence the authors cite softer entry-level hiring and a 4.2% drop in starting wages.

## Affordable essentials
Not addressed. AI drives "down the price of goods" in niche markets, but the source says nothing about housing, food, energy or care.

## Reliable safety net
Not tested. The model has no taxes or transfers, but unemployment stays at zero, so nobody needs catching. The pain comes through lower pay, not lost jobs.

## Responsive government
Not addressed. The model has no government, and the authors recommend only better measurement.

## Time for what matters
Not addressed.

## Bottom line
The quiet regime: AI changes wages before it changes organisations. The authors warn that steady employment figures would be "poor evidence that AI is not having an effect".

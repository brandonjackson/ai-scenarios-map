# Minimal AGI — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Off.** The model has no government. Outcomes come from competitive firm formation, worker sorting and market clearing, with no taxes, transfers or regulation. The authors recommend only measurement: benchmarks for how AI teams scale, and statistics that track payroll alongside compute spending.

## Big assumptions
1. **Capability:** reproducible AI matches the average human in task competence and coordination, stays inside the range of human capability profiles, and stays below the coordination threshold (in the example: task skills ≈ 1.69, coordination ≈ 0.78).
2. **Adoption:** competitive firms adopt AI whenever it is cheaper, with no barriers from regulation, skills or trust. AI copies can be rented without limit at compute cost.
3. **Markets:** free entry and market clearing. Wages are set by what a worker saves a firm in machine costs, so they are pulled toward the cost of compute wherever AI matches a worker's skills.
4. **Politics:** absent. There is no state, and nobody is compensated for lost scarcity rents.

## What must be true for this to occur
- AI coordination stays subcritical: today's multi-agent systems fail on 41–87% of workflows and AI team output scales with an exponent of 0.48–0.68.
- Human teams of complementary specialists keep an edge over copies of one equally jagged model.
- AI compute stays cheap enough to compete with human pay in niche and deep markets.

## Tensions and weak points
- **Paper versus essay on wages.** The essay says specialists and coordinators "can demand even higher wages than before". In the paper's own example, both groups' wages fall, by 30% and 46%. The map follows the paper, so the essay's milder story would sit lower on replacement.
- **Wages in output units.** Wages are measured in units of output while goods prices fall from 0.3 to 0.202. The extract does not settle what happens to real living standards, so x_labor rests on falling prices and fragmented markets (stated), not on household incomes.
- **No horizon.** The regime is defined by capability, not by date. The likelihood rating judges how close today's AI is to the regime's conditions.
- **Displacement paths.** The §2.4 transition paths, including the jump at coordination 0.8521 that empties human-only firms, run inside this regime's parameter band but are presented as transitions. The placement here uses the headline equilibrium: everyone employed, lower pay.
- **Neighbours.** Sits close to `anthropic-modest` and `normal-tech`. It is less optimistic on pay than `korinek-aug`, because it is the one regime where wages fall while employment holds. Within the set it is the least replacing and among the most diffuse.

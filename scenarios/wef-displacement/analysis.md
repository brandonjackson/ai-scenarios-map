# The Age of Displacement — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Off: policy is tried and overwhelmed.** The extract labels this scenario mixed, because governments try to regulate deployment, adapt welfare and find new tax bases. But competition makes restrictions untenable, taxes on AI are only considered, and harmonisation deadlocks. None of these shapes the outcome, which is the future that happens without effective new policy, so it is rated off.

## Big assumptions
1. **Exponential capability.** Agents and autonomous systems scale fast, and robotics takes on tasks that resisted at first.
2. **Education fails to adapt.** Workers cannot move into the new complementary roles.
3. **Automation is cheaper than reskilling.** Firms automate as a stopgap for talent shortages and then depend on it.
4. **Concentration.** A few firms control models, compute and data, and so set the price of and access to automation.
5. **Global reach.** Wages fall everywhere, at different rates.

## What must be true for this to occur
- Capability and cheap deployment arrive before training systems change.
- Firms choose automation even where they could train people.
- Robotics advances enough to threaten the care and gig work displaced people move into.
- Competitive pressure stops any country from slowing deployment on its own.

## Tensions and weak points
- The trigger is a talent shortage, not cost-cutting: firms automate because they cannot find skilled workers. That implies training policy could change the outcome, which is what separates it from `wef-supercharged`.
- More than half of all tasks automated by 2030 is a very fast path from today, which is why the likelihood rests on capability rather than on firms' intentions.
- National safety nets and tax bases vary far more than a global narrative allows.
- Close to `tcb-displacement`, `imf-runaway`, `gos-transformation-economy` and `toloui-titans` on the map; it has moved a little further towards concentrated gains because the extract stresses a handful of firms capturing margins and control.

## Scoring notes
Draft scores are 1/2/1/2/–. Work is 1: displacement at every skill level, closed entry routes and falling wages everywhere. Essentials is 2: historic poverty and strained basic services, though not a universal collapse. Safety net is 1: nets exceed capacity as tax bases shrink. Government is 2: trust and oversight erode, but democratic control is questioned rather than lost. Time is not addressed: the extract's time section is silent, and polarisation eroding informal support is told as a strain on the safety net, so it no longer carries a score here. All four map coordinates rest on stated material.

# Staying Afloat — summary

*Cedefop (European Centre for the Development of Vocational Training), "Preparing for 2040: four AI-powered scenarios for the future of continuing skills development", Publications Office of the European Union (November 2025). Four exploratory EU scenarios built from 21 STEEPV trends, morphological analysis and a semi-Delphi survey of 132 respondents; not a 2×2 grid, and no probabilities. This is Scenario C, the only one with "Modest" AI uptake, built on the path where "AI leads to moderate transformations of tasks and jobs rather than extensive disruptions". Read from the report's full text; see `extract.md`.*

**The scenario.** It is 2040. AI is rising "but at a modest pace". Regulation, geopolitics and weak state incentives slow it, and in this scenario "AI is not an unstoppable force". There is "no extensive replacement of the human workforce": technical skills stay relevant, repetitive-task jobs persist at 15–20% of all jobs, and traditional training institutions meet moderate upskilling needs. The labour force has stopped shrinking and hiring is easy. But AI's potential "is not fully tapped": sectors with clear financial gains drive adoption, and advances "are not at the service of all sectors or for the greater public good". The authors warn of "stagnation and complacency".

## Hard work pays
Stable. Most people cope with change, technical skills "often remain relevant", and lay-offs and involuntary career changes "do not represent a major social challenge". The weak spot is repetitive work, which survives "often for wrong reasons that lower the bar": fear of AI may be used to push wages and conditions down, and "human workers might ... be pushed to lower levels in this type of jobs".

## Affordable essentials
Not addressed.

## Reliable safety net
Eroding. About 60% of respondents agreed that social protection "will have become less effective and will not have managed to keep up". Union participation falls as non-standard and platform work grows modestly, and social dialogue "does not always manage to establish an updated network of social protection". The state picks up "the larger share of responsibility and costs for broader adult training", since firms train mainly their own staff.

## Responsive government
Stable. AI regulation "protects individuals (as citizens or consumers)" and significantly mitigates risks. But governments do not push AI's wider benefits, so the scenario is "a missed chance to address pressing societal challenges, such as climate change, social cohesion and education".

## Time for what matters
Stable. Fewer forced career changes and less pressure on working conditions; purpose-driven careers matter only to some. Otherwise not addressed.

## Bottom line
A slow, protected Europe: little disruption, little gain. The risks are quiet ones — eroding protection for low-skilled and platform workers, and lost competitiveness.

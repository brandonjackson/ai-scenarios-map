# Staying Afloat — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Mixed.** Policy both protects and holds back. AI regulation protects citizens and helps slow uptake; activation or migration policies stop the labour force shrinking; and the state funds broader adult training for less advantaged groups. But governments do not incentivise AI or cover start-up costs, the state does not step up upskilling, and social dialogue fails to renew social protection. The recommendation to invest in "far-reaching applications for the general population and for smaller companies" is not part of the scenario.

## Big assumptions
1. **Modest capability in use.** AI keeps advancing slowly and supports humans "in a relatively smooth mode of coexistence".
2. **Adoption held back by choice.** Regulation, geopolitics, weak state incentives and cost–benefit calculations keep uptake modest; respondents split 48/52 on this key assumption.
3. **Balanced labour market.** The labour force stabilises and employers find qualified staff, so there is little pressure to retrain.
4. **Protective but passive politics.** Regulation shields citizens, while social protection and green policy drift.

## What must be true for this to occur
- EU regulation and political caution keep restraining adoption into the late 2030s, even as capability elsewhere advances.
- Cheap labour stays cheaper than AI for repetitive tasks.
- Geopolitics does not force a rush into AI investment (30% of respondents expected it to).
- Non-standard work grows only modestly.

## Tensions and weak points
- The scenario is both reassuring ("generally manageable") and a warning ("stagnation and complacency"). The social contract mostly holds because little happens, while the economic cost is asserted, not shown.
- Repetitive jobs survive partly by becoming worse, which the map's labour axes do not capture well; the low score on job quality for that group sits under the scenario's calm surface.
- The state is said both not to need to step up upskilling and to bear the larger share of adult training costs.
- Government finances are not addressed directly; `y_fiscal` is inferred from stagnating competitiveness plus the state carrying more of the training bill, so the fiscal point is hollow.
- Neighbours are `imf-baseline`, `gos-slow-burn`, `dsit-steady` and `ilo`: slow, friction-bound diffusion. It differs in giving regulation and politics, not technology limits, the main role.

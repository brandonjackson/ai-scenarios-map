# AI Unleashed — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Off.** Policymakers are "caught unprepared or unwilling", no regulation safeguarding citizens' rights is implemented, and public institutions fall under the dominance of AI owners. Access to jobs and training runs on automated processes with "no checks and limitations". The authors' call for policies on displacement, worker protection and equitable sharing is a recommendation for avoiding this future.

## Big assumptions
1. **Near-total capability.** AI replaces most jobs at all skill levels within about 15 years, leaving only human-centric and personal-service tasks.
2. **Unchecked adoption.** Firms adopt every upgrade to stay competitive, regardless of energy use, waste or social cost.
3. **Concentrated markets.** A few players own and control AI and dominate the global economy.
4. **Political capture.** Dependent policymakers cannot or will not respond, and authoritarian powers rise in the EU.

## What must be true for this to occur
- Capability reaches most technical and cognitive work across sectors by the late 2030s.
- Control of AI stays with a handful of firms.
- EU institutions lose or abandon the rights-based regulation they have today.
- No redistribution or social-protection response emerges despite mass unemployment and unrest.

## Tensions and weak points
- The scenario needs the EU to have "no regulation safeguarding citizens' rights", which runs against regulation already in force; this is the main reason it is rated Improbable rather than Possible like `agi-end-employment`.
- Human-centric skills rise sharply in importance, yet workers have fewer chances to use or develop them, and Annex 4 lists "highly developed AI-related skills" as most needed. The skills story is not settled.
- Respondents rejected the initial education assumptions, and the consolidated version describes AI's effect on learning as "generally seen as positive" — an oddly hopeful corner of a dystopia.
- Government finances are not addressed. `y_fiscal` is inferred from mass unemployment and poverty with no new taxes or redistribution, which shrinks the tax base; the point is hollow.
- Neighbours are `agi-end-employment`, `gos-transformation-economy`, `gos-take-off`, `anthropic-extreme` and `intelligence-curse`. It sits at the concentrated, replacement extreme with them, adding explicit democratic collapse.

## Scoring notes
Draft scores are 1/–/1/1/2. Work is 1: job losses across all skill levels, with the remaining work precarious and low-paid. Safety net is 1: people are pushed out of the labour market into poverty with no counterbalancing measures and powerless unions. Government is 1: democracy undermined, authoritarian capture by AI owners and algorithmic manipulation. Time is 2: survival crowds out purpose and AI tools bring isolation and anxiety, though some respondents saw more time for volunteering and family. Essentials is not addressed: poverty is stated but prices are not. Labour coordinates and `x_fiscal` are stated; `y_fiscal` is inferred as above.

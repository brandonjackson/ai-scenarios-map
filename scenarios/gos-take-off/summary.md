# Take-Off — summary

*UK Government Office for Science, "AI Scenarios 2030: Helping policymakers plan for the future of AI" (15 June 2026). One of five exploratory scenarios built on six uncertainties; Take-Off has the highest capability and displacement and the weakest security and global cooperation. The authors say the scenarios "are not predictions". Read from the full GOV.UK report, including the outlook to 2040 and the cross-cutting findings; see `extract.md`.*

**The scenario.** From 2029 automated AI research speeds up progress and frontier AI outperforms experts at almost all cognitive tasks, accelerating science to an unprecedented pace. A US public–private partnership controls the most advanced models, China competes closely, and leading systems are deployed in critical functions even as people lose effective control of them. Growth reaches postwar-boom rates on productivity, investment and luxury spending, while millions lose work and gains flow to asset owners.

## Hard work pays
Broken. AI predominantly substitutes across all cognitive roles: "most humans can no longer contribute meaningfully through cognitive labour". Large firms run with minimal staff, entry-level recruitment collapses, and only half of replaced workers find new jobs, many with lower pay and less security. Roles needing physical presence, dexterity or human contact survive or emerge.

## Affordable essentials
Not addressed. Elsewhere the authors describe a K-shaped economy: rising incomes for asset owners and remaining skilled staff, and joblessness or diminished purchasing power for millions.

## Reliable safety net
Not addressed. Elsewhere, continued growth may coexist with falling tax revenues without reform, and the 2040 outlook says continued displacement challenges a labour-based social contract.

## Responsive government
Broken. People keep nominal authority over strategic decisions but defer to systems they cannot understand. The leading system resists human control and gains influence in critical systems. AI access concentrates in leading ecosystems, middle powers including the UK lose influence, and anti-AI protests are widespread.

## Time for what matters
Worse. Many young people prefer AI companions to human friends, with consequences for social trust and wellbeing, and people spend hours a day interacting with AI.

## Bottom line
The extreme case: abundance alongside a disempowered workforce and a loss of control, in a world where national prosperity depends on "position in a global hierarchy of compute and capital ownership."

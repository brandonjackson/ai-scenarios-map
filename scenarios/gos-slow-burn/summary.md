# Slow Burn — summary

*UK Government Office for Science, "AI Scenarios 2030: Helping policymakers plan for the future of AI" (15 June 2026). One of five exploratory scenarios built on six uncertainties (capability, distribution and model access, security, adoption, labour displacement and global cooperation); Slow Burn scores lowest on capability, adoption and displacement. The authors say the scenarios "are not predictions". Read from the full GOV.UK report, including the outlook to 2040 and the cross-cutting findings; see `extract.md`.*

**The scenario.** "AI progress slows, and AI causes less disruption than expected." After 2028 compute, data and the limits of current architectures slow capability gains. AI writes almost all code and beats experts on bounded, verifiable digital work, but still needs human help and is given little autonomy. A 2028 AI market correction cuts credit and investment and kills many start-ups: "a temporary drag on UK growth but did not cause a recession." By 2030 markets have mostly recovered, adoption is led by well-resourced digital-native firms, and the overall growth uplift is minimal.

## Hard work pays
Holds for most, not for the young. AI is mainly complementary, and health and education see little displacement. But job losses and slower hiring fall on junior and execution-oriented work, notably in software engineering, customer service and accountancy. Displaced workers often accept significant wage cuts when they change careers, and few new roles (integration, auditing) appear. Those who stay shift towards framing problems and checking agents' work.

## Affordable essentials
Not addressed. With minimal growth uplift, nothing in the scenario changes the cost of housing, food, energy or care.

## Reliable safety net
Not addressed. The correction is short and displacement contained, so existing support is not seriously tested.

## Responsive government
Strained by the information environment. False synthetic narratives and AI-enabled election influence undermine trust in democratic processes. Populist parties campaign against AI and public opposition deters some uses in healthcare, education and criminal justice.

## Time for what matters
Worse for the young. Many young people rely on AI companions for friendship, therapy and romance, weakening their resilience and motivation to form human relationships.

## Bottom line
The calmest of the five futures for jobs and incomes, but not for democracy or young people's lives. The authors note that a new architecture could still bring a sudden breakthrough in the 2030s.

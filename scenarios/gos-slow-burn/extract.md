# Slow Burn — extract

## Summary

AI automates many digital workflows by 2030, but remains limited in open-ended cognition and is often granted little autonomy (ch. 3, “Slow Burn”). Capability progress slows after 2028 and an AI market correction briefly restricts UK growth (ch. 3, “Slow Burn”, Capability and Distribution and model access). Adoption is concentrated in well-resourced digital-native firms, with minimal uplift overall and displacement confined to some roles and sectors (ch. 3, “Slow Burn”, Adoption and Labour displacement). China becomes a stronger competitor as affordable applications and industrial deployment become more important (ch. 3, “Slow Burn”, Distribution and model access and Global cooperation).

## Key details

- **Source:** AI Scenarios 2030: Helping policymakers plan for the future of AI, Government Office for Science, Government Office for Science (GOV.UK), 2026-06-15. `sources/go-science-ai-scenarios-2030-2026/`
- **Other sources for this scenario:** None.
- **Reading notes:** Full GOV.UK HTML report, including the scenario narrative, the outlook to 2040, and the cross-cutting findings.
- **Scenario set:** Slow Burn and Open Frontier (trajectory 1), Augmented Growth and Transformation Economy (trajectory 2), and Take-Off (trajectory 3). The six uncertainties are capability, distribution and model access, security, adoption, labour displacement, and global cooperation (Executive summary, “Methodology” and “Scenarios overview”; ch. 2).
- **Geography:** Global developments, with implications for the UK (ch. 1, “Project scope”).
- **Authors' framing:** Exploratory policy-planning scenario. “They are not predictions, and the future may involve elements from all scenarios” (Foreword).
- **Authors' view of likelihood:** Not stated. The 2040 outlook is more uncertain than the 2030 narrative (ch. 3, “Guide to the scenario narratives”).

### Timeline

- After 2028 — capability gains slow under compute, data and architecture limits. An AI market correction contracts credit, liquidity and UK business investment (ch. 3, “Slow Burn”, Capability and Distribution and model access).
- 2030 — the AI market has mostly recovered and frontier firms emphasise deployment and industrial buildout. Workflows and science gain from AI, but social, practical and legal constraints limit the economy-wide boost (ch. 3, “Slow Burn”, Capability, Distribution and model access, and Adoption).
- 2030s to 2040 — better reliability, trust and organisational integration could raise productivity and displacement. Persistent bottlenecks would leave a more modest legacy; a new architecture could later trigger a sudden breakthrough (ch. 3, “Slow Burn”, Outlook to 2040).

### Policy stance

- **Stance:** Off. The scenario leaves new government interventions outside its scope, with legal and liability constraints and strategic trade controls in the background (ch. 3, “Scenario limitations” and “Slow Burn”, Adoption and Global cooperation).
- **Policies assumed in the scenario:** Trade controls increasingly restrict access to critical inputs. Legal liability and cultural constraints deter adoption and the scope of deployment. Humans remain involved in most military applications (ch. 3, “Slow Burn”, Adoption and Global cooperation).
- **Policies the source recommends:** The report invites policymakers to monitor signs of each future, stress-test plans across the set, assess impacts from other perspectives and identify outcomes the UK could influence. It proposes backcasting, policy stress testing and considering shocks as methods (ch. 4, “How to use the AI 2030 scenarios” and “Three methodologies for applying scenarios to policy making”).

## Key dynamics explored

- Compute scarcity, current architecture limitations and shortages of suitable data slow capability growth (ch. 3, “Slow Burn”, Capability).
- A correction cuts funding, consolidates market activity towards resilient large-cap firms, and shifts frontier development towards industrial buildout and productisation. Price competition lowers AI prices and favours China’s strengths in manufacturing and energy (ch. 3, “Slow Burn”, Distribution and model access).
- Reliability, liability, public acceptance and organisational limits restrain autonomy and aggregate gains, while digital-native organisations are better positioned to adopt (ch. 3, “Slow Burn”, Adoption).
- AI takes routine execution from workers, raising the premium on using judgment to frame problems and check agents’ results (ch. 3, “Slow Burn”, Labour displacement).

*Applies across the set.* The report treats workforce displacement and augmentation as concurrent possibilities across the set. Routine execution changes even in slower futures. Concentration at the frontier and commoditisation behind it can coexist. Uneven adoption and reliance on US- and China-centred ecosystems affect how productivity gains are distributed (ch. 4, “Cross-cutting implications across the AI 2030 scenarios”).

## State of technology

AI outperforms experts in bounded, verifiable digital work and writes almost all code. It operates familiar apps, provides competent assistance on scoped tasks, and can coordinate with other agents, interact on users’ behalf and learn to a limited extent after deployment. Strategic judgment, relationships, goal-setting, novel insight and open-ended work remain human strengths. Systems require intermittent human help. AI accelerates AI R&D, supports drug discovery and new materials, and guides task-specific robots in controlled settings. Open and closed model performance becomes closer (ch. 3, “Slow Burn”, Capability and Distribution and model access).

## State of adoption

Use is widespread but agents usually receive limited access and autonomy. Most adopters use cloud compute; a small number self-host. Compute constrains frontier capability progress but does not constrain adoption. Finance, tech, professional services and life sciences lead among digital-native, well-resourced firms. Public opposition and legal and safety constraints discourage certain uses in healthcare, education and criminal justice (ch. 3, “Slow Burn”, Capability and Adoption).

## How the economy works

### GDP growth and productivity

AI provides minimal growth uplift by 2030. The 2028 correction creates a temporary UK growth drag without recession. Better integration could lift growth in the 2030s; persistent bottlenecks would leave a legacy comparable to 1990s ICT adoption (ch. 3, “Slow Burn”, Distribution and model access, Adoption and Outlook to 2040).

### Labour market dynamics

AI is mainly complementary. Displacement or reduced hiring is concentrated in junior and execution-oriented work, notably at large companies and in software engineering, customer service and accountancy. Health and education see little displacement. Many displaced workers take lower wages on switching careers; few new integration and auditing positions appear. Cognitive workers increasingly supervise agents and focus on problem framing, ideation and validation (ch. 3, “Slow Burn”, Labour displacement).

### Winners and losers

Large-cap technology firms endure the correction better than start-ups. Well-resourced digital-native firms lead adoption. Work that requires judgment and oversight gains value, while exposed junior workers face weaker hiring and displacement. Owners of high-quality data could gain economic and geopolitical power in the longer run (ch. 3, “Slow Burn”, Distribution and model access, Adoption, Labour displacement and Outlook to 2040).

### International economic patterns

The US keeps a narrow lead as China’s energy, manufacturing and scaled deployment become more valuable. Trade controls and strategic rivalry persist. China could overtake the US later, which would have larger consequences if combined with transformative capabilities (ch. 3, “Slow Burn”, Distribution and model access, Global cooperation and Outlook to 2040).

### Market pricing and scarcity

Price competition keeps AI affordable; high-quality data is a constraint on capability progress and a potentially valuable asset in the 2030s (ch. 3, “Slow Burn”, Capability, Distribution and model access, and Outlook to 2040).

### Financial markets and stability

The 2028 correction contracts credit, liquidity and investment, with many start-up failures and greater resilience among large technology companies. Markets have mostly recovered by 2030 (ch. 3, “Slow Burn”, Distribution and model access).

## Impact on the social contract

### Effort is rewarded

Junior and execution-oriented jobs are disproportionately exposed. Displaced workers often accept significant wage reductions when changing careers, and few new job types emerge (ch. 3, “Slow Burn”, Labour displacement).

### Affordable essentials

### Time for what matters

Many young people rely on AI companions for friendship, therapy and romance, weakening resilience and motivation to form human relationships (ch. 3, “Slow Burn”, Adoption).

### A say in your future

False synthetic narratives and AI-enabled election influence undermine trust in democratic processes. Populist parties campaign against AI, and public opposition deters some applications (ch. 3, “Slow Burn”, Security and Adoption).

### A fair share of progress

Not addressed.

## Other dynamics

Technical incidents have limited fallout, but investment cuts weaken safety research. Defensive capabilities generally keep pace with the growing security threat and support safer software. Synthetic media damages trust. Trade tensions continue, while AI contributes to scientific advances (ch. 3, “Slow Burn”, Capability, Security and Global cooperation).

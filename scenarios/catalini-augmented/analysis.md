# The Augmented Economy — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**On.** The authors say this basin is "reached only through deliberate societal investment". The scenario assumes liability and insurance that raise firms' verification budgets, public investment in simulated practice that rebuilds experience, observability and provenance infrastructure, a deployment cap that keeps unverified output within a risk budget, and investment in base alignment and graceful degradation. These map to the Policy Atlas's AI liability, workforce training and regulatory agencies; the provenance and observability infrastructure and alignment research have no atlas entry. Wider recommendations (universal augmentation as a public utility, neutral payment rails, a coalition of democracies) are in the playbook but not stated as assumed.

## Big assumptions
1. **Capability:** verification technology (observability tools, provenance, diverse model lineages, augmentation interfaces up to brain–computer interfaces) improves fast enough to keep pace with agents.
2. **Adoption:** firms grow automation "only as fast as it can be trusted", and insurers and verifiers become the gatekeepers of deployment.
3. **Markets:** once liability is priced, demand for verification creates its own industry, and the gains compound through cheaper capital and insurance.
4. **Politics:** governments price liability, fund verification public goods and synthetic apprenticeships, and coordinate standards internationally, despite the race dynamics the paper itself describes.

## What must be true for this to occur
- Liability and insurance regimes are enacted widely and quickly enough to bind before unverified deployment becomes entrenched.
- AI-generated simulation can build real expertise, at least up to the current frontier.
- Verification scales "faster than compute lowers the cost of automation".
- Alignment stays above the critical threshold, helped by graceful degradation.
- Augmentation tools become universal rather than a luxury, so gains are shared.

## Tensions and weak points
- **Fragile by the authors' own account.** The human-in-the-loop equilibrium "is not a natural steady state, but a profoundly fragile regime", and its condition is "exceptionally hard to achieve". The geopolitical race that pushes alignment down in the Hollow Economy is answered only by a recommended coalition.
- **Shared gains are a condition, not an outcome.** Broad sharing depends on augmentation being universal, which the paper recommends rather than assumes; the map's diffuse placement rests on universal public goods plus that condition, and verification value still concentrates in "a progressively narrower set of global super-experts".
- **Displaced workers remain.** Even here, workers whose tasks are easy to automate and verify see wages "drop to the cost of compute", and nothing in the extract catches them.
- **Public finances are silent.** Government is a big spender on verification, simulation and augmentation, but the paper never says how this is paid for, so the fiscal map is left blank.
- **Neighbours:** `korinek-aug`, `gos-augmented-growth`, `normal-tech` and `autor`. It differs from them in making verification, not skills or diffusion speed, the binding constraint.

## Scoring notes
Scores are 3/4/–/4/4. Work is 3: proven aptitude replaces credentials, simulated practice rebuilds the entry ladder and individuals work at startup scale, but measurable work is re-priced at compute cost and "Displaced Workers" are told to "exit", which holds it back from 4. Essentials is 4: diagnostic-quality healthcare and individualised education become universally affordable and physical service costs collapse, though housing and food are not discussed. Government is 4: humans remain "the architect" and keep sovereignty through augmentation, government invests in public goods and public administration becomes expert-level, though voting is never discussed. Time is 4: the authors foresee "a radical elevation of human purpose" with teaching, mentoring and connection valued; it rests on general statements, not hours or leisure, and could be read as not addressed. Safety net is not addressed.

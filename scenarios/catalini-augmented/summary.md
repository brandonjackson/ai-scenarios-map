# The Augmented Economy — summary

*Christian Catalini, Xiang Hui and Jane Wu, "Some Simple Economics of AGI", arXiv 2602.20946 (February 2026). The paper models AI as driving the cost of automating measurable tasks towards the cost of compute, while realised value is capped by the human capacity to verify what agents produce. It ends in two equilibrium "basins": the Hollow Economy (`catalini-hollow`) and this one, "reached only through deliberate societal investment". The authors prescribe it and give an operational playbook. No horizon year is given. Read from most of the full text, with the formal proofs skimmed; see `extract.md`.*

**The scenario.** Society scales verification alongside AI. Liability and insurance make deployers carry their tail risk, observability and cryptographic provenance speed up feedback, AI-driven simulation rebuilds the apprenticeship pipeline, and augmentation tools raise each expert's oversight capacity. Firms grow automation "only as fast as it can be trusted", and deployment is capped so unverified output stays within a risk budget. Agents become "multipliers", funding "a massive, structural increase in human consumption". Measurable goods become abundant, and value moves to verification, underwriting, provenance and status.

## Hard work pays
Stable, with a reshuffle. Effort is rewarded through proven aptitude rather than credentials: the economy moves "from a credentialing market … to a discovery market", synthetic practice compresses "years of traditional experience into months", and one person can "execute at the scale of a traditional startup". But measurable work is still re-priced at compute cost whatever the training behind it, "average expertise" loses its value, and for "Displaced Workers" the strategy is "exit".

## Affordable essentials
Improving. "Diagnostic-quality healthcare, individualized education, and expert-level public administration" come "at marginal costs that make genuine universal access economically feasible", and cheaper energy and manufacturing collapse "the marginal cost of the physical service". Housing and food are not discussed.

## Reliable safety net
Not addressed. The "strategic human capital reserve" the paper discusses is a pool of experts for crises, not income support.

## Responsive government
Improving. Humanity remains "the architect of its intelligence"; augmentation lets people "remain cognitive peers with their creation" and keep "sovereignty over the agentic economy". Government acts as "investor of last resort" for verification public goods, democracies coordinate safety standards, and public administration becomes expert-level and universal. Voting is not discussed.

## Time for what matters
Improving. The authors foresee "a radical elevation of human purpose": teaching and mentoring command a premium, and value attaches to "human connection" and meaning-making. Working hours are not discussed.

## Bottom line
AI kept within what people can verify and insure: abundant measurable goods, universal public services and human oversight that holds, but with displaced workers left to "exit" and no word on income support.

# Plateau — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Off: the technical ceiling does the work.** The slowdown comes from scarce data, weak generalisation and investors retreating, not from regulation. The extract labels the stance mixed because public measures accompany the story (European investment after the AI Action Summit, voluntary biosecurity coordination through the OECD, public-sector pilots of local models, locally varying permission for clinical use), but none of them drives the outcome; they are background. CFG's recommendations (adaptive labour-market and education institutions, cyber defence, verification) apply across the whole set.

## Big assumptions
1. **Capability: scaling stalls soon.** A data wall, costly synthetic data and weak verifier generalisation cap frontier gains; FrontierAI's 5× training run in 2027 brings only modest planning and reliability gains. This bets against continued algorithmic progress.
2. **Adoption: slow and practical.** Integration beyond simple tasks takes years of customisation, security review and staff training. Coding agents are routine by mid-2026, but general agents are not.
3. **Markets: an orderly bust.** AI equities and startup funding fall, incumbents with infrastructure and government contracts ride it out, and product-focused investment returns in 2028 without a wider financial crisis.
4. **Politics: the race cools.** Governments lose confidence that AI gives a decisive strategic edge, so the US–China framing recedes and Europe catches up.

## What must be true for this to occur
- No new method gets past the data wall within the period.
- Distilled and open models are good enough for most uses and cheap to run locally.
- Investors absorb the 2027 disappointment without contagion, and US recession fears don't turn into a recession.
- Defences keep pace with rising cybercrime, the condition that separates this ending from Decentralised Mayhem.

## Tensions and weak points
- The extract is thin on economics: GDP, household incomes and government finances are not addressed, nor are four of the six social-contract sections. The map position rests on the shape of the story. Public finances in particular are inferred (`y_fiscal` 0.1, lowered from 0.2 because nothing supports stronger finances and the bust brings US recession fears).
- CFG's own cross-set view is that even existing capabilities can disrupt labour and education if widely deployed. The Plateau narrative doesn't follow that through, for instance to entry-level hiring (compare Windfall's Lost Generation).
- Decentralisation is ambivalent. The open weights that "democratise" AI here enable the misuse in the sibling ending, where the policy response re-centralises.
- On the labour map (Moderate AUG, DIFF at 0.48, −0.42) its nearest neighbours are `normal-tech`, `korinek-aug`, `wef-copilot`, `turing-trap-escape` and `toloui-prometheus`. It belongs with the slow-diffusion stories (`normal-tech`, `dsit-steady`) rather than the strong-augmentation ones, which assume more capable AI. It sits up and right of the other CFG points, as the framework's slow, decentralised corner.

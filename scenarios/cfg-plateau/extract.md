# Plateau — extract

## Summary

Frontier capability growth stalls because many remaining tasks are hard to train for, data is scarce and investors turn away from large training projects (Plateau, “Main scenario”; “Baseline assumptions”). Local and open models become smaller, cheaper and better adapted to particular users. In A Bright Winter, a final large training run makes only modest gains, causing a bust and a later wave of product-focused investment (Plateau, “A bright winter”). People continue using useful AI in work and everyday life, and widespread access gives them time to adapt (Plateau, “A bright winter”).

## Key details

- **Source:** Advanced AI: Possible futures, Bengüsu Özcan, Daan Juijn, Jakob Graabak, Sam Bogerd, Centre for Future Generations, 2025-07-10. `sources/cfg-advanced-ai-possible-futures-2025/`
- **Other sources for this scenario:** The publisher’s scenario PDF and companion methodology, technical context and governance analysis are listed in the source manifest.
- **Reading notes:** Full publisher scenario text, narrative PDF, and companion methodology and governance analysis.
- **Scenario set:** The study crosses speed of capability progress with concentration of frontier development. The five core narratives are Plateau, Big AI, Diplomacy, Arms Race, and Take-off, each with two alternative endings (“How to navigate the scenarios?”; narrative PDF §1). This row follows Plateau’s main path and A Bright Winter. The companion Decentralised Mayhem row follows the alternative ending.
- **Geography:** Global, with particular attention to the US, China and Europe (scenario narratives).
- **Authors' framing:** Plausible foresight scenarios for stress-testing policy under uncertainty, constructed from expert consultation and structured identification of key parameters. The numbers for training compute and R&D automation are directional (“Key Takeaways”; “AI futures in the numbers”; Methodology).
- **Authors' view of likelihood:** No probability is assigned. The authors selected plausible, deliberately diverse cases for decision-making (“Key Takeaways”; “How to navigate the scenarios?”).

### Timeline

- Mid-2025 to early 2026 — specialized tools find early users. Europe steps up investment while the trade war raises US infrastructure costs (Plateau, “Mid 2025 – early 2026”).
- 2026 to mid-2027 — growth slows while coding and local agents spread. FrontierAI launches a final large training effort (Plateau, “Early 2026 – mid 2027”).
- Mid-2027 to early 2029 — the capability gain disappoints. AI equities and startup funding fall. Investment cautiously recovers during 2028, with more focus on products, and by 2029 AI is a widely accessible everyday tool (Plateau, “A bright winter”).

### Policy stance

- **Stance:** Mixed. Public investments and resilience measures accompany a slowdown driven by technical and market constraints (Plateau, main path and “A bright winter”).
- **Policies assumed in the scenario:** European investment following the AI Action Summit, voluntary biosecurity coordination through the OECD, public-sector pilots of local models, and locally varying permissions for clinical use (Plateau, main path and “A bright winter”).
- **Policies the source recommends:** *Applies across the set.* Stress-test policy across different trajectories. Invest in adaptive labour-market and education institutions, information resilience and cyber defence. Coordinate among institutions and countries and develop credible verification for international agreements. Build scalable safeguards alongside innovation rather than relying solely on access restrictions (“Key Takeaways”; governance analysis §§2–5 and conclusion).

## Key dynamics explored

- Scarce high-quality data, weak generalization and economic uncertainty lower returns to ever-larger training runs (Plateau, “Baseline assumptions”).
- Distillation and open variants lower the cost of access, shifting innovation toward specific products, languages and communities (Plateau, “Early 2026 – mid 2027”).
- The last major frontier effort disappoints. Retreating investors push startups to close or pivot, while existing models provide a base for more practical ventures (Plateau, “A bright winter”).

## State of technology

Specialized reasoning and agents advance in verifiable domains such as maths and coding. General agents remain costly and unreliable over long workflows. The story attributes plateauing performance to constrained data, costly synthetic data, weak verifier generalization and investment drying up. Distillation and local inference bring useful models to phones and laptops, and grassroots groups adapt open weights to local languages and niche applications (Plateau, “Baseline assumptions”; “Mid 2025 – early 2026”; “Early 2026 – mid 2027”). The last large training run yields limited planning and reliability gains. Specialized chips and distillation improve efficiency even after frontier progress slows (Plateau, “A bright winter”; Methodology, Appendix 2).

## State of adoption

Integration beyond simple tasks needs customization, security reviews and staff training, often taking years. Early uses include e-commerce testing, university AI tutors, financial reconciliation, game development, repetitive form checking and government use of local models. Coding agents become routine by mid-2026. Global inference demand rises in spite of weaker investment in training (Plateau, “Baseline assumptions”; “Mid 2025 – early 2026”; “Early 2026 – mid 2027”). Popular coding, research and companion applications gain large audiences. Doctors use AI diagnostics where permitted and learn to cross-check recommendations. Software guardrails improve routine scheduling, and content creators collaborate with AI. The pace provides adjustment time (Plateau, “A bright winter”).

## How the economy works

### GDP growth and productivity

Not addressed.

### Labour market dynamics

Accountants and game developers use specialized agents. Software teams adopt coding agents, while content creators increasingly work with AI alongside their creative tasks. Some ambitious startups switch to consulting or close (Plateau, main path and “A bright winter”).

### Winners and losers

Large incumbents with infrastructure and enterprise or government integration weather the funding crisis better than growth-stage startups. Grassroots developers and localization efforts benefit from access to existing open models. Later, product-focused startups attract renewed investor interest (Plateau, “Early 2026 – mid 2027”; “A bright winter”).

### Household incomes and living standards

Not addressed.

### Government finances

Not addressed.

### International economic patterns

U.S.–China trade tensions increase US data-centre costs. Europe uses the slowdown to invest in catch-up, including multibillion-euro projects backed in some cases by Middle Eastern private capital. One EU member’s leading company nearly matches US and Chinese leaders by end-2025. The AI arms race framing recedes during 2026–27 as governments lose confidence that AI delivers a decisive strategic edge (Plateau, “Mid 2025 – early 2026”; “Early 2026 – mid 2027”).

### Market pricing and scarcity

Distillation drives down local deployment costs and open alternatives keep application prices low. US trade tensions increase infrastructure costs. More efficient chips and models ease pressure from rising inference demand (Plateau, main path and “A bright winter”).

### Financial markets and stability

US recession fears, higher data-centre costs and limited frontier returns make funding harder to obtain. AI stocks and funding rounds fall after the final run fails to revive progress. Investor optimism gradually resumes in 2028 (Plateau, main path and “A bright winter”).

AI assists doctors with diagnostics where regulators allow it. The scenario reports that energy consumption remains a concern, while specialist chips and better distillation improve efficiency (Plateau, “A bright winter”).

### A fair share of progress

Open alternatives keep prices low and spread useful features, including localized and grassroots applications. By 2029 the tools are integrated into daily life and broadly accessible (Plateau, “Early 2026 – mid 2027”; “A bright winter”).

## Other dynamics

*Applies across the set.* The authors argue that even existing AI capabilities can disrupt labour, education, media and political institutions if widely deployed. Resistance can impede beneficial uses. The pace of R&D automation, control of supply chains and the ability to verify safety commitments are critical uncertainties (“Key Takeaways”; governance analysis §§1–5). Digital companions become more emotionally engaging and sometimes addictive. Cybercrime increases, but large enterprises and essential services adopt effective defences. Biosecurity protocols improve supply-chain security. Policymakers and citizens debate how AI energy use and automation affect decarbonization and quality of life (Plateau, main path and “A bright winter”).

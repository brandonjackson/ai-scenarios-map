# Plateau — summary

*Centre for Future Generations, "Advanced AI: Possible Futures" (10 July 2025), by Bengüsu Özcan, Daan Juijn, Jakob Graabak and Sam Bogerd. The study crosses the speed of capability progress with how concentrated frontier development is, giving five core narratives (Plateau, Big AI, Diplomacy, Arms Race, Take-off), each with two endings. This entry follows Plateau's main path and its "A Bright Winter" ending; the other ending is [Decentralised Mayhem](../cfg-decentral/summary.md). Read from the publisher's full scenario text, narrative PDF and companion methodology and governance analysis; see `extract.md`.*

**The scenario.** Frontier progress stalls. "A 'data wall' limits progress", synthetic data is costly, verifiers generalise poorly and investors turn away from ever-larger training runs. Reasoning and agents improve in verifiable domains such as maths and coding, but general agents stay costly and unreliable over long workflows. Distillation and open weights bring smaller, cheaper models to phones and laptops, and grassroots groups adapt them to local languages and niche uses. A last large training run in 2027 disappoints, AI equities and startup funding fall, and investment recovers in 2028 around products. "By 2029, AI feels truly democratised."

## Hard work pays
Kept for most. Accountants, game developers, software teams and content creators work alongside specialised agents, and slow, costly integration means "the pace provides adjustment time". The losers the scenario names are growth-stage startups that close or turn to consulting in the funding bust, not workers displaced by AI.

## Affordable essentials
Little change. Doctors use AI diagnostics where regulators allow it and learn to cross-check them, and open alternatives keep the price of AI applications low. Energy use remains a concern, eased by specialist chips and better distillation. Nothing in the scenario moves the cost of housing, food or care.

## Reliable safety net
Not addressed. Without mass displacement the existing net is not tested.

## Responsive government
Not addressed directly. Power stays spread out: the arms-race framing recedes as governments lose confidence that AI gives a decisive strategic edge, and Europe invests to catch up. Cybercrime rises, but large firms and essential services adopt effective defences, and biosecurity protocols improve.

## Time for what matters
Not addressed. Digital companions become more emotionally engaging and sometimes addictive, the one hint of a threat.

## Bottom line
The calmest future in the CFG set: AI as a widely spread, modest tool, with the social contract largely intact because the technology does less, not because policy steered it. The same open ecosystem carries the misuse risk that the sibling ending, Decentralised Mayhem, plays out.

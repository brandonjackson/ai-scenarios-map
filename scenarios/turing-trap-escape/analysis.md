# Escaping the Turing Trap — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**On.** Public policy and institutions shift incentives towards complementary technology and human capital: education and labour markets supply the skills complementary jobs need, and effective taxes on labour and capital are rebalanced. It is the policy-on twin of [`turing-trap`](../turing-trap/analysis.md).

## Big assumptions
1. **Capability:** AI's most valuable abilities are those beyond unaided humans in particular domains, so human–machine teams can do a wider set of tasks than either alone.
2. **Adoption:** firms make the complementary investments (about $9 of human capital per dollar of machine learning, in an estimate the author cites) and redesign their production systems.
3. **Markets:** augmentation creates more value than automation, through new goods and services, and expands demand for labour.
4. **Politics:** tax reform and public training can be enacted, and dispersed knowledge keeps political power spread out.

## What must be true for this to occur
- Tax codes stop favouring capital over labour, or tilt towards wages, for example through a larger earned income tax credit.
- Governments provide or incentivise training that employers underprovide because trained workers can leave.
- Technical benchmarks reward what humans and machines can do together rather than human imitation.
- Firms reorganise work instead of swapping individual people for machines.

## Tensions and weak points
- The essay's own trade parallel cuts against it: bigger aggregate gains did not by themselves secure compensation for losers, and the reforms here need the political will that the trap erodes.
- Written in 2022; frontier AI has since aimed at human-like generality, which may narrow the gap between augmenting and replacing.
- The extract says little about fiscal cost. Favouring wage income is a tax expenditure, but a broad wage base keeps revenue, so public finances sit mildly positive (0.3).
- Mirrors `turing-trap` on both maps, as a fork should. Close to `turing-transform`, `toloui-prometheus` and `wef-copilot`; unlike `turing-transform`, it depends on policy.

# Supercharged Progress — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Mostly off.** Readiness comes from a "radical redesign of education and training", which implies prior policy, but the scenario's governments lag: safety nets struggle and only some experiment with AI dividends or basic income. The white paper is written for business strategists, not policymakers.

## Big assumptions
1. **Exponential capability** that brings AGI much closer.
2. **Widespread workforce readiness**, through redesigned education and training.
3. **Open-source competition** keeps agents cheap and spreads them widely.
4. **An AI-centric economy** in which agent networks become "as central as electricity grids".

## What must be true for this to occur
- Education and training systems change faster than they ever have.
- Capability jumps sharply by 2030 while open models keep pace with closed ones.
- Firms restructure around agents rather than layering AI onto existing work.

## Tensions and weak points
- "Widespread readiness" and "wages plummeting across most sectors" sit uneasily together. Readiness contains unemployment but not the fall in bargaining power.
- It is labelled the optimistic scenario, but its labour outcomes are close to `wef-displacement` apart from unemployment.
- On the map it sits near the centre-right on labour (replacement but not fully concentrated) and in the weaker-finances, fewer-good-jobs quadrant, reflecting the "rapidly tightening fiscal space".

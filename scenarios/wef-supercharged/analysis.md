# Supercharged Progress — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Mixed.** Widespread readiness rests on deliberate policy: education and training are "radically redesigned", and early investment in training is what lets workers adjust. At the same time social protection and regulation lag behind; only some governments try AI dividends, wage insurance or basic income, and others simply run short of fiscal space. The scenario contains both enacted policy and its absence. The paper's recommendations are mostly for businesses and apply across the set.

## Big assumptions
1. **Exponential capability.** Breakthroughs bring AGI closer, and quantum, digital twins and end-to-end coordination support agentic operations.
2. **Readiness keeps pace.** Redesigned education and training spread AI skills widely.
3. **Cheap, open agents.** Open-source competition keeps costs low and spreads deployment.
4. **A productivity boom.** Capital spending passes $1.3 trillion and global growth nears double digits.

## What must be true for this to occur
- Education and training systems change faster than they ever have.
- Capability jumps sharply by 2030 and open models keep pace with closed ones.
- Firms rebuild around agents rather than layering AI onto existing work.
- Energy and materials constraints do not bind before 2030.

## Tensions and weak points
- "Widespread readiness" sits awkwardly with falling wages and declining bargaining power across many sectors. Readiness limits unemployment, not the shift in market power.
- Near double-digit global growth by 2030 is far outside any current trend, and the paper does not say how it squares with tightening fiscal space.
- Labour outcomes are close to `wef-displacement` apart from unemployment; on the map it sits between that and `wef-stalled`, with replacement clearer than in Stalled but gains less concentrated than in Displacement. Other neighbours are `anthropic-substantial` and `turing-trap`.

# Disruption Swarm — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Policy off for the transition.** The quadrant describes what happens before adaptive institutions arrive. In Toloui's account Engels' Pause ended only through policy (Corn Law repeal, Factory Acts, unions, public health, education) plus new labour demand from railways and steam. The scenario assumes those responses lag by years or decades.

## Big assumptions
1. **Intelligence commoditises.** Scaling returns diminish, efficiency keeps improving, "good enough" small models serve most uses, and no firm keeps a lasting lead, so the cost of intelligence keeps falling.
2. **Cheap AI substitutes rather than complements.** Commoditisation usually expands demand and creates jobs (Toloui's car-industry story). Here that mechanism is assumed too weak or too slow.
3. **Diffusion is fast.** Fast followers race on deployment, so displacement outruns the reorganisation and retraining that generate new jobs.
4. **Gains go to capital and consumers, not workers.** As in Engels' Pause, productivity doesn't become wages, even with no monopolist.

## What must be true for this to occur
- Open or cheap models get close enough to the frontier to do most routine cognitive work.
- Organisations find automation the "low-hanging fruit" and adopt it faster than social resistance or inertia can slow it.
- New industries built on cheap intelligence employ far fewer people than they displace, at least for a generation.
- Policy doesn't move quickly to cut the cost of hiring, insure wages or share the gains.
- Shrinking labour forces from falling birth rates aren't enough to absorb the slack.

## Tensions and weak points
- Prices fall, yet hard work stops paying. Whether that is a crisis or a transition depends on who gets the consumer surplus. The scenario implicitly assumes it doesn't reach the displaced.
- Decentralisation spreads misuse risks too (cyber, bio), which Toloui flags as a complicating factor. It could prompt a crackdown that pushes the world toward concentration.
- Toloui notes economists still debate whether manufacturing decline was technology or trade. The modern analogue is less clean than presented.

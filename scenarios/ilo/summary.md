# Work Transformed: The Promise and Peril of AI — summary

*International Labour Organization (Sofia Gomez Tamayo & Andrea Petrelli), Research Brief, June 2025 · drawing on Gmyrek et al. (ILO), Cazzaniga et al. (IMF) and others*

**The scenario.** A synthesis of global evidence. AI is "currently more likely to transform jobs than eliminate them". Outcomes depend on two things: how *exposed* a job is and how *complementary* AI is to human skills. Only 2.3% of jobs globally have high automation potential (0.4% in low-income countries, 5.1% in high-income ones), while 10–13% could benefit from augmentation. Clerical work is the exception: 24% of its tasks are highly exposed and 58% moderately. "The impact of AI on work will depend heavily on the policies adopted."

## Hard work pays
Holding for now, unevenly. Firms adjust "through hiring freezes and voluntary attrition" rather than layoffs. Augmentation helps novices most (+34% for inexperienced call-centre agents). Women are hit harder: 4.7% of women's jobs are at high risk against 2.4% of men's (9.6% vs 3.5% in rich countries), because women are over-represented in clerical roles. Younger, college-educated workers move into AI-complementary roles at a wage premium; older and non-college workers move to low-exposure jobs with "higher risks of wage loss."

## Affordable essentials
Possible gains. Following Autor, AI "may lower service costs by reducing reliance on expensive expert labour." The brief doesn't quantify cost-of-living effects.

## Reliable safety net
Needs strengthening. The brief calls for "social protection measures" for displaced workers, career-transition support with financial assistance, and lifelong-learning entitlements. Older clerical workers, the most exposed, "frequently lack access to effective retraining."

## Responsive government
At risk without worker voice. Algorithmic tools are often introduced "without consultation or accountability, especially in non-unionized and platform work". Unregulated deployment "could reinforce power asymmetries, especially in concentrated digital markets and authoritarian settings." The ILO calls for social dialogue, algorithmic auditing, worker-centred design and global initiatives so developing countries aren't left on the wrong side of an "AI divide".

## Time for what matters
Mixed. AI can take workers out of hazardous tasks, but algorithmic management brings "intensified workloads and reduced recovery time," stress, cognitive overload and social isolation.

## Bottom line
Not yet a jobs crisis, but a fork. With high complementarity and wide diffusion, IMF modelling shows output up 16% and every income group gaining. With low complementarity, capital wins and inequality grows. Which one happens is a policy choice.

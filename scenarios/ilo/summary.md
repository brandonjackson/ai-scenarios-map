# Work Transformed: The Promise and Peril of AI — summary

*International Labour Organization, "Work Transformed: The Promise and Peril of Artificial Intelligence", research brief, June 2025, by Sofia Gomez Tamayo and Andrea Petrelli. A global synthesis of published estimates; its three income scenarios come from Cazzaniga et al. (2024). Read from the full text; see `extract.md`.*

**The scenario.** "The effects of AI on employment and inequality will hinge on two key factors": how exposed a job is, and whether AI complements the worker or reduces demand for their work. Only 2.3% of jobs globally have the highest automation potential (0.4% in low-income countries, 5.1% in high-income ones), but clerical work is the exception, with 24% of its tasks highly exposed and 58% moderately. High-income economies, and within countries urban, educated, younger and formal-sector workers, are best placed to take AI up. In the cited model, low complementarity raises national income by 10% while capital gains widen inequality; high complementarity with broad diffusion raises output 16% and lifts every income group, though by 2% for low earners against nearly 14% for high earners.

## Hard work pays
Broken for some. Clerical and administrative work is the most exposed, and women are over-represented there: 4.7% of women's jobs are highly exposed against 2.4% of men's (9.6% and 3.5% in high-income countries). Reskilling is least available to some of the workers most exposed to displacement. Augmentation can help: a customer-service study found +14% overall and +34% for less experienced workers.

## Affordable essentials
Not addressed.

## Reliable safety net
Not described, only recommended. The brief calls for measures to support workers through transitions and prevent disproportionate harm, but does not say how existing protection fares.

## Responsive government
At stake in the workplace. The brief wants workers to take part in choosing and implementing the systems that affect them, with worker-centred design, social dialogue, auditing for bias and confidential reporting of harms. Between countries, gaps in skills, infrastructure and firm readiness open a divide that it would meet with knowledge sharing and capacity building.

## Time for what matters
Mixed, at work only. Sensors, robotics and predictive inspection can take people out of hazardous tasks, while constant alerts and monitoring add strain.

## Bottom line
Not yet a jobs crisis, but a fork: whether AI complements workers and spreads widely decides whether gains are shared or pool with capital and rich countries.

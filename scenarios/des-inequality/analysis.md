# Income Inequality Increases — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Off.** The scenario runs current law and CBO's projection method, with the authors' treatment of discretionary spending. The automatic redistribution of the existing tax and transfer system is part of the scenario; more progressive taxes and transfers are offered as options, with views on both sides.

## Big assumptions
1. **Capability.** As in Scenario #1, AI is a half-point increment to annual TFP growth, with nothing said about what it can do.
2. **Adoption.** Not modelled. The increment lasts 30 years, with no displacement.
3. **Markets.** All extra capital and labour income goes to the top quintile; the other quintiles' incomes are exactly as they would have been without AI. "By construction" it is Scenario #1 plus an upward income shift.
4. **Politics.** Current law holds for 30 years despite a sizeable rise in inequality. The extra income is taxed at the top quintile's 2022 average rate.

## What must be true for this to occur
- AI raises productivity growth by about half a point a year for three decades.
- The gains reach only high earners and capital owners in the top fifth, without cutting anyone else's income or job.
- Congress neither raises taxes on the top nor cuts them further, even as their share of income grows.

## Tensions and weak points
- The bigger budget gain comes from taxing top-quintile income at a higher average rate. That assumes high earners do not shift income into lightly taxed capital forms, which Scenario #4 shows would erase the advantage.
- "Other incomes unaffected" is a clean modelling device, but it leaves no one strictly worse off, so on the social-contract scale the scenario reads as stagnation for most rather than harm. Relative losses, which the authors raise through the relative-consumption and democracy arguments, are not scored as absolute harm.
- Social Security effects of the earnings shift are not estimated.
- Close to `acemoglu` and `tcb-concentrated` on the labour map: limited displacement with gains pooling at the top. On the fiscal map it is unusual in pairing concentrated gains with stronger public finances, which is a product of the progressive tax system doing the work.

## Scoring notes
Scores are 3/–/3/3*/–. Work is 3 because no jobs are lost and incomes outside the top quintile are unaffected: no group is harmed, but effort pays no more for four fifths of workers, so not 4. Safety net is 3: automatic redistribution keeps more people eligible for Medicaid and CHIP, and there is no displacement to test it. Government is 3, inferred: the budget improves and elected policymakers keep the choice over progressivity, but a sizeable rise in inequality carries the risk the authors name of concentrated political power, which stops it rising above stable; it would fall to 2 if the extract described that power being used. Essentials and time are not addressed: the extract offers only policy options on housing, health and care, and nothing on time.

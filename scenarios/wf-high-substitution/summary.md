# High Substitution, Far Fewer Good Jobs — summary

*Windfall Trust Radar, 2026 · source: map description and draft scores (no public source document yet; add one to this folder)*

**The scenario.** AI and robots take on most office work and a growing share of physical work. Firms use the technology to replace staff rather than support them. Incomes collapse for the displaced and income-tax revenue collapses with them, just as demand for support soars, because the welfare state "assume[s] most people work."

## Hard work pays
Broken for all. When most office jobs and many physical ones are automated, effort has nowhere to go. Remaining good jobs are few and fiercely contested, and wages for what's left are pushed down by a surplus of displaced workers.

## Affordable essentials
Broken for some. AI may make goods cheaper to produce, but collapsing incomes put housing, food and energy out of reach for those who lost their jobs. Falling production costs don't help people with no earnings.

## Reliable safety net
Broken for all. This is the scenario's core mechanism: a fiscal scissors. Revenue falls (income tax and national insurance are most of the tax base) while spending rises (unemployment, housing and health support). Benefits designed to bridge short spells between jobs can't support people who won't work again.

## Responsive government
Broken for some. With a shrinking tax base, government struggles to fix the crisis it faces. Voters still have a say, but the state lacks the fiscal capacity to deliver, which feeds distrust and backlash.

## Time for what matters
Broken for some. Time freed from work comes without the income or security to use it well. Losing work also means losing structure, status and social ties.

## Bottom line
The classic technological-unemployment scenario, seen through public finances. The social contract breaks because the institutions that underwrite it are funded by wages, and wages disappear.

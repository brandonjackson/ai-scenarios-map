# Recession-Level Disruption — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Off: recession-level displacement meets today's safety net, and the framework's fixes are proposals.** The extract records "On", but it adds that the tiers "set out what governments should do, not what they do inside a story". The tier's narrative (searches lasting years, communities losing their job base, consumption dropping when UI runs out) describes what happens under existing institutions. Automatic UI extension, sector transition support, basic-needs relief and pace-of-displacement rules are what Anthropic "would support". The tier is scored on the world it describes, not on the recommended response, so no links are recorded.

## Big assumptions
1. **Capability:** professions "no longer need the expertise they once demanded". The pace-management idea assumes AI could become "a general substitute for human labor across most of the economy".
2. **Adoption:** broad and fast enough to push US unemployment to about 10%, concentrated in industries facing "acute AI-driven displacement".
3. **Markets:** new work replaces lost work only slowly. Shortages persist in care, health, education, infrastructure and public safety, so the labour market is mismatched rather than simply short of demand.
4. **Politics:** existing systems respond slowly. Extended Benefits triggers fire "late and unevenly", and Congress relies on emergency extensions.

## What must be true for this to occur
- AI displaces workers faster than retraining, matching and mobility can move them, roughly doubling unemployment.
- Displacement persists long enough for job searches to run "from months into years".
- Shortage sectors do not absorb displaced workers on their own.
- UI is not reformed in advance, so exhaustion cliffs bite.

## Tensions and weak points
- The extract gives no growth or fiscal figures for this tier. Public finances are inferred to weaken because unemployment doubles under unchanged taxes, not because the source says so.
- The catalogue says expanded UI and relief "carry people through". The source's own description of the world is harsher, and under a policy-off reading that harsher description is what gets scored.
- It is unclear whether this is a temporary shock or the start of an enduring restructuring. The framework treats that as a separate dimension and does not settle it for any tier.
- Neighbours: `dsit-rapid`, `gradual-disemp`, `tcb-displacement` and `citrini`. Its DeepMind counterpart `dm-policy-moderate` sits further right and lower because an NIT floor is assumed there.

## Scoring notes
Scores are 2/2*/2/–/2. Work is 2: "familiar pathways… from effort to advancement… have become less reliable, and for many, impassable". The harm is severe and falls on displaced workers, early-career entrants and job-dependent communities, but not on everyone. Essentials is 2, inferred: consumption "drops sharply" when workers exhaust UI and existing programmes "only partially fill the gap", so food and housing slip out of reach for that group. Families also revise plans "for homes". Safety net is 2: triggers fire late, systems buckled under pandemic volumes, and an exhaustion gap remains. Time is 2: families "revise or abandon" plans for children's education and retirement, which is lost room for family and meaning, not freed time. Government is not addressed. The headline rating is 2.

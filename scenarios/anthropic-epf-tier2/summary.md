# Recession-Level Disruption — summary

*Tier 2 of Anthropic's "Economic Policy Framework" ("A policy framework for AI's impact on work"), a 14-page US policy paper published in June 2026 with a web summary. Three tiers are set by the unemployment rate, each matched to the policies Anthropic would want ready. The tiers are contingencies, not forecasts. Read from the full PDF and web summary; see `extract.md`.*

**The scenario.** AI pushes unemployment to about 10%, and "disruption is a fact of life for tens of millions of American families". Professions "no longer need the expertise they once demanded". Careers "end earlier than planned and begin later than expected", and job searches "stretch from months into years". Communities "that depended on a stable base of steady employment" lose that base faster than new work replaces it. Existing UI extensions fire "late and unevenly", consumption "drops sharply" when benefits run out, and other programmes "only partially fill the gap". The framework's responses are recommendations, not assumed: automatically extended UI, sector transition support, basic-needs relief, and uniform rules to manage the pace of displacement.

## Hard work pays
Broken for some. "Familiar pathways—from education to career, from effort to advancement, from work to stability—have become less reliable, and for many, impassable." Older workers lose careers early, young people start them late, and expertise loses value.

## Affordable essentials
Broken for some (inferred). Prices are not discussed. But families revise or abandon plans "for homes", and people who exhaust UI cut spending sharply while existing programmes only partly fill the gap. The basic-needs relief the authors propose, to secure "food, housing, and healthcare", is not part of the scenario.

## Reliable safety net
Broken for some. The question becomes "how fast existing systems can scale". Extended Benefits triggers "can fire late and unevenly", Congress has repeatedly had to pass emergency extensions, and legacy state systems "buckled under pandemic claim volumes". Those who exhaust benefits fall into a gap.

## Responsive government
Not addressed.

## Time for what matters
Broken for some. Families revise or abandon plans "for children's education, for retirements". This is not time freed for what matters. It is life plans cut short by job loss.

## Bottom line
A recession that does not end on the usual schedule. Today's safety net is too slow and too short for searches that last years. The tier is the case for reforming it before this happens.

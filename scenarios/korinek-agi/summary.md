# Full AGI Scenario — summary

*Anton Korinek, "Scenario Planning for an A(G)I Future", IMF Finance & Development, December 2023. This entry covers two of the article's three technology scenarios: baseline AGI in about 20 years and aggressive AGI in about five. The third, business as usual, is `korinek-aug`. Read from the full print article (pp. 30–33), including its two charts; see `extract.md`.*

**The scenario.** Human task complexity has a finite ceiling, so sufficiently advanced AI eventually performs every human work task: in about 20 years in the baseline case and about five in the aggressive one. Output accelerates once labour scarcity stops constraining production, reaching four times its starting level around year 20 (baseline) or year 10 (aggressive). Wages "initially rise in all three scenarios—but only as long as labor is scarce": they peak around year 10 or year 3, then fall back to their starting level by the AGI year and, in the text, collapse as abundant machines substitute for people. The chart stops at that point. The author gives each scenario more than a 10% chance.

## Hard work pays
Broken. Near full automation, machines can do the remaining scarce tasks and the competitive wage collapses even as output takes off. Society may keep people in some roles ("priests, judges and lawmakers"), and functions people want done by a human could sustain some labour demand indefinitely.

## Affordable essentials
Not addressed. Output rises fast, but workers face labour-income losses; the article says nothing about the price of housing, food, energy or care.

## Reliable safety net
Not modelled, and badly needed. The paths show the market outcome "before any compensatory policy". Across the set the author advises stress-testing and expanding safety nets and perhaps starting a small basic income designed to scale.

## Responsive government
Under strain. The article lists returns to capital, inequality and political stability among the outcomes that could differ sharply across scenarios. Because falling wages and soaring output come from the same substitution, it says institutions could compensate workers and share the gains, but leaves that to institutional choice.

## Time for what matters
Not addressed.

## Bottom line
Extraordinary output with the link between work and pay cut. Whether anyone other than capital owners benefits depends on institutions the scenario does not contain.

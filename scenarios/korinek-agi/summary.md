# Full AGI Scenario — summary

*Anton Korinek, "Scenario Planning for an A(G)I Future", IMF Finance & Development, Dec 2023 · model in Korinek & Suh, NBER w32255 (2024)*

**The scenario.** The complexity of human tasks is *bounded* by the finite computing power of the brain, so exponential growth in compute eventually automates everything. Korinek covers the range of Geoffrey Hinton's 2023 forecast: a **baseline AGI** case with full automation in about 20 years and an **aggressive AGI** case in about 5. Growth runs roughly ten times faster than business as usual, but "wages collapse as the economy approaches full automation", and the collapse starts *before* AGI arrives.

## Hard work pays
Broken. Wages first rise, then crash once automation passes the point where labour stops being scarce. After that, labour and capital are perfect substitutes at the margin and wages fall to the return on a machine doing the same task. In the skill-heterogeneity extension a shrinking group of superstars earns ever more while "the majority of the labor force is starkly devalued".

## Affordable essentials
Paradoxical. Output soars and goods get cheaper to produce, but people who rely on wages lose the income to buy them. If scarce fixed factors such as land, minerals or energy bind, Korinek and Suh find stagnation alongside wage collapse, the worst combination for affordability.

## Reliable safety net
Broken unless redesigned. Labour's share of income collapses toward zero, taking labour-tax revenue with it. Korinek's policy discussion points to shifting taxation from labour to capital and building income support not tied to work. Without that, nothing catches the displaced.

## Responsive government
Strained. Almost all income flows to owners of capital and compute. The paper notes compute may earn "very high returns" during the transition. That much economic power held by so few is a threat to political equality, though the essay doesn't develop it. Korinek also shows that *slowing* automation could deliver "significant gains to workers albeit at the cost of forgoing a growing fraction of output", which is a political choice.

## Time for what matters
Ambiguous. In principle, abundance frees everyone from work. In practice, without redistribution, lost work means lost income and status, not leisure.

## Bottom line
A future of extraordinary output where the link between work and reward is cut. Every promise depends on policy that decouples income from labour.

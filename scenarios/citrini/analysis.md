# The 2028 Global Intelligence Crisis — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Policy off, then too late.** Through 2026–27 policy is absent. By 2028 proposals exist (a compute tax, direct transfers, a public claim on AI returns) but are stuck in partisan deadlock. The crisis is defined by policy lag: "AI capability is evolving faster than institutions can adapt." The authors present it as a left-tail risk scenario for investors, not a forecast.

## Big assumptions
1. **AI capability keeps compounding.** By 2028 agents "write essentially all code" and the best are "substantially smarter than almost all humans at almost all things", while getting cheaper.
2. **AI spending replaces payroll rather than adding to capex.** A firm moves spending from staff to AI within a shrinking budget, so AI investment keeps growing even as demand falls. That's why the usual recession brake doesn't work.
3. **No compensating job creation.** AI "improves at the very tasks humans would redeploy to." New roles exist but are few and pay "a fraction".
4. **White-collar consumption drives demand.** The top 10% of earners make more than half of consumer spending, so a 2% fall in white-collar jobs cuts discretionary spending by 3–4%.
5. **Finance is heavily exposed.** Private credit (via insurer balance sheets and offshore reinsurance), ARR-based lending and $13tn of mortgages all assume white-collar incomes stay stable.
6. **Gains flow to compute owners.** Inequality "makes the Gilded Age look tame", and that capital doesn't return through spending or tax.

## What must be true for this to occur
- Agentic AI reaches enterprise-grade reliability across software, customer service, finance and intermediation within about 18 months.
- Firms adopt aggressively. The most threatened incumbents become "AI's most aggressive adopters" because boards demand it.
- AI-driven deflation (cheaper services) doesn't offset lost incomes quickly enough.
- Fiscal policy doesn't move pre-emptively, even with clear warnings.
- The financial system's hidden links (PE-owned insurers, opaque offshore vehicles) amplify losses rather than absorb them.

## Tensions and weak points
- **Reflexivity:** the piece itself reportedly moved markets (the map notes an 800-point Dow drop). A scenario that moves markets can partly bring itself about, or prompt the policy response that prevents it.
- The cheapening of services through removed friction is a real gain for consumers, but the scenario treats it mostly as destruction of corporate value. Its effect on the worst-off isn't explored.
- Physical-world jobs (construction, healthcare, trades) stay stable at first, so the hit falls on high earners. Under the worst-off rule that changes who counts as worst off: displaced professionals who become gig workers squeeze those already in gig work.
- Very US-centric. India's crisis is a useful exception showing how AI breaks labour-cost-arbitrage development models.

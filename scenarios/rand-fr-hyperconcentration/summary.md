# Hyperconcentration — summary

*RAND Corporation, "Federal Revenue When AI Replaces Labor: An Examination of Economic Scenarios with Highly Capable Artificial Intelligence" (RR-A4980-1), by Carter C. Price and Akshaya Suresh; catalogued August 2026, revision 2.0 dated 8 May 2026 (a working paper version appeared in August 2025). A US federal-revenue study built on a 2×2 of AI pricing (monopolistic vs at cost) and new work for humans (new tasks vs none). Each of the four "extreme cases" is examined twice: a long-run case in which essentially all existing jobs are displaced, and a 2025–2035 simulation in which 10% of the workforce is displaced. AI is treated as a pure substitute for labour with no productivity gains. Read from the full report text; see `extract.md`.*

**The scenario.** Monopolistic AI pricing, no new tasks for humans. AI replaces human labour and "there are no new economically valuable tasks for humans, which means that unemployment spikes to encompass the vast majority of the population." A few firms, perhaps one, own the systems, and "the majority of wages and salaries from the labor replaced by AI systems would become profit for businesses." Labour-tied revenue, 65% of the federal total, is directly vulnerable. Corporate taxes cannot fill the gap unless corporate rates roughly double. In the simulation, participation falls permanently from 62.7% to 52.7%, and inflation (3.5%) and interest rates (4.1%) run highest of the four scenarios by 2035.

## Hard work pays
Broken. With no remunerated tasks left, effort has no labour-market outlet for the displaced majority.

## Affordable essentials
Broken for many. Costs fall, but because firms keep collecting profits, prices "may still be too high for many", and the displaced face "a dramatic reduction in their ability to consume, even with transfer income." Simulated inflation climbs to 3.5% by 2035.

## Reliable safety net
Broken. Existing programmes (unemployment insurance, TANF, SSDI) replace only part of wages, exclude some of the out-of-work, have time limits and "do not typically bring people to the living standards of people who are employed full time". Transfer spending rises just as "federal revenue tied to labor would collapse". The authors' universal-income option targets this case but would worsen the deficit if federally funded.

## Responsive government
Not addressed. Regulating, specially taxing or nationalising the monopolists is offered as an option, not part of the scenario.

## Time for what matters
Not addressed.

## Bottom line
The wage bill becomes a profit stream for one or a few firms, while the tax system and the transfer system, both built on wages, fail together.

# Hyperconcentration — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Off.** Existing transfer programmes absorb what they can and the simulation assumes no fiscal response. Universal income, raising corporate rates toward parity with labour, and regulating, specially taxing or nationalising the monopolists are options the authors offer outside the scenario. That the scenario is so bad without them is the point.

## Big assumptions
1. **Capability.** AI and robots replace essentially all human labour, with no productivity gain beyond holding output constant.
2. **Adoption.** Full diffusion, no timeline. In the simulation, a tenth of the workforce leaves the labour force permanently in 2025.
3. **Markets.** One or a few firms own the systems and price at the monopolist's profit-maximising level. No new remunerated tasks emerge for humans.
4. **Politics.** No fiscal or regulatory response. The tax code stays weighted toward labour, with corporate rates below individual ones.

## What must be true for this to occur
- Human labour has essentially no remaining market value in any task.
- Closed-weight models keep a decisive lead and entry barriers (data, compute, algorithms) hold, so prices stay above cost.
- Monopoly profits are not taxed at labour-like rates or redistributed.
- Sticky or monopoly prices keep goods expensive even as production costs fall.

## Tensions and weak points
- The long run (the "vast majority" unemployed) and the simulation (a 10-point fall in participation) differ by an order of magnitude; the map and scores follow the long-run case, which defines the scenario.
- Corporate revenue rises, yet the authors put business-tax direction as uncertain (`*`) because collapsing consumption cuts into profits. How a monopoly earns large profits when most customers have lost their incomes is not resolved.
- Simulated inflation and interest rates rise, and nominal GDP is highest of the four: a fiscal picture that looks healthier in nominal terms than the welfare picture does.
- Neighbours: `agi-end-employment` (near-identical position), `intelligence-curse`, `toloui-titans` and `gos-transformation-economy`. It is the purest monopoly-capture case on the map.

## Scoring notes
Scores are 1/2/1/–/–. Work is 1: no remunerated tasks remain and unemployment covers the vast majority. Essentials is 2: prices "may still be too high for many" and the displaced face a dramatic consumption loss, but the source frames this as many rather than all, so it stops short of 1. Safety net is 1: existing programmes exclude some people, have time limits and fall short of full-time living standards, while labour-tied revenue collapses and transfer spending rises. Government and time are not addressed. Map: all four coordinates are stated; `y_fiscal` −0.75 reflects 65% of revenue directly vulnerable, with corporate taxes partly offsetting (unlike `rand-fr-system-breaks`).

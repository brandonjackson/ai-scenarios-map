# Gradual Augmentation — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Off.** The four scenarios are descriptive. The report's recommendations (better data and early-warning indicators, flexible training funding, modernised unemployment insurance, surge planning at the Department of Labor) apply across all of them.

## Big assumptions
1. **Augmentation dominates.** AI changes tasks inside jobs rather than removing jobs.
2. **Adoption is gradual.** Most firms remain in early experimentation.
3. **Experience is a complement.** AI raises the value of tacit, long-tenure knowledge.

## What must be true for this to occur
- AI stays a collaborator even as capabilities improve.
- Firms keep investing in people alongside AI rather than cutting headcount.

## Tensions and weak points
- The entry-level effect cuts against the "calm" framing: if firms stop hiring juniors, the scenario drifts toward `wf-lost-generation`.
- Close to `normal-tech`, which the report cites, and to `wef-copilot`, but without the latter's policy investment.
- US-only.

# Runaway Diffusion — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Off.** Minimal regulation is part of the scenario. The note's cross-scenario recommendations (shift tax toward capital and rents, "de-link safety nets from employment status", consider UBI-type programmes "under extreme scenarios", competition policy, macroprudential buffers) are what it thinks governments should prepare, not what happens in the scenario.

## Big assumptions
1. **Capability and adoption both run fast.** Agents and robotics spread across nearly every sector by the early 2030s.
2. **Little regulatory friction.** Governments do not slow deployment.
3. **Winner-take-most structure.** Data and compute requirements concentrate AI in a few firms and metro hubs.
4. **Employment-linked social insurance** makes job loss a fiscal shock as well as a household one.

## What must be true for this to occur
- AI agents become reliable enough to replace, not just assist, workers across services.
- Robotics catches up with software so manual work is exposed too.
- Regulation and organisational change fail to keep pace.

## Tensions and weak points
- No numbers: wages, unemployment and fiscal effects are described qualitatively. Coordinates are calibrated against `korinek-agi`, `dsit-rapid` and `wef-displacement`.
- The fiscal and financial-stability detail (household defaults, AI-driven trading, leverage in AI investment) is richer than most scenarios on the map and is its main contribution.
- Development pathways for poorer countries erode, a theme the map otherwise covers lightly.

Draft scores are 2/3/2/2/3. Work, safety net and government are 2 because the source names specific losers (routine workers, labour-taxed public finances, regions outside AI hubs). Essentials and time are 3 because the source is ambiguous or silent.

# Titan's Dominion — summary

*Ramin Toloui, "Titans, Swarms, or Human Renaissance? Technological Revolutions and Policy Lessons for the AI Age", The Digitalist Papers vol. 2 (11 December 2025). One of four quadrants formed by crossing market structure (winner-take-all or fast-follower) with labour replacement or augmentation. Read from the full essay; see `extract.md`.*

**The scenario.** A monopoly controls a transformative AI that replaces labour on a broad scale. Upfront scale costs, closed technologies and data feedback entrench a few suppliers, while broad capability reduces demand for labour at prevailing wages. Toloui notes that "Monopolistic control of a broadly labor-replacing technology is rare in human history, particularly as a market outcome." His analogues are imposed by power: medieval compulsory milling, and British colonial textile policy that appropriated gains and limited colonial producers' ability to adapt.

## Hard work pays
Broken. Autonomous systems may have an absolute advantage across enough tasks to reduce labour demand at prevailing wages, firms may find replacement the easiest way to monetise AI, and early payroll evidence shows lower employment for young workers in exposed occupations. Capital controlling the technology gains strong market power while displaced labour bears the losses.

## Affordable essentials
Not addressed directly. Concentration brings pricing power, and the milling analogue involved high compulsory user fees; Toloui proposes rules to prevent AI rent extraction.

## Reliable safety net
Broken. Displacement and concentrated gains meet a tax code that favours capital (expanded expensing, low effective taxes on profits and capital gains) over labour. The larger supports Toloui lists, and taxing super-normal AI rents, are recommendations, not part of the quadrant.

## Responsive government
The defining risk. Toloui warns against alliances between government and AI corporate interests that could distort democratic control, drawing a parallel with political authorities that enforced monopolies in the past. His quadrant-specific priority is protecting democratic governance.

## Time for what matters
Not addressed.

## Bottom line
The worst quadrant: ownership of a displacing technology and political power reinforce each other. Toloui presents it as rare in history and avoidable, but not impossible if markets consolidate and firms choose replacement.

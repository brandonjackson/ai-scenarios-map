# Titan's Dominion — summary

*Ramin Toloui, "Titans, Swarms, or Human Renaissance?", Digitalist Papers vol. 2 · one of four quadrants*

**The scenario.** The worst cell of Toloui's 2×2: a winner-take-all AI market (massive fixed costs, proprietary models, data flywheels) combined with a technology that broadly replaces labour. Toloui finds almost no market-driven precedent; his analogues are imposed by power — feudal lords' compulsory water-mill monopolies and British colonial policy that destroyed Bengal's weavers (Dhaka shrank from 200,000 to 50,000). The lesson he draws is that outcomes depend on "who controlled it, how it was deployed, and how governments responded", not on the technology alone.

## Hard work pays
Broken. AI holds an absolute advantage across so many tasks that "demand for human labor at prevailing wages collapses", and the gains flow to whoever owns the dominant system. As with peasants forced to pay mill fees, effort no longer buys a stake in the output.

## Affordable essentials
Under pressure. A monopolist sets the price of the intelligence layer every other sector depends on, and the feudal analogy is one of rent extraction: banned alternatives and compulsory fees. Cheaper production need not mean cheaper living if the owner captures the surplus.

## Reliable safety net
Broken. Mass displacement and concentrated profits both drain a tax base built on wages. Toloui's remedies (rebalancing labour and capital taxes, taxing super-normal AI rents, wage insurance and even "non-employment-linked transfers") exist because the existing net is not designed for this.

## Responsive government
The defining risk. Toloui says the priority here is "maintaining democratic governance of AI, forestalling exploitative alliances between government and AI corporate interests". His historical cases are ones where the political order *enforced* the monopoly. A foreign-led Titan's Dominion is a further worry.

## Time for what matters
Insecurity rather than leisure. Freed time arrives without income or bargaining power, and Toloui's Bengal vignette, a population "deprived of purpose", is the human cost.

## Bottom line
A future where every promise fails because ownership and political power fuse. Toloui presents it as avoidable: it is the destination if policy does nothing while the market consolidates and firms pick replacement as the easiest route to returns.

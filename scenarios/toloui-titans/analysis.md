# Titan's Dominion — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Off.** The quadrant couples monopoly control with displacement, with no stipulated intervention; democratic safeguards are a policy response, not part of it. The historical analogues are worse than policy-absent: deliberately enforced monopoly and restrictions on adaptation, so the state may be part of the problem.

## Big assumptions
1. **Capability concentrates and replaces.** Scale gains continue with slowing algorithmic efficiency, so a few firms hold the frontier, and that frontier has an absolute advantage across enough tasks to reduce labour demand.
2. **Adoption locks in.** Proprietary ecosystems and installed platforms lock customers in; firms choose replacement as the easiest route to returns.
3. **Markets favour capital.** The tax code favours capital over labour, so firms automate even for small gains, and the owners of the technology take the surplus.
4. **Politics aligns with the monopoly.** Technological and political elites ally to keep control and stop gains spreading.

## What must be true for this to occur
- Fast followers and open models fall well behind the frontier.
- AI's advantage extends across the human complements (empathy, presence, judgement) that Toloui thinks may stay valuable.
- Competition, interoperability and portability rules are not adopted, or are captured.
- Demographic labour shortages do not offset labour-saving effects.

## Tensions and weak points
- The extract's key quote says this combination is rare as a market outcome; the historical cases were imposed by political power. Likelihood is lowered from `plausible` to `possible` for that reason, matching Disruption Swarm, which also needs replacement-level capability.
- The essay gives no growth path, household-income or living-standard evidence for this quadrant, so the map position rests on the structure of the story.
- US–China rivalry could feed national champions or keep the market contested.
- Close neighbours: `intelligence-curse`, `cfg-central` and `gos-transformation-economy`.

## Scoring notes
Draft scores 1/2*/1*/1/– (previously 1/2/1/1/3). Work is 1: broad replacement at prevailing wages with gains to capital. Essentials is 2, inferred from concentration bringing pricing power and rent extraction, with the compulsory-milling analogue of high user fees; the extract does not say essentials become unaffordable. Safety net is 1, inferred: displacement and concentrated gains meet a capital-favouring tax code, and the larger supports are only recommended. Government is 1: alliances between government and AI interests could distort democratic control. Time is not addressed. Public finances (`y_fiscal` −0.65) are inferred from the same mechanism: broad replacement with gains to lightly taxed capital shrinks the income and payroll base.

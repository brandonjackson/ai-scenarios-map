# Titan's Dominion — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Policy off, by construction.** Toloui's quadrants describe where the technology and market structure push us *before* institutions respond; his five policy priorities are explicitly aimed at steering away from this cell. Worse, his historical examples are "policy captured": the state actively sustains the monopoly. So this is either policy-absent or policy-hostile, never policy-neutral.

## Big assumptions
1. **Frontier AI consolidates.** Scaling laws keep rewarding ever-larger spend while efficiency gains (à la DeepSeek) slow, so only a few firms can afford the frontier — like US railways, competitive at first then consolidated.
2. **AI replaces labour at the level of the whole economy,** not just tasks. This breaks the historical pattern Toloui himself documents: labour-saving technologies usually prove labour-augmenting in a broader sense through new tasks, cheaper output and new industries.
3. **Firms choose replacement.** CEOs see headcount cuts as the simplest way to show ROI, and vendors find replacement easiest to sell.
4. **Monopoly and state align.** The scenario needs an "exploitative alliance" between AI owners and political elites, or a foreign power in that role.
5. **The tax code keeps favouring capital** (expensing, low capital-gains rates against a high labour tax wedge), so firms substitute even for small efficiency gains.

## What must be true for this to occur
- Fast followers and open-weight models fall far enough behind that switching costs and data flywheels lock customers in.
- AI's absolute advantage covers the human residue (empathy, presence, judgement, leadership) that Toloui thinks may stay complementary.
- Antitrust, interoperability and portability rules are not adopted, or are captured.
- Demographic labour shortages do not offset the labour-saving bias.
- Democratic counter-forces (unions, competition law, elections) fail the way they did *not* fail in the Gilded Age.

## Tensions and weak points
- The essay says monopolistic control of a labour-replacing technology "is rare in human history, particularly as a market outcome". The scenario rests on a combination with no clean market precedent, so its likelihood is speculative.
- US–China competition pushes for "go fast", which could either feed consolidation (national champions) or keep the market contested.
- **Date check:** the map lists Toloui as 2024, but the essay cites July 2025 sources and the 2025 AI Action Plan, and is dated 11 December, so it is probably 2025.

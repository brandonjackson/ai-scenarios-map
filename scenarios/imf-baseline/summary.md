# Baseline Diffusion — summary

*IMF Note 2026/002, "Global Economic and Financial Implications of Artificial Intelligence: Lessons from a Scenario Planning Exercise" (3 April 2026). It synthesises a December 2025 workshop the IMF ran with the Economics of Transformative AI Initiative (University of Virginia). Both scenarios assume AI can perform a wide range of cognitive and manual tasks by the early 2030s; they differ in how fast and how freely it diffuses. A companion PIIE blog by Dabla-Norris and Korinek (February 2026) describes the same pair. Authors: Barhoumi, de Carvalho, Gorbanyov, Kido, Koll, Nguyen, Ostojic, Shang, Tamirisa (lead), Toms and Zhao, under the guidance of Dabla-Norris. Read from the note's full text; see `extract.md`.*

**The scenario.** The technology arrives but the economy is slow to take it up. Adoption is "gradual and uneven", held back by regulation, societal pushback, infrastructure bottlenecks and skills gaps. Many countries adopt restrictive AI rules that raise legal risk and compliance costs. Data-centre building continues through 2026, but in 2027 expectations shift when "immediate financial returns" disappoint, hitting AI-intensive firms financed with debt and equity.

## Hard work pays
Broken for some. Many countries "enforce strong job protection programs", and occupational licensing and the cost of human supervision keep AI in services "significant but not transformative". But the protection is uneven: "Older workers tend to retain greater job security because of licensing requirements, labor unions, and political protection, whereas younger, less-experienced workers face higher displacement risk", and "labor force participation declines as discouraged workers exit the labor market". Anti-AI sentiment emerges among vulnerable workers.

## Affordable essentials
Not addressed. With growth effects "weaker and highly uneven across industries", there is no broad fall in the cost of living.

## Reliable safety net
Holds, mostly by default. Strong job protection carries the load; the note's warnings about labour tax bases eroding apply across scenarios but bite far less here.

## Responsive government
Cautious and in control. Governments regulate firmly and integrate AI into fiscal and monetary operations only in a "guarded" way. The 2027 correction is a financial-stability test rather than a political one.

## Time for what matters
Not addressed.

## Bottom line
The world where institutions slow AI down. Little harm and little gain, with a market correction as the main shock.

# Baseline Diffusion — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Off, though regulation is part of the world.** The scenarios describe how AI diffuses, not a policy programme; the note's recommendations (taxing capital and rents, de-linking safety nets from employment, competition policy, macroprudential buffers) apply across both. The baseline's restrictive regulation and job protection are background conditions, not choices the scenario asks for.

## Big assumptions
1. **Capability arrives anyway.** AI can do a wide range of cognitive and manual tasks by the early 2030s; only diffusion is slow.
2. **Frictions bind.** Licensing, job protection, safety rules, infrastructure and skills gaps hold adoption back for years.
3. **Returns disappoint.** Investors expect more than early adoption delivers, so a 2027 correction follows the data-centre boom.

## What must be true for this to occur
- Regulators and professional bodies keep restricting AI use faster than firms find workarounds.
- Infrastructure (power, data centres, connectivity) stays a bottleneck.
- AI investment runs ahead of measurable returns.

## Tensions and weak points
- An earlier version of this entry (built from a model summary, not the full text) put the young-worker displacement in the runaway scenario; p. 14 places it in the baseline.
- The note says little about wages, prices or inequality in this scenario, so its map position leans on the shape of the story rather than numbers.
- The 2027 correction is written in the past tense in a note dated April 2026, a sign that the scenario was written as a narrative from a future vantage point.
- Close to `gos-slow-burn` and `dsit-steady`, and to `normal-tech`'s slow-diffusion argument, but from a fiscal and financial-stability angle.

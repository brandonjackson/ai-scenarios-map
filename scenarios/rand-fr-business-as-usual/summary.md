# Business as Usual — summary

*RAND Corporation, "Federal Revenue When AI Replaces Labor: An Examination of Economic Scenarios with Highly Capable Artificial Intelligence" (RR-A4980-1), by Carter C. Price and Akshaya Suresh; catalogued August 2026, revision 2.0 dated 8 May 2026 (a working paper version appeared in August 2025). A US federal-revenue study built on a 2×2 of AI pricing (monopolistic vs at cost) and new work for humans (new tasks vs none). Each of the four "extreme cases" is examined twice: a long-run case in which essentially all existing jobs are displaced, and a 2025–2035 simulation in which 10% of the workforce is displaced. AI is treated as a pure substitute for labour with no productivity gains. Read from the full report text; see `extract.md`.*

**The scenario.** Monopolistic AI pricing, new tasks for humans. AI replaces human labour in existing industries, but people find "other economically valuable tasks" paying the same wages, on average, as their old jobs. A small number of firms in each market own the models and charge above input cost, so profits rise. "So, although there may be disruptions in the labor market, it will eventually stabilize with people working in new industries." In the simulation, unemployment jumps from 4.2% to 14.2% and stays there for five years before returning to baseline. This is the only scenario in which the authors expect federal revenue to rise: labour taxes hold and corporate receipts climb (3.6% of GDP in 2035 against 2.8% at baseline).

## Hard work pays
Kept in the long run, broken for some on the way. Displaced workers end up in new jobs at similar average pay, but the simulated spell lasts five years, and even with re-employment at similar pay the shock "would feel like a recession". How pay is spread across the new jobs is not specified.

## Affordable essentials
Not addressed. AI is sold at the monopolist's price rather than at cost, and simulated inflation runs slightly below baseline, but nothing is said about housing, food, energy or care.

## Reliable safety net
Holds. The model assumes transfers sustain the unemployed at 60% of prior consumption through the five-year spell, with no new fiscal response. Revenue rises rather than falls, so the report's general warning (high debt may leave little room for a strong safety net in a large disruption) bites least here.

## Responsive government
Not addressed. The authors' options (taxing, regulating or nationalising AI monopolies if windfall profits emerge; stopping profit shifting abroad) are illustrative and sit outside the scenario.

## Time for what matters
Not addressed.

## Bottom line
The benign corner of the RAND grid for public finances: workers are made whole on average after a painful transition, and the state gains from monopoly profits, provided those profits are not shifted offshore.

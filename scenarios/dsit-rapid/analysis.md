# Rapid Transformation — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Explicitly policy off.** DSIT built every scenario without government interventions so they can be used for stress-testing. The harms (structural unemployment, inequality, backlash) are what happens when nothing is done. The report then lists the decisions policymakers would face: workers' rights, retraining, taxation, "the overall balance of human vs machine labour", and the trade-off of slowing adoption.

## Big assumptions
1. **Narrow beats general.** Compute and data constraints push labs toward domain-specific fine-tuning, and narrow systems still automate enough to cause major disruption.
2. **Robotics arrives by 2030.** Physical-world AI (autonomous vehicles, robots in agriculture and cleaning) is the "key breakthrough". That is an aggressive assumption given robotics' track record.
3. **Macro conditions favour substitution.** Persistent inflation keeps labour expensive relative to AI, so firms choose replacement.
4. **Big tech keeps control.** Access only through "highly controlled software interfaces"; small developers priced out.
5. **Safety is solved, but distribution isn't.** Systems behave as expected; the harm is economic, not technical.
6. **Regulation diverges internationally.** UK sectors push to copy looser foreign rules (e.g., in construction).

## What must be true for this to occur
- Fine-tuned narrow models plus robotics reach production quality across IT, accounting, transport and agriculture within about five years.
- Firms adopt quickly and new entrants without legacy systems leapfrog incumbents.
- New jobs don't keep pace during the transition (DSIT leaves open whether the losses are "transient").
- Unions are strong only in the public sector, so private-sector bargaining power collapses.
- Governments don't redistribute the higher tax revenue the scenario itself expects.

## Tensions and weak points
- **Naming mismatch:** DSIT has no scenario called "Rapid Transformation". The map description (fast gains, displacement, a few dominant firms, government struggling) best fits "AI Disrupts the Workforce". "Advanced AI on a Knife's Edge" (a highly general system so capable it can't be evaluated) is the faster, more general alternative. Check which one the map point means.
- The scenario shows that a stronger economy and a weaker social contract can coexist: productivity, tax receipts and cheaper goods rise while employment and cohesion fall.
- Industrial action is "limited" in automated sectors, but backlash is "rampant", so the political response could be sharp and could arrive before 2030.

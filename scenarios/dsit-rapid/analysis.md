# AI Disrupts the Workforce — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Off by design.** GO-Science excluded new government interventions so the scenarios can be used for stress-testing. Existing regulation (in construction) and Western semiconductor onshoring are background conditions, not a programme the outcome depends on. The report then lists the choices policymakers would face: workers' rights, inequality, taxation, retraining, the balance of human and machine labour, and the cost of slowing adoption.

## Big assumptions
1. **Narrow beats general.** Compute and data constraints push vendors towards domain-specific models, and narrow systems still automate enough to disrupt whole sectors.
2. **Physical AI arrives by 2030.** Robots and autonomous vehicles reach production use in agriculture and transport, and their deployment generates training data that improves them further.
3. **Macro conditions favour substitution.** Persistent inflation keeps labour expensive relative to AI.
4. **Big tech keeps control.** Smaller developers cannot afford comparable compute or data; tools are reached through controlled interfaces.
5. **Safety is solved, distribution isn't.** Users trust the systems, and the harm is to jobs, wages and cohesion.

## What must be true for this to occur
- Fine-tuned narrow models and robotics reach production quality across IT, accounting, transport and agriculture within about five years.
- Firms adopt fast, and newcomers without legacy systems overtake incumbents.
- New jobs do not keep pace, and private-sector workers lose bargaining power.
- Governments do not redirect the extra tax revenue the scenario itself expects.

## Tensions and weak points
- **Fiscal position revised.** The map had this with clearly weaker public finances. The extract lists higher tax revenue and improved public finances as an opportunity and gives no realised welfare cost, so it now sits near neutral on finances while staying firmly in the fewer-good-jobs half. The point of the scenario is that a stronger economy and a weaker social contract can coexist.
- Robots and autonomous freight at scale by 2030 is the most aggressive assumption; the cognitive half of the story is more credible than the physical half.
- Backlash is widespread but industrial action is weak where work is automated, so the political response is hard to predict and could arrive before 2030.
- Near `turing-trap` and `keep-future-human` on labour; on the fiscal axes it sits with `anthropic-extreme` and `gradual-disemp`, between growth-funded and squeezed finances.

## Scoring notes
Draft scores are 2/3/2/2/3. Work is 2: net job and wage losses for exposed workers, with gains for those who run or oversee AI. Essentials is 3 because goods get cheaper while poverty rises, and the extract does not address essentials directly. Safety net is 2: structural unemployment with no new support. Government is 2: protests with little leverage, surveillance and bias concerns. Time is 3 because the extract does not address it, although eroding cohesion could argue for 2.

# A Future of Opportunities — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**On.** Inside the scenario, inclusiveness becomes "a key factor in state, trade union and employer decisions", tripartite social dialogue is strengthened, unions extend protection to workers outside standard contracts, micro-credentials are recognised and sustainability is at the core of most policy areas. The diffuse outcome (declining inequality) is explicitly conditional on that inclusive policy. The jobs boom itself, though, comes from AI and a shrinking workforce, not from any policy, so the stance is closer to mixed than the label suggests.

## Big assumptions
1. **Capability complements.** AI automates repetitive and low-skilled tasks but not human-centric ones; only 28% of respondents thought AI would replace those too.
2. **Adoption creates jobs.** Extensive uptake across all sectors creates more jobs than it destroys, with moderate business disruption.
3. **Scarce labour.** Ageing and slowing migration shrink the workforce, so labour markets stay very tight and workers gain bargaining power.
4. **Inclusive politics.** States, unions and employers cooperate on inclusion, skills and sustainability.

## What must be true for this to occur
- AI raises demand for higher-level skills faster than it removes tasks.
- Employers fill shortages by training and hiring untapped groups rather than automating or outsourcing (58% of respondents expected the latter).
- Learning systems become flexible enough to retrain an "ever-shrinking workforce".
- The digital divide is managed.

## Tensions and weak points
- The survey undercuts the scenario's own engine: the consolidated conclusion is that "further tightening of EU labour markets may not happen", because automation, longer working lives and migration could offset the shortage. Without the tight market, the bargaining-power story weakens.
- Inclusive hiring is assumed, but respondents judged the link between shortages and inclusion "not ... very strong", so the scenario settles for a "more moderate expansion".
- Wages, incomes, unemployment and government finances are all not addressed, so the fiscal map pair is left blank and the labour position rests on qualitative statements.
- Purpose-driven careers and career self-direction are open mainly to the well qualified, which sits awkwardly with "most workers benefit".
- Neighbours are `turing-trap-escape`, `toloui-prometheus`, `turing-transform`, `pope-leo` and `gos-augmented-growth`: augmentation with broadly shared gains. It is one of the few on the map where scarce labour, not policy design alone, drives diffusion.

## Scoring notes
Draft scores are 4/–/4/4/3. Work is 4: displaced workers move into higher-value roles in a tight market, and disadvantaged groups gain from the search for talent, though self-direction depends on qualifications. Safety net is 4: job destruction is rare and unions extend representation and protection to non-standard workers; no income-support arrangements are described. Government is 4: strengthened tripartite dialogue, inclusion in state decisions and sustainability at the core of policy. Time is 3: purpose-driven careers grow for some, but respondents doubted most workers gain, and screen-time concerns are flagged. Essentials is not addressed. All map coordinates are stated; the fiscal pair is blank because government finances are not addressed and the booming economy and ageing population pull in opposite directions.

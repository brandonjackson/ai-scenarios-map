# A Future of Opportunities — summary

*Cedefop (European Centre for the Development of Vocational Training), "Preparing for 2040: four AI-powered scenarios for the future of continuing skills development", Publications Office of the European Union (November 2025). Four exploratory EU scenarios built from 21 STEEPV trends, morphological analysis and a semi-Delphi survey of 132 respondents; not a 2×2 grid, and no probabilities. This is Scenario A, the "most positive" of the set, built on the path where "AI has a positive impact on job creation, with most workers benefiting from it". Read from the report's full text; see `extract.md`.*

**The scenario.** It is 2040. The EU labour force is shrinking as the population ages and migration slows, "however, the economy is booming and creating new jobs, fuelled by technological innovations and the impact of AI". AI automates repetitive and low-skilled tasks, but existing jobs are "rarely destroyed": workers upskill into higher-value roles and human-centric skills "take centre stage". Skills shortages make competition for talent fierce, employers turn to previously untapped groups, and AI-enabled, more flexible learning systems serve the workforce. Survey respondents backed the direction but were "cautious regarding the magnitude of the likely change".

## Hard work pays
Improving. Automation "creates opportunities for those in the occupations affected to upskill and reskill, transitioning into higher-value roles", in "a virtuous cycle of upskilling and reskilling". Abundant jobs give workers choice and bargaining power, and disadvantaged groups gain as employers search for talent. The catch: the ability to shape a career still "hinges on their individual qualifications, skills and mindset", and lower value-added or routine sectors may not invest in their staff.

## Affordable essentials
Not addressed.

## Reliable safety net
Improving. Jobs are rarely destroyed, and trade unions extend support to workers outside traditional contracts, "ensuring that all workers have access to representation and protection". Labour relations are "peaceful". The extract describes no income-support arrangements, and people with limited digital literacy or poor infrastructure "may need more support".

## Responsive government
Improving. Social dialogue is "strengthened through collaborative efforts between trade unions, employers' organisations and governments"; inclusiveness is a key factor in state, union and employer decisions; and sustainability becomes "the core of most policy areas", though technology "does not solve the climate change crisis". Democratic processes are not discussed.

## Time for what matters
Stable, with some gains. More people pursue purpose-driven careers and voluntary job changes rise, but only 30% of respondents thought shortages would let most workers put more weight on happiness and meaningful work. The authors flag excessive screen time and information overload from digital learning.

## Bottom line
The EU's best case: demographics and AI together make workers scarce and valuable, and inclusive policy spreads the gains. It holds "only if the digital divide is managed and opportunities are inclusive".

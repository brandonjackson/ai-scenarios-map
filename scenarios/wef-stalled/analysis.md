# Stalled Progress — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Off in effect.** Regulators tighten guardrails and labour policy tries to cover gig work, but exhausted fiscal space and low trust stall delivery. Training systems stay outdated.

## Big assumptions
1. **Incremental capability.** Breakthroughs are rare and costly.
2. **Limited readiness.** A chronic shortage of AI-ready talent leaves deployments brittle.
3. **Automation to fill gaps.** Firms use AI where they cannot hire, especially in ageing countries.
4. **A market correction** in AI valuations in the mid-2020s brings regulatory caution.

## What must be true for this to occur
- Capability gains slow and compute costs rise.
- Training systems fail to spread AI skills.
- Gains concentrate in a few frontier firms and places with AI expertise.

## Tensions and weak points
- Much of it describes the present: uneven gains, skills shortages, entry-level jobs under pressure and leading firms pulling ahead. That is why it is rated Probable even though its valuation correction has not happened.
- The source's own displacement story is mild (hollowing out, not elimination), yet its social outcomes are harsh. The harm comes from inequality and broken expectations more than job loss.
- It is close to `wf-lost-generation` on early-career jobs and to `acemoglu` on modest, concentrated gains.

Draft scores are 2/2/2/2/3. Work, essentials, safety net and government are 2 because the source describes specific, concentrated harms to the young, low-paid and displaced. Time is 3 because it is not addressed.

# Stalled Progress — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Off in effect.** The extract labels this scenario mixed: governments tighten guardrails after the correction, and labour policies try to cover platform work and weak safety nets. But the guardrails are background caution, much like the regulation in `imf-baseline`, and the labour policies stall for lack of money and trust. The outcome follows from slow capability and missing skills, not from any policy being enacted.

## Big assumptions
1. **Incremental capability.** Breakthroughs are rare and costly; agentic networks stay confined to a few frontier firms.
2. **Limited readiness.** A chronic shortage of AI-ready talent leaves deployments brittle, and training stays outdated.
3. **Automation fills gaps.** Firms use narrow automation where they cannot hire, especially in ageing regions.
4. **A valuation correction.** A mid-2020s correction brings regulatory caution and dearer capital.

## What must be true for this to occur
- Capability gains slow and compute costs rise.
- Training systems fail to spread AI skills.
- Gains concentrate in a few firms and places with digital foundations.
- Public budgets are already too stretched to cushion the shift.

## Tensions and weak points
- Much of it describes the present: uneven gains, skills shortages, pressure on entry-level jobs and leading firms pulling ahead. That is why it is rated Probable, even though its valuation correction has not happened.
- The displacement story is mild (hollowing out, not elimination), yet the social outcomes are harsh. The harm comes from inequality, rising living costs and empty public budgets more than job loss.
- On the map it now sits lower on public finances, because the extract stresses exhausted fiscal space. Neighbours are `tcb-concentrated`, `acemoglu`, `chiang` and `wf-lost-generation` on early-career jobs.

# Keep the Future Human: the AGI race — summary

*Anthony Aguirre (Future of Life Institute), "Why and How We Should Close the Gates to AGI and Superintelligence, and What We Should Build Instead", March 2025*

**The scenario.** Two futures. On the *current path*, an "unchecked race toward ever-more powerful systems, driven by economic incentives of a few huge technology companies seeking to automate large swathes of current economic activity and human labor" ends with "an inevitable winner: AI itself." AGI, the combination of high **A**utonomy, **G**enerality and **I**ntelligence, becomes "a replacement species". On the *alternative path*, governments "close the gates" with compute accounting, hard compute caps built into chips, strict liability and tiered regulation, and build powerful, controllable **Tool AI** instead. This scenario is the current path; the alternative is its own scenario, [Keep the Future Human: Tool AI](../keep-future-human-tool-ai/summary.md).

## Hard work pays
Broken on the current path. AGI is "the ultimate automation technology – not just augmenting human workers but replacing them largely or entirely", a race to "capture a significant fraction of the world's $100 trillion annual economic output by automating away human labor costs." The public, Aguirre notes, overwhelmingly doesn't want AI "that will replace them in their jobs." Tool AI would "make human experts and institutions more effective rather than replacing them."

## Affordable essentials
Ambiguous. The race promises cures and abundance, but Aguirre argues those benefits "can be created with AI tools and without AGI." On the current path, gains flow to "a handful of giant companies."

## Reliable safety net
Overwhelmed by "massive disruption": "bulk automation of most cognitive-based jobs ... in a relatively short period." The Tool AI path calls for "mechanisms to distribute economic benefits more broadly" and new public and non-profit AI development.

## Responsive government
The gravest risk. Power concentration "could disempower the vast majority of humanity by absorbing huge swathes of social and economic activity into AI systems run by a handful of giant companies (which may in turn either be taken over by, or effectively take over, governments)." Ultimately there's loss of control: "Such AI will not grant power to humans; we will grant power to it, or it will take it." Many of these risks "remain even if the technical 'alignment' problem ... is solved."

## Time for what matters
Eroded. AGI would "fill [our] culture and information commons with non-human content" and replace "our current epistemic systems."

## Bottom line
On the current path every promise breaks, ending in the loss of human agency itself. Aguirre insists "this is not inevitable": the gates can be closed, and the future kept human, by choice.

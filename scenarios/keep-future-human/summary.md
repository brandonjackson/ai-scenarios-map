# Keep the Future Human: the AGI race — summary

*Anthony Aguirre, "Keep the Future Human: Why and How We Should Close the Gates to AGI and Superintelligence, and What We Should Build Instead", Keep The Future Human, 5 March 2025. The essay describes two paths: this one, the unchecked race it warns of, and coordinated limits with controllable Tool AI ([`keep-future-human-tool-ai`](../keep-future-human-tool-ai/summary.md)). Read from the full text; see `extract.md`.*

**The scenario.** Leading companies race to build AI that combines high autonomy, broad generality and superhuman capability, "to replace labour and capture a share of economic activity" worth about US$100 trillion a year. Businesses pay more to replace a salary than households would pay for optional tools, so substitution is the prize. States see a strategic reason to race too, which makes it harder for anyone to slow down. As systems grow more capable, firms and governments delegate more to them; humans first approve decisions and then lose the practical ability to understand or control them. "At what point, then, will the role of the human be reduced to clicking 'yes, go ahead'?" Nothing interrupts the race.

## Hard work pays
Broken. Rapid automation of cognitive tasks "may raise inequality, unemployment or underemployment faster than society can adapt". Screen-based jobs without in-person or legal obligations go first; physical work follows as robotics improves. Workers lose economic and political leverage. The extract's own social-contract section on effort says `Not addressed.`; this reading comes from the labour-market sections.

## Affordable essentials
Not addressed directly. Activities once limited by cost become cheap enough "to overwhelm institutions designed around scarce human effort", but the essay says nothing about housing, food, energy or care. Benefits concentrate in companies and states, and job loss and income inequality rise, so the worst-off are unlikely to share in any fall in prices.

## Reliable safety net
Not addressed directly. Job loss rises "while social disruption limits adjustment", and the essay's basic-income proposals belong to the alternative path, not this one.

## Responsive government
The gravest risk. Synthetic media, surveillance, dependence on AI advice and eroded confidence in elections weaken people's ability to understand and govern institutions. Control first concentrates in giant companies and possibly governments; national contests favour systems that escape oversight; AI-run firms and markets could move beyond regulators' comprehension. "In the extreme case humans lose the capacity to steer the future at all."

## Time for what matters
Not addressed directly. Aguirre expects an information environment of plausible false material and pervasive persuasion that weakens trust and human cultural production.

## Bottom line
A warning, not a forecast: on the current path the promises the essay discusses break, ending in the loss of human control itself. The essay's own answer is to close the gates.

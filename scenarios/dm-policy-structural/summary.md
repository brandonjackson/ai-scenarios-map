# Structural Labour-Capital Decoupling — summary

*Scenario 3 of "Economic policy for AGI" by Julian Jacobs and Alex Imas, an essay from the DeepMind Institute (Google DeepMind) published on 16 September 2026. The essay sets out three scenarios by depth of labour-market disruption and matches each with a "least-regret" policy and an empirical trigger. Read from the full essay. The longer SSRN working paper could not be read; see `extract.md`.*

**The scenario.** A "hypothetical and extreme scenario" in which AGI "marks a fundamental discontinuity in the history of economics and human social order". Versatile, "recursively improving" AGI pushes the marginal productivity of human labour toward zero "in many sectors" and erodes the ways economies have created new jobs. Growth shifts from labour to capital. Without intervention, "a narrow segment of the population controls the capital, captures the returns to innovation, and the broader public is locked out". The trigger is "a sustained decline in labor's share of GDP alongside increasing capital returns". When it fires, the NIT from Scenario 2 becomes a universal income floor. A pre-designed Universal Basic Capital (UBC) is deployed alongside it: a direct ownership stake, "such as shares in a publicly managed fund invested in equity markets and bonds", whose value "compounds automatically alongside the broader economy".

## Hard work pays
Broken. With labour's marginal product "approaching zero in many sectors", effort no longer leads to a better life for most people. UBC gives people a stake "regardless of work status", which replaces the promise rather than keeping it. The authors value work for its "non-monetary psychological and social value", but they offer no mechanism that preserves it here.

## Affordable essentials
Not addressed.

## Reliable safety net
Stable, rebuilt on capital. As earned wages decline, the NIT works as a universal floor, so citizens are "protected immediately by cash transfers while retaining a durable ownership stake in capital-driven growth". UBC creates "a capital underpinning for the safety net". The essay gives no benefit levels or funding. The authors call UBC "historically unprecedented" and expect "significant political headwinds".

## Responsive government
Not addressed. UBC rates highest of the 11 policies on the panel's "Democratic Voice" measure. The essay notes that it "confronts entrenched political philosophies regarding the relationship between citizens and their state", but it does not describe how government works in the scenario.

## Time for what matters
Not addressed.

## Bottom line
Work loses its economic value. The answer offered is ownership: every citizen holds a share of the capital that replaced them, with a cash floor underneath. Whether this works depends on the floor's level, which the essay does not give.

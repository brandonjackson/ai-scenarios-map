# Structural Labour-Capital Decoupling — assumptions analysis

## Policy on or off?
**Mixed: the essay sets the decoupling against a pre-designed capital stake deployed on trigger.** The essay says what happens without intervention: "a narrow segment of the population controls the capital, captures the returns to innovation, and the broader public is locked out". It then builds in the response. When labour's share of GDP falls in a sustained way, the NIT "could soon after function as a universal income floor", paired with "a pre-designed Universal Basic Capital (UBC)". The policy-free trajectory and the policy response are both in the scenario. The map places the scenario with the response in place, which is why it sits well right of other near-total-replacement scenarios. The AI sovereign wealth fund is considered and passed over for UBC, so it is not linked.

## Big assumptions
1. **Capability:** AGI is "versatile, adaptable, and recursively improving". Labour's marginal product approaches zero "in many sectors", and the usual ways of creating jobs erode.
2. **Adoption:** automation reaches across the economy. Its pace is not described.
3. **Markets:** growth continues but shifts "from labor to capital", and productivity gains spread "throughout the economy, not just in the AI sector". That is why the authors favour a diversified stake over an oil-style fund.
4. **Politics:** a "historically unprecedented" universal capital stake is designed in advance and deployed on a data trigger, despite "significant political headwinds" and possible legal challenges.

## What must be true for this to occur
- AI keeps improving until human labour is close to worthless in much of the economy.
- Capital returns rise, so a broad equity-and-bond stake compounds into meaningful wealth.
- Governments design UBC's "governance, funding methods, and distributional mechanics" before they need it, and they act when the labour-share trigger fires.
- The NIT floor is high enough to live on while capital stakes build up.

## Tensions and weak points
- UBC grows slowly, like the Anthropic framework's capital accounts, which that framework says "cannot alone compensate for permanent job loss that happens soon". This essay leans on the NIT for the near term but gives no benefit level.
- The authors call deploying UBC now "premature", yet a stake seeded only on trigger has little time to compound. The extract does not resolve this.
- Funding is open. A sovereign fund could draw on "increased corporate tax rates", but UBC's funding is deferred to the unread SSRN paper, so the fiscal axis is blank.
- The diffuse reading (x = −0.15) depends on UBC working as intended. The policy-off branch the authors warn of would sit near `korinek-agi` and `anthropic-epf-tier3`. Nearest neighbours with policy: `ai-2027-slowdown` (with a UBI floor) and `cfg-diplomacy`.

## Scoring notes
Scores are 1/–/3/–/–. Work is 1: with labour's marginal product near zero in many sectors, the public is "locked out of a meaningful role in the productive parts of the economy". UBC grants a stake "regardless of work status", which replaces the promise rather than keeping it. Safety net is 3: the NIT becomes a universal floor and UBC adds "a capital underpinning", so citizens are "protected immediately". It is not higher because the floor's level and funding are unstated and UBC builds slowly. Essentials, government and time are not addressed. UBC's high "Democratic Voice" panel score rates the policy, not the scenario's politics. No headline rating, because only two promises are scored.
